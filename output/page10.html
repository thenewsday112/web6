👩🏽‍⚕️ Dietitian's Note

Cauliflower is one of my favorite vegetables for volume eating-adding generous portions of lower-calorie foods to make meals more satisfying. Its high water content adds volume, and blending it creates a silky cauliflower cream sauce with far fewer calories and much less saturated fat than a traditional heavy-cream sauce.

White beans add plant protein and fiber, making this a more substantial meal. Beans can support healthy cholesterol levels as part of a heart-healthy eating pattern. Using cauliflower cream sauce instead of cream and butter also reduces saturated fat, which can raise LDL cholesterol.

I stir in the lemon juice after cooking to limit heat-related vitamin C loss. Vitamin C also helps your body absorb the iron in plant foods, including white beans.

👩🏼‍🌾 Ingredients

Below, I share ingredient notes from both a nutrition expert's and trained chef's perspective.

  • Cauliflower: Creates the silky cauliflower cream sauce. Steam until very tender so it blends smoothly.
  • Garlic: Steaming the cloves with the cauliflower mellows their sharpness.
  • Artichoke hearts: Add subtle tang and savory depth. Choose water-packed artichokes to keep the recipe oil-free.
  • Nutritional yeast: Gives the cauliflower cream sauce a cheesy, savory flavor without dairy.
  • Stone-ground or Dijon mustard: Adds tang and brings out the savory flavors.
  • Unsweetened nondairy milk: Helps the sauce blend smoothly and adds a little plant protein.
  • White beans: Make this dish hearty and satisfying. Cannellini beans have a creamy texture and hold their shape well.
  • White miso: Adds savory depth and saltiness. Dissolve in 2 tablespoons water before stirring into the cauliflower cream sauce off the heat.
  • Lemon juice and zest: Brighten the sauce. Add both off the heat to limit heat exposure and preserve their fresh flavor.
  • Fresh herbs: Parsley, dill, and chives add freshness to the creamy beans.

🌱 Substitutions

This recipe is versatile and easy to adapt. Below are some simple ingredient substitutions.

  • Cauliflower: Use fresh or frozen florets. Steam until tender and drain well before blending.
  • Artichoke hearts: Use thawed frozen artichoke hearts, cooked until tender, instead of canned.
  • White beans: Try cannellini, butter beans, Great Northern beans, or navy beans.
  • Nondairy milk: Use unsweetened soy, pea, hemp cashew, or oat.
  • Mustard: Use stone-ground and Dijon interchangeably.
  • Fresh herbs: Adjust the parsley, dill, and chives to your taste and what you have available.
  • Gluten-free: Choose gluten-free miso and check the mustard and broth labels.

📝 How to Make White Beans in Cauliflower Cream Sauce

The complete recipe with exact amounts is below. These additional tips will help you get the best results.

  1. Cut the cauliflower into medium-sized, evenly sized florets so they steam at the same rate.
  1. Steam the Cauliflower. Steam until very tender-it should mash easily with a fork. Optional: Add the garlic during the final 5 minutes to mellow its flavor. Drain well so excess water doesn't thin the cauliflower cream sauce.
  1. Blend the Cauliflower Cream Sauce. Start with the listed amount of liquid and blend until completely smooth before adding more.
  1. Scrape down the sides as needed-the sauce should be thick but pourable.
  1. Warm the Cauliflower Cream. Pour the blended sauce into a large skillet and warm over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally.
  1. Add the White Beans. Gently fold the beans into the warm sauce and cook until heated through. Add a splash of water or broth if needed to loosen the sauce.
  1. Dissolve the Miso. Stir the miso with water in a small bowl until no lumps remain. This helps it distribute evenly throughout the sauce.
  1. Finish Off the Heat. Remove the skillet from the stove and let cool slightly. Stir in the fresh herbs, lemon zest, lemon juice, and dissolved miso. Serve with a sprinkle of fresh herbs and top with roasted delicata squash and Quick Pickled Red Cabbage for a delicious balance of sweet and savory.

🍋 Why Add Miso and Lemon Off the Heat?

Lemon juice contains heat-sensitive vitamin C. Stirring it into the cauliflower cream sauce after cooking limits further heat exposure. Adding the zest at the same time keeps its citrus aroma fresh.

Miso is fermented, but not all miso contains live cultures. If you use unpasteurized miso with live cultures, allowing the cauliflower cream sauce to cool slightly before adding it may help protect them. Removing a piping-hot pan from the stove alone does not guarantee that live cultures survive. The miso still adds delicious fermented flavor either way.

🍚 What to Serve with White Beans in Cauliflower Cream Sauce

Spoon these white beans in cauliflower cream sauce over amaranth, quinoa, or brown rice for a satisfying bowl. Pair with roasted delicata squash for sweetness or sautéed greens for a simple vegetable side.

Toasted sourdough is also perfect for scooping up the cauliflower cream sauce. Finish with extra herbs and black pepper.

✔️ Storage & Meal Prep Tips

Refrigerate: Store the white beans in cauliflower cream sauce in an airtight container for 4 to 5 days.

Reheat: Warm gently on the stovetop, adding a splash of water, nondairy milk, or broth to loosen the cauliflower cream sauce. Add fresh herbs and lemon before serving.

Freeze: Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before reheating. The cauliflower cream sauce may separate slightly; stir well as it warms.

🙋🏽‍♀️ Recipe FAQs

Can I make cauliflower cream sauce without dairy?

Yes! Steamed cauliflower blends with soy milk or vegetable broth to create a creamy base. Artichoke hearts, nutritional yeast, garlic, and mustard add savory flavor without cream, butter, or cheese.

Is cauliflower cream sauce good for cholesterol?

This cauliflower cream sauce is a cholesterol-conscious alternative to sauces made with heavy cream and butter because it contains much less saturated fat. Pairing it with white beans adds fiber and plant protein. Beans can support healthy blood cholesterol as part of an overall heart-healthy eating pattern.

Can I use frozen cauliflower for cauliflower cream sauce?

Yes. Steam the frozen cauliflower until very tender, drain well, and blend as directed. Adjust the liquid gradually to keep the cauliflower cream sauce from becoming too thin.

Is this recipe gluten-free?

It can be. Choose gluten-free miso, as some varieties contain barley, and check the mustard and broth labels.

White Beans in Cauliflower Cream Sauce Recipe

White beans in Cauliflower Cream Sauce with artichokes, garlic, miso, lemon, and fresh herbs. An easy, satisfying vegan dinner without heavy cream, butter, or added oil. Course Main Course, Side Dish Cuisine Vegan Diet Vegan, Vegetarian Prep Time 15 minutes minutes Cook Time 5 minutes minutes Total Time 20 minutes minutes Servings 3 servings Calories 373kcal Author Nisha Melvani

Equipment

  • Blender
  • Saucepan

Ingredients

  • 3 cups cauliflower florets (about one medium head, or 300 grams)
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 1 cup artichoke hearts drained, preferably packed in water
  • 3 tablespoons nutritional yeast
  • 1 teaspoon stone-ground mustard or Dijon
  • ½ teaspoon onion powder
  • ¼ teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
  • ½ cup unsweetened nondairy milk or vegetable broth, plus more as needed
  • 3 cups cooked white beans drained and rinsed if canned
  • 1 ½ tablespoons white miso or to taste
  • 2 tablespoons water for dissolving the miso
  • ¼ cup chopped fresh parsley
  • 2 tablespoons chopped fresh dill optional
  • 2 tablespoons chopped fresh chives or scallions optional
  • ½ teaspoon lemon zest about one small lemon
  • 2 tablespoons fresh lemon juice about one small lemon

Instructions

  • Cut the Cauliflower. Cut the cauliflower into evenly sized, medium-sized florets so they steam at the same rate.
  • Steam the Cauliflower. Steam for about 10 to 12 minutes, until very tender-it should mash easily with a fork. Optional: Add the garlic during the final 5 minutes to mellow its flavor. Drain well so excess water doesn't thin the cauliflower cream sauce.
  • Blend the Cauliflower Cream Sauce. Transfer the cauliflower and garlic to a blender. Add the artichoke hearts, nutritional yeast, mustard, onion powder, black pepper, and nondairy milk or broth. Blend until completely smooth, adding more liquid only as needed for a thick but pourable sauce.
  • Warm the Cauliflower Cream Sauce. Pour the blended sauce into a large skillet and warm over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally.
  • Add the White Beans. Gently fold the beans into the warm sauce and cook for about 5 to 7 minutes, until heated through. Add a splash of water or broth if needed to loosen the sauce.
  • Dissolve the Miso. In a small bowl, stir the miso with the water until smooth.
  • Finish Off the Heat. Remove the skillet from the stove and let cool slightly. Stir in the fresh herbs, lemon zest, lemon juice, and dissolved miso to taste. Serve with a sprinkle of fresh herbs.

Video

Notes

Why finish off the heat? Add lemon juice, zest, and miso after the sauce cools slightly to help preserve vitamin C, fresh citrus aroma, and any live cultures in unpasteurized miso. For serving: Serve with amaranth, quinoa, or a grain of your choice. Top with roasted delicata squash for a delicious balance of sweet and savory.

Brown Butter Financiers

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Of all the pastries in France, one of my favorites is the financier. Enriched with nuts, and moistened with butter, every bakery in France offers their own version of them. They come in different sizes, shapes, and even flavors; almond is the most popular, but you’ll sometimes come across financiers made with ground hazelnuts or pistachios. I like them all. There are a few theories how this mini-gâteau got its curious name.

One is that, traditionally, they’re baked in small, rectangular molds. Once baked and unmolded, the little cakes resemble bars of gold, which appeals to people in the financial world. Another is that people in France are known to indulge in an afternoon sweet stop at their local bakery, called a goûter. Because people who work in the financial industry normally wear nice outfits or suits, something that’s neat to eat is appreciated, so they can stay presentable when heading back to the office.

I don’t have to worry about that, nor has anything come out of my oven ever turned to gold. (Quelle dommage!) But when I found myself with some leftover brown butter from infusing it in bourbon for Brown Butter Old Fashioneds, since I treat butter like gold, I repurposed it as a base for a batch of financiers.

Using browned butter keeps the butter flavor even more in focus. Some people get a little anxious when they see dark specks in pastries and desserts; I once had a waiter ask me what the dark flecks in the vanilla ice cream were. When I told him they were vanilla beans, and asked him (incredulously) what he thought they were, he replied, “I thought they were dirt.”

So after you brown the butter, when pouring it out of the pan, you don’t want to scrape up too much of the dark bits at the bottom of the pan, but I don’t mind a few in my cakes (in fact, I prefer them). So no need to fret about having a few in your batter. These cakes are also very forgiving, which is why so many bakeries in France offer them. They’re easy to make, keep well, and are the perfect afternoon snack – or as the French say, un snack.

Print

Browned Butter Financiers

For financiers, I use mini-muffin tins, which are easily available. I’m not a fan of silicone bakeware but know that some people like it. This batter is pretty forgiving so can be baked in madeleine molds or even in larger muffin tins, filling them only about halfway. If you use another size mold, you’ll likely need to adjust the baking time; bake them until browned on top, and the feel just set in the center when you touch them. As mentioned, I made these with leftover brown butter from the Brown Butter Old Fashioned recipe. I started with 4 ounces (8 tablespoons/115g) of butter, which yielded the amount called for in the recipe. If starting from scratch, and making your own brown butter, start with that amount of brown butter, then you can measure it out when it’s browned and cooled. (If you need a bit more butter, you can simply add a bit of melted butter to it, to reach the 2 1/2 oz/75g amount. There are links at the end of the post with detailed instructions on making brown butter. Course Dessert Cuisine French Keyword almonds, financiers, french pastry Servings 24 financiers

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (140g) almond or hazelnut flour
  • 3/4 cup plus 2 tablespoons (180g) sugar
  • 5 tablespoons (45g) flour
  • generous pinch salt
  • 4 large egg whites at room temperature
  • 1/2 teaspoon pure vanilla or almond extract
  • 2 1/2 ounces (75g) brown butter slightly warm (liquified)

Instructions

  • Preheat the oven to 375ºF/180ºC and butter the insides of 24 mini muffin tins generously with softened, not melted, butter, making sure to butter the upper rims of the indentations.
  • In a medium bowl, mix the almond or hazelnut powder, sugar, flour and salt. Stir in the egg whites and vanilla or almond extract, then the browned butter.
  • Fill each indentation of the mini muffin tins almost to the top. Rap the tins sharply on the counter to level the tops, then bake for 13 minutes, until nicely browned. Let the financiers cool in the tins, then remove them, using a sharp knife to help release them, if necessary.

Notes

Storage: The financiers can be stored in an air-tight container for up to one week. They can be frozen for up to two months.

  • Sorghum flour: A naturally gluten-free flour that adds structure. Use it alongside the oat flour as written for the most predictable result. (See substitutions.)
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder: Use natural unsweetened cocoa powder for this version.
  • Baking powder, baking soda, and apple cider vinegar: Help lift the thick batter. Add the baking soda and vinegar at the end, then transfer the batter straight to the oven.

This recipe is versatile and easy to adapt. Below are some simple ingredient substitutions.

🙋🏽‍♀️ Recipe FAQs

How do you make a chocolate brownie recipe vegan?

Replace the eggs and dairy with ingredients that provide moisture, richness, and structure. In this recipe, blended tofu, bananas, nut butter, and nondairy milk do that work.

Can you taste the tofu?

Tofu has a mild flavor. Blend it completely smooth with the bananas, cocoa, nut butter, and vanilla so there are no visible pieces in the batter.

Can I use silken tofu?

Use extra-firm tofu for this recipe. Silken tofu contains more moisture and may change how the brownies set.

Are banana bread brownies gluten-free?

This version is made with sorghum flour and oat flour. Use certified gluten-free oat flour and check the other ingredient labels if you need the recipe to be gluten-free.

Why are my brownies gummy?

Too much banana, underbaking, or slicing while warm can contribute to a gummy center. Measure the banana carefully, bake until there's no wet batter on the tester, and cool completely before slicing.

Vegan Banana Bread Tofu Brownies Recipe

Super moist vegan banana bread tofu brownies. High-protein, gluten-free, rich in chocolate flavor, and sweetened with bananas and dates. Course Breakfast, Healthy Treat, Snack Cuisine Vegan Diet Vegan, Vegetarian Prep Time 10 minutes minutes Cook Time 55 minutes minutes Total Time 1 hour hour 5 minutes minutes Servings 8 servings Calories 288kcal Author Nisha Melvani

Equipment

  • Blender
  • Baking dish (see notes)

Ingredients

  • 3 Medjool dates
  • 2 cups mashed bananas about 4 very ripe bananas
  • ½ cup nondairy milk
  • 14 ounces extra-firm tofu drained and patted dry
  • 2 tablespoons peanut butter or nut or seed butter, or tahini
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • ¾ cup plus 2 tablespoons oat flour
  • 1 cup sorghum flour see notes
  • ⅓ cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 ½ teaspoons baking powder
  • ¼ teaspoon salt
  • ½ teaspoon baking soda
  • 2 teaspoons apple cider vinegar

Instructions

  • Soak the dates: Soak in hot water for 10 minutes to soften. Drain, remove the pits, and tear into smaller pieces for easier blending.
  • Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Line an 8-inch square baking pan with parchment paper.
  • Measure: Mash the bananas and measure 2 cups.
  • Drain the tofu: Drain and pat dry with a clean kitchen towel, and break into chunks.
  • Blend: Blend the milk, bananas, dates, tofu, nut butter, and vanilla until completely smooth. Use the blender's approved tamper, or stop and scrape down the sides as needed.
  • Combine: Whisk the oat flour, sorghum flour, cocoa powder, baking powder, and salt in a large bowl. Fold in the blended mixture until no dry flour remains.
  • Finish the batter: Sprinkle the baking soda evenly over the batter and pour the vinegar over it. Quickly fold until incorporated. Immediately transfer to the pan and smooth the top. Transfer the pan to the oven and bake right away for the best rise.
  • Bake: Bake for approximately 55 minutes, or until the top is set and springs back lightly when gently pressed. Let cool completely before slicing.
  • Cool: Cool completely in the pan before lifting out and slicing into 12 squares.
