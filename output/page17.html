Thanksgiving Quesadilla

From start to finish, this quesadilla only takes ten minutes to make. It’s built from leftovers, so it’s easy to adapt on the fly. So, don’t worry if you don’t have all the ingredients. Just use what you have.

I don’t even care if I sound crazy. I’ve been raving to all of my brothers and sisters, friends and strangers at the grocery store even, about this way to use up leftovers ever since I first landed on it, almost two years ago. A little bit of this and a little bit of that add up to whole lot of deliciousness!

Ingredients and Substitutions

Tortillas – I prefer the flavor and texture of flour tortillas, hands down. But, you can go with corn tortillas if you want. (If you choose to use corn tortillas, you’ll need two and you won’t be able to fold them as described below.)

Cheese – Monterey jack cheese is my go-to here, but whatever mild, melty cheese you have on hand will work well.

Butter – I use salted butter in all of my recipes, unless otherwise stated. It helps the tortilla brown and keep from sticking to the pan.

Turkey – Whatever leftover turkey you have on hand post-Thanksgiving will work. I always use our roast turkey. If you happen to run short of turkey, you can sub rotisserie chicken here to make the most of any remaining side dishes.

Green Bean Casserole – My favorite green bean casserole brings wonderful flavors and texture to the mix.

Stuffing – While it lasts (which isn’t long) I include some of this sausage apple cranberry stuffing. Any stuffing you have will work.

Creamed Corn – I love adding creamed corn (or even corn pudding if we’re lucky enough to have any leftover!) to the quesadilla for the sweet crisp bite the kernels add.

Mashed Potatoes – Mashed potatoes add some creamy, rich flavor and support .

Sweet Potatoes – If you have them, don’t sleep on adding sweet potatoes as well! They add a great, mildly sweet contrast to the other savory ingredients.

Cranberry Sauce – My boys and I love to use some leftover cranberry sauce for dipping. Don’t judge me til you’ve tried it.

Notes on Equipment

  • Large nonstick skillet. I have been through about a million skillets over the years. For the past several years this has been my everyday champ, and it crisps a fully loaded quesadilla without a thing sticking.
  • Grater
  • Flexible nylon turner. I reach for this turner almost every single day. It’s thin enough to slide cleanly under the folded quesadilla yet sturdy enough to flip it without losing the filling, and the nylon is gentle on my nonstick pan.
  • Sharp knife
  • Cutting board

How to Make a Thanksgiving Quesadilla

Gathering the Ingredients: Before I start cooking, I gather all of the ingredients and place them next to the stove for easy assembly.

Preheating the Pan: Set a large non-stick pan over medium-low heat on the stove to warm up.

Prepping the Ingredients: While it heats, you’ll want to shred the cheese and chop the turkey and green bean casserole into tiny pieces.

Buttering the Tortilla: I lightly butter one side of a tortilla. Once the pan is hot, I set it buttered side down in the pan.

Adding Cheese and Turkey: Sprinkle half of the tortilla with about half the cheese, then layer the chopped turkey on top of the cheese.

Topping with the Rest: You’ll want to layer very small amounts of the green bean casserole, stuffing, mashed potatoes, and sweet potatoes over the chicken. I use my hands to place little pieces of each dish all over half of the tortilla. Once I have all the layers in place, I add a final layer of cheese.

Folding It Over: Now you’ll gently fold the side of the tortilla over the fillings and press down with the back of a spatula.

Cooking: You’ll let it cook undisturbed for 2-3 minutes, until the cheese begins to melt and the bottom tortilla is a golden brown.

Flipping: I carefully flip the folded quesadilla over to the other side and let it continue cooking for another 2-3 minutes.

Serving: Once both sides are nice and crispy, use your spatula to transfer the quesadilla to a cutting board and slice into wedges while still hot. Serve warm with cranberry sauce.

Expert Tip

This is a really hearty quesadilla. Even if you’re only adding a little bit of each ingredient, it all adds up. And, when you’re working with a thick quesadilla like this, heat management plays a big role. While I normally cook my quesadillas over medium heat, I cook this one a little slower to allow all of the ingredients time to warm and grow toasty while the cheese melts. If you’d like to cook them faster, I recommend heating the leftovers in the microwave for 30 seconds or so before cooking the quesadillas.

Use Your Hands – I find it simplest to just use my hands to distribute small portions of potatoes and green beans and stuffing across the quesadilla. Yes, it’s messy, but the results are worth it and you’ll have plenty of time to wash up while the quesadilla is getting toasty in the pan. Also, disposable gloves are a beautiful thing.

Chopping Filling Ingredients Finely – Chop those ingredients smaller than you think. The pieces of turkey and green beans need to be tiny and bite-size, or it’s going to be messy trying to eat it. Aim for ½” pieces.

Keeping Layers Thin – While it might be tempting to pack as much as possible into these quesadillas, patience is key. Cramming too much filling into these quesadillas will prevent them from cooking evenly, and the tortilla may not be able to keep everything contained.

Serving Suggestions

The one thing I never skip is a little dish of cranberry on the side for dipping. homemade cranberry sauce is the classic move, but hot pepper cranberry sauce brings a little heat that plays well against all that savory filling. A spoonful of cranberry chutney or cranberry pepper jelly is good here too, if you have a jar in the refrigerator.

Make Ahead & Storage

How to Store: Leftover quesadilla wedges can be stored in an airtight container in the fridge for 3 days.

How to Reheat: I usually reheat the quesadilla in a nonstick skillet on medium heat, to refresh the crispiness. You can also reheat in the air fryer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best way to flip a quesadilla without spilling?

I usually use a large spatula to circle the base of the pan, checking for any areas where the tortilla might be stuck. After doing that, I slip the spatula underneath the quesadilla in one quick, smooth motion. Then, I lift the quesadilla just enough to be able to flip it over on the folded edge, keeping the tortilla within the bounds of the pan.

Should I strain/drain any of the filling ingredients?

I typically don’t need to. But, if something has a lot of juices, you’re right to be concerned. Too much moisture is a bad thing. Use a slotted spoon and leave the juice behind.

How do I slice a quesadilla without making a mess?

One of the best things I’ve learned is to wait about a minute and let it rest before slicing. The cheese is still plenty hot, but it binds the other ingredients better.

Course Main Course Cuisine Mexican Prep Time 10 minutes minutes Cook Time 5 minutes minutes Total Time 10 minutes minutes Servings 1 serving Calories 509kcal Author Mary Younkin

Ingredients

  • 1 teaspoon butter
  • 1 8-10" flour tortilla
  • ⅓ cup freshly shredded Monterey jack cheese
  • ¼ cup Roast Turkey chopped into ½-inch pieces
  • 2-3 tablespoons Green Bean Casserole roughly chopped into ½-inch pieces
  • 2-3 tablespoons Sausage Apple Cranberry Stuffing or your favorite stuffing recipe
  • 2 tablespoons Creamed Corn
  • 2 tablespoons Herb Mashed Potatoes
  • 2 tablespoons Whipped Sweet Potatoes
  • Cranberry Sauce for serving

Instructions

  • Place all of the quesadilla fillings next to the stove for easy assembly. Warm a nonstick skillet over medium-low heat. Lightly butter one side of the tortilla with butter and place it in the hot pan. Generously sprinkle cheese over the tortilla and top half of one side with turkey.
  • Working quickly, top the turkey with green bean casserole, stuffing, mashed potatoes, and sweet potatoes. Fold the cheese side of the tortilla over the fillings and press it down over them. Cover with a lid.
  • Let the quesadilla cook for 2-3 minutes. When the cheese is beginning to melt and the bottom tortilla is browning slightly, uncover and flip the folded quesadilla over to cook the other side.
  • Continue to cook uncovered until all the cheese is melted and the tortilla is crisp around the edges. Using a spatula, transfer the quesadilla back to a cutting board and cut it into wedges while it is hot and serve warm with cranberry sauce.

I'm talking decadence! Think peanut avocado sushi, cream cheese veggie sushi, or smoked tofu sushi. These rolls will not leave you hungry, and OMG are they tasty.

But my favorite sushi roll to order at a restaurant has always been tempura pumpkin sushi. Have you had it? These rolls are stuffed with sweet winter squash covered in crispy tempura batter and served with eel sauce (which is often accidentally vegan!). It's about as delicious and satisfying as sushi gets, and I had to come up with a recipe so I could make it at home.

This vegan pumpkin sushi works with most types of sweet winter squash, so it's perfect for the cooler months of the year. You'll be dipping strips of the squash in tempura batter (adapted from my vegetable tempura recipe), then frying them until golden and crispy, and wrapping it all in sushi rice and nori. It's exactly what you need on those occasions when you're craving sushi and comfort food.

Ingredients You'll Need

Below you'll find a list of ingredients in this recipe, with notes and substitutions. Scroll all the way to the bottom of the post to see the full recipe, including the amount of each ingredient.

  • Sushi rice. Stick with sushi rice rather than any old variety of rice for this recipe. It's super sticky and has a short grain, so it's perfect for shaping and holding together sushi rolls.
  • Rice vinegar. You can find this in the international section of the grocery store.
  • Salt.
  • Sugar. Use organic sugar to keep the recipe vegan. Conventional sugar may be processed with animal bone char.
  • Soy sauce. Tamari and liquid aminos will both work as substitutes.
  • Brown sugar. This should also be organic.
  • Mirin. This is a cooking wine you can find in the international section of most grocery stores.
  • Cornstarch.
  • Toasted sesame oil. This is a flavoring oil — not a cooking oil. Look for it in the international aisle.
  • Vegetable oil. Any high-heat oil you normally use for frying is good here — peanut oil, corn oil, and canola oil are all fine options.
  • Winter squash. I generally use butternut squash for my pumpkin rolls, but acorn, kabocha, and delicata are also great options. Sugar pumpkin will work, but it won't be as sweet or dense as the other options.
  • Flour. I've only tested the recipe with all-purpose wheat flour (also known as white flour), so that's your best bet for success!
  • Beer or seltzer water. I love making my tempura with beer, which adds a savory, slightly bitter flavor. But seltzer water is totally fine if that's what you've got.
  • Baking powder. You only need this if you're going with seltzer water.
  • Nori.
  • Accompaniments. You'll want to grab some toasted sesame seeds, pickled ginger, and wasabi paste from the international aisle at the store.

How It's Made

Below is a detailed photo tutorial on how to make this dish. Scroll all the way down if you'd like to skip right to the recipe!

Step 1: Soak the rice. Place it in a saucepan and cover it with water. Let it sit for about 20 minutes, then drain and rinse it. Return it to the pot.

Step 2: Cook the rice. Add water to the pot, along with rice vinegar, salt, and sugar. Place it over high heat, get the water boiling, then reduce the heat and let it simmer until the water is absorbed. Leave the lid on the pot for about 10 minutes when it's done, then fluff it with a fork.

Steps 3 & 4: Make the sauce. Combine your soy sauce, brown sugar, and mirin in a small pot, place it over medium heat, and bring it to a simmer. Let it cook at a low simmer for five minutes, while you mix some cold water and cornstarch. Add the mixture to the sauce, let it thicken, then take it off of heat and add the sesame oil.

Step 5: Heat your oil. Place about 2 inches of oil into a large pot, and set it over medium heat. Let it heat up while you begin preparing the fillings. You'll want it to get to about 350°F.

Step 6: Precook the squash. Cut the squash into small baton-shaped pieces, arrange them on a microwave-safe plate, cover it with a damp paper towel, then zap them for about 3 minutes. You just want to start softening them up.

Steps 7 & 8: Prepare dipping stations. Place some flour in one bowl, and in a second bowl, whisk together flour, cornstarch, salt, and either ale or seltzer water. If you go with seltzer water, you'll also need some baking powder.

Step 9: Fry the squash. Start by dredging a few pieces in the flour you just added to the bowl, then dip them in the batter, and transfer them to the hot oil. Fry your squash in batches, being careful not to crowd the pot. Once they're golden brown, place them on a wire rack to drain.

Steps 10 & 11: Prepare to roll. Cover your bamboo mat with plastic, then place a sheet of nori over the mat. Spread rice in a thin layer on the nori, wetting your hands to keep the rice from sticking.

Steps 12-16: Fill and roll. Flip your rice covered nori, then arrange the squash in a strip, about an inch from the edge. Roll it up tightly. Slice it into 8 pieces, then repeat the process to make 3 more rolls.

Tip: This short video demonstrates the process for rolling uramaki, also known as an inside out sushi roll. If you're not feeling that today, try making this recipe as maki (nori on the outside), which you can see demonstrated in this video.

Step 17: Finish and serve. Sprinkle your rolls with sesame seeds, then serve them with your vegan eel sauce, pickled ginger, and wasabi.

Variations

  • Add to the filling. Tempura-fried squash goes great with chives, avocado, vegan cream cheese, pickled carrots, daikon, or cucumber. Or get creative and try your own additions!
  • Crunchy topping. Sprinkle the finished rolls with crushed tempura bits or fried onions for some extra crunch.
  • Quinoa sushi. Replace up to half the sushi rice with cooked quinoa.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can this recipe be made gluten-free?

I haven't tried making this recipe with gluten-free flour, so I'm not sure. If you'd like to try it, I think an all-purpose gluten-free flour blend would be your best bet. You'll also need to make sure your beer is gluten-free (or stick with seltzer), and replace the soy sauce with gluten-free tamari.

Can I make this recipe without frying?

Yes, but I recommend skipping the tempura if you'd like to bake it. Simply roast the squash instead. You can follow the basic method in my roasted sweet potato sushi recipe.

Can I use brown rice?

Yes, but make sure it's brown sushi rice. You may need to alter the cooking method a bit — refer to the package instructions.

How should I store leftover pumpkin sushi?

Pumpkin sushi is best served the day it's made, but if you do have leftovers, they'll keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to three days.

Tempura Pumpkin Sushi

This pumpkin sushi is made with sweet, crispy crusted pieces of winter squash tucked inside rolls of nori and rice, then finished with a tangy vegan "eel" sauce. It's a fun, decadent homemade sushi roll that's perfect for fall! Course Entree Cuisine American, Japanese Prep Time 20 minutes minutes Cook Time 35 minutes minutes Soak Time 20 minutes minutes Total Time 1 hour hour 15 minutes minutes Servings 4 Calories 652kcal Author Alissa Saenz

Equipment

  • 1 Sushi mat
  • 1 Instant read thermometer

Ingredients

For the Rice

  • 1 ⅓ cups sushi rice
  • 1 ½ cups water, plus more for soaking
  • 2 tablespoons rice vinegar
  • 1 ½ teaspoons salt
  • 1 teaspoon organic granulated sugar

For the Vegan Eel Sauce

  • ¼ cup soy sauce
  • ¼ cup organic brown sugar
  • ¼ cup mirin
  • 1 ½ tablespoons cold water
  • 2 teaspoons cornstarch
  • 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil

For the Filling

  • Vegetable oil
  • 12 ounces winter squash (such as butternut, kabocha, delicata, or acorn), cut into ¼ to ½ inch thick batons
  • ½ cup all-purpose flour

For the Tempura Batter

  • ½ cup all-purpose flour
  • ¼ cup cornstarch
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • ¼ teaspoon baking powder (only use if you're making the batter with seltzer — do not use with beer)
  • ¾ cup vegan ale or plain seltzer water, chilled

For Rolling

  • 4 nori sheets

For Serving

  • Toasted sesame seeds
  • Pickled ginger
  • Wasabi paste

Instructions

  • Place the rice into a medium saucepan and cover it with cold water. Let it sit for 20 minutes, then drain the water and return the rice to the pot.
  • Add the 1 ½ cups of water, rice vinegar, salt, and sugar. Stir, then set the pot over medium heat. Bring the water to a boil, cover the pot, and reduce the heat to a low simmer. Let the rice simmer for about 20 minutes, until the liquid is all absorbed. Turn off the heat and let the pot sit covered for 10 minutes. Uncover the pot and fluff the rice with a fork.
  • Make the sauce while the rice cooks. Stir the soy sauce, brown sugar, and mirin together in a small saucepan. Set it over medium heat and bring the mixture to a simmer. Reduce the heat and let it cook at a low simmer for about 5 minutes.
  • While the mixture simmers, stir the cold water and cornstarch together in a small container. After the sauce has simmered for 5 minutes, stir in the cornstarch mixture and bring the mixture back to a simmer. Once it starts to simmer, it should thicken slightly. Remove it from the heat, then stir in the sesame oil. Let the sauce cool.
  • Place about 2 inches of oil into a large pot and set it over medium heat.
  • While the oil heats up, place the squash batons onto a microwave-safe plate and cover them with a damp paper towel. Microwave the squash for about 3 minutes, until the pieces are just beginning to soften up. Be careful when removing the paper towel, as the squash will release hot steam.
  • Place the ½ cup of flour into a small bowl.
  • In a second bowl, whisk together the batter ingredients.
  • Once the oil reaches about 350°F, place a few of the squash batons into the bowl containing the flour. Gently roll the batons to coat them in flour, then, one at a time, dip each baton into the batter. Coat the baton with batter, then shake off any excess before transferring it to the hot oil. Repeat the process, adding as many batons as you can at a time without crowding (you'll end up with about 4 batches). Fry the batons for about 3 minutes, then use a slotted spoon to transfer them to a wire rack to drain. Continue until all of the squash is fried.
  • Wrap a bamboo mat in plastic wrap and place one sheet of nori over top. Keep a small bowl of water nearby and wet your fingertips.
  • Distribute one quarter of your rice in an even layer over the nori, pressing it down until it's nice and thin. Wet your hands periodically to help prevent the rice from sticking to them.
  • Carefully invert your rice-covered nori sheet so that the rice layer sits on the plastic wrap and the nori is face up.
  • Arrange about a quarter of your squash filling in a strip extending widthwise across your nori, about an inch away from you.
  • Carefully roll the end of the mat closest to you over your filling strip, tucking and squeezing to get it nice and tight. Continue rolling until you reach the end.
  • Take your roll and place it seam side down on a cutting board. Using a large, sharp knife, cut the roll into eight pieces, wiping the blade clean with a damp towel between cuts.
  • Repeat the process to make three more rolls using the remaining ingredients.
  • Sprinkle the rolls with toasted sesame seeds, then serve with vegan eel sauce, pickled ginger, and wasabi.

Ingredients You'll Need

Below you'll find a list of ingredients in this recipe, with notes and substitutions. Scroll all the way to the bottom of the post to see the full recipe, including the amount of each ingredient.

  • Flour. We're using all-purpose wheat flour (also known as white flour), which is the only variety I've tested the recipe with, so it's the only type I can promise will work.
  • Sugar. Use organic sugar to keep the recipe vegan. Conventional sugar is sometimes processed with animal bone char.
  • Baking soda.
  • Spices. You'll need cinnamon, ginger, cloves, and nutmeg.
  • Salt.
  • Apple cider. Make sure you're using apple cider, and not apple cider vinegar. In some regions it's called cloudy apple juice. Confused? Make sure you're buying a beverage and not something from the salad dressing aisle.
  • Vegetable oil. Any neutral baking oil will work, such as canola oil, corn oil, or melted coconut oil.
  • Applesauce.
  • Vanilla extract.
  • Vegan butter. Most grocery stores carry this in the refrigerated section, near the regular butter.

How It's Made

Below is a detailed photo tutorial on how to make this dish. Scroll all the way down if you'd like to skip right to the recipe!

Steps 1 & 2: Prepare. Start by preheating your oven to 350°F. Grease a 10-inch bundt pan, then flour it.

Step 3: Mix dry ingredients. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking soda, spices, and salt together in a nice big bowl.

Step 4: Mix wet ingredients. Stir the apple cider, oil, applesauce, and vanilla together in a separate container.

Step 5: Mix the batter. Add the wet ingredients to the dry, then beat everything together with an electric mixer. Just mix until the batter is smooth.

Step 6: Pour and bake. Pour the batter into your greased and floured pan, then bake the cake for about 40 to 45 minutes. A toothpick will come out clean when it's done.

Steps 7 & 8: Cool then remove. Let the cake cool completely in the pan on a cooling rack, then run a knife along the edges to loosen it before inverting it on a plate.

Steps 9 & 10: Top it. Brush the outside of the cake with melted vegan butter, then sprinkle it with a mix of cinnamon and sugar.

Step 11: Serve. Your apple cider doughnut cake is ready! Slice it up and enjoy!

Variations

  • Add spices. Additional warming spices like cardamom, allspice, and even black pepper would work in this cake. Get creative, but start conservative, as these spices are potent!
  • Drizzle with glaze. Make a glaze by stirring together 1 cup of powdered sugar, 2 tablespoons non-dairy milk, and a teaspoon of vanilla. Drizzle the cake instead of or in addition to coating it with cinnamon sugar.
  • Powdered sugar coating. Replace the cinnamon sugar with powdered sugar, or, better yet, cinnamon powdered sugar by mixing 1 cup powdered sugar with 1 tablespoon ground cinnamon.
  • Stir-ins. Add chopped nuts, diced apple, or dried fruit such as raisins to the batter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can this cake be made gluten-free?

I'm not sure. If you'd like to try it, an all-purpose gluten-free flour blend would be the safest option, but I haven't tested this myself.

My cake doesn't want to come out of the pan. Help!

Keep running a knife around the edges, both inner and outer, loosening as you go. Be patient, and gently pry the cake out when it starts to feel loose. I also have better luck when I grease my bundt pan with vegetable shortening, as opposed to cooking spray, so consider using that for your next cake.

Can I use apple juice instead of apple cider?

I haven't tried this, but generally a mix of apple juice and applesauce is the best substitute for cider. Use ¾ cup of apple juice and an additional ¼ cup of applesauce.

How should I store my cake and how long will it keep?

Store your apple cider doughnut cake in an airtight container, such as a cake keeper, at room temperature for up to a day, in the fridge for up to 5 days, or in the freezer for up to 3 months.

  • 2 ¾ cups all-purpose flour

  • 2 teaspoons baking soda
  • 1 tablespoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 teaspoon ground ginger
  • ½ teaspoon ground cloves
  • ¼ teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • ¾ teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup apple cider (not apple cider vinegar - Note 1)
  • ¾ cup vegetable oil

  • 1 cup organic granulated sugar

Instructions

  • Preheat the oven to 350°F.

  • In a separate container such as a large liquid measuring cup, stir together the apple cider, oil, applesauce, and vanilla extract.
  • Pour the apple cider mixture into the bowl with the flour mixture, then beat everything together using an electric mixer on high speed for about 1 minute, until the batter is smooth.

Coconut and Pecan Combination

The combination of coconut and pecans is what really makes this cake special. In this recipe the coconut is added to the batter, but I’ve eaten plenty of Italian Cream Cakes with coconut in the frosting. Lots of chopped pecans are in both the batter and the frosting. Be sure to finely chop them or you’ll have a heck of a time spreading the frosting.

This cake has quite a few eggs in it. Five to be exact. The eggs are separated and the egg whites are beaten to stiff peaks and then folded into the batter, helping to give it a light texture.

  1. Prepare the cake pans. Grease and flour 3 9-inch cake pans and preheat the oven to 350 degrees F.
  2. Make the cake batter. Cream together the butter and shortening, then beat in sugar and egg yolks.

Italian Cream Cake

The combination of coconut and pecans is what really makes this layer cake. Covered in a creamy, cream cheese frosting. Course Dessert Cuisine Southern Keyword Christmas, layer cake Prep Time 20 minutes minutes Cook Time 25 minutes minutes Total Time 45 minutes minutes Servings 20 servings Calories 660kcal Author Christin Mahrlig

Equipment

  • 9-inch cake pans

Ingredients

  • 8 tablespoons butter (1 stick) softened
  • 1/2 cup vegetable shortening
  • 2 cups sugar
  • 5 egg yolks
  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons vanilla
  • 1 1/4 cups sweetened shredded coconut
  • 1 cup chopped toasted pecans
  • 5 egg whites

Frosting

  • 2 (8-ounce) packages cream cheese softened
  • 12 tablespoons butter softened
  • 2 (1-pound) boxes powdered sugar
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 2 ½ cups chopped toasted pecans

Instructions

  • Preheat oven to 350 degrees. Grease and flour 3 9-inch cake pans.
  • Using an electric mixer, cream butter and shortening.
  • Add sugar and beat until smooth.
  • Add egg yolks and mix thoroughly.
  • In a medium bowl, whisk together flour, baking soda, and salt.
  • Add flour mixture to butter mixture alternating with buttermilk.
  • Stir in vanilla extract.
  • Stir in coconut and pecans.
  • Using a clean bowl and a whisk attachment, beat the egg whites until stiff peaks form. See below for tips on beating egg whites.
  • Fold in egg whites and pour batter into prepared pans.
  • Bake at 350 degrees for 22 to 25 minutes. Let cool.
  • To make frosting, beat cream cheese and butter until smooth.
  • Beat in powdered sugar 1 cup at a time.
  • Add vanilla extract and stir in pecans. If too thick you can add a tablespoon or 2 of milk.
  • Spread frosting on top of bottom layer of cake. Top with second layer. Cover with Frosting. Top with third layer and spread remaining frosting on top and sides. Store in refrigerator.

Tips for Beating Egg Whites
  • Separate the eggs while they are cold, but beat the egg whites when they are at room temperature.
  • Use a clean bowl. Any traces of grease or oil will effect the volume you get in the eggs.
  • Make sure there are no traces of egg yolk in the whites.

Ingredients and Substitutions

The Beans – I’ll give my full advice on how to buy the best green beans in the Expert Tip, but be choosy with your beans. This is the ingredient that makes or breaks this casserole.

Butter & Garlic – I use salted butter and fresh garlic when cooking the mushrooms. It makes a difference, but you can swap for garlic powder in a pinch.

How to Make Fresh Green Bean Casserole

Getting Stuff Started: I start by setting a pot of water over high heat on the stove. Then, I preheat the oven to 350°F.

Prepping the Vegetables: While the water heats up, we’ll rinse the green beans. Then, you just have to trim the ends and chop them into 2-inch pieces. I clean the mushrooms and then chop them into ½-inch pieces, and mince the garlic while I’m at it.

Boiling the Beans: Once the water is boiling, I add the green beans and salt. You’ll let them cook for 5-6 minutes until tender crisp.

Setting Up the Ice: While the beans are cooking, we’re going to fill a large bowl with ice water and set it up next to the sink.

Chilling the Beans: Drain the beans in a colander and plunge them immediately into the ice water to stop cooking. Once cool, I drain them again.

Cooking the Mushrooms: Don’t wash that pot! Instead, we’re going to melt butter over medium-high heat in it. Then, I add the mushrooms, garlic, salt, and pepper. You need to cook them about 5 minutes, until all the liquid has been released, reabsorbed, and evaporated.

Adding Flour: Sprinkle the flour over the mushrooms and cook for about a minute, stirring constantly.

Making the Sauce: I stir in the chicken broth next and keep stirring until it comes to a simmer. Then, reduce the heat to medium and add your heavy cream.

Simmering: Continue stirring and simmer for 2 to 3 minutes, until the sauce thickens. Give it a taste and add salt and pepper if desired.

Assembling the Casserole: I place the green beans in a 9×13 baking dish and pour the sauce over them. Stir carefully so everything is evenly coated.

Baking: Top the casserole with a layer of fried onions, and then pop it into the oven for 15-20 minutes. You’ll know it’s ready when the sauce is hot and bubbling.

Expert Tip

The secret to the best green bean casserole should probably come as no surprise. It’s all about the texture. When you blanch the beans, you don’t want to overcook them. We’re just giving them the head start they need before going into the oven. The goal is for them to come out of the oven mostly soft with just the slightest bite remaining. You don’t want a soft, mushy filling beneath a crunchy topping. We’re going to serve a creamy sauce, soft mushrooms, slightly crisp green beans, and a crunchy topping. Together, that balance of textures takes it right over the top.

Taste Before Baking – This is the pro move that chefs make. So, don’t skip the taste test. Make sure that before you load up the baking dish, you’re happy with the balance of flavors in the cream sauce. Adjust salt and pepper to taste!

Make It Ahead – I used to juggle all the things on the stove and in the oven on the big day. But, you can actually make the sauce and blanch the green beans ahead of time, and store them separately a few days in advance. That makes it super easy to assemble the casserole and pop it into the oven right before the meal.

Let It Rest – There are two good reasons why you don’t want to serve this one immediately. First, that’s a recipe for getting burned. Nobody likes that! But, on another level, you need to give it some time to cool so that the sauce can thicken.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I make green bean casserole ahead of time?

I typically prep this recipe a few days before I plan to serve it. I wrap the boiled and chilled green beans in a paper towel, then place them in a gallon-size freezer bag in the refrigerator. Can I use a slow cooker for this one?

Absolutely! I’m a huge believer in using slow cookers on Thanksgiving to ensure everything stays hot and finishes at the same time. Just combine the green beans and the sauce in a 6 qt slow cooker and set it to HIGH until bubbling, then reduce to LOW for another 2-3 hours. What kind of green beans should I use?

Fresh Green Bean Casserole

Skip the canned soup! This Fresh Green Bean Casserole has a from-scratch mushroom cream sauce, and crispy fried onions on top. Course Side Dish Cuisine American Prep Time 15 minutes minutes Cook Time 30 minutes minutes Total Time 45 minutes minutes Servings 10 servings Calories 260kcal Author Mary Younkin

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs fresh green beans ends trimmed and chopped into 2-inch pieces
  • 2 tablespoons kosher salt for cooking
  • 3 tablespoons butter
  • 1 lb fresh mushrooms chopped into ½-inch pieces
  • 4 cloves garlic minced
  • ½ teaspoon kosher salt
  • ¼ teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
  • ¼ cup all-purpose flour
  • 1½ cups chicken broth
  • 1½ cups heavy cream
  • 3-6 ounces canned fried onions for topping

Instructions

Blanching Instructions

  • Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add green beans and 2 tablespoons salt to boiling water. Cook beans until bright green and crisp-tender, about 5-6 minutes.
  • Fill a large bowl with ice water while beans are cooking. Set it next to the sink. Drain beans in a colander and plunge immediately into ice water to stop cooking. Once cooled, drain beans again in the colander.

Sauce Instructions

  • Add butter to the empty pot and melt over medium-high heat until foaming subsides. Add mushrooms, garlic, salt, and pepper; cook until mushrooms are tender and all liquid evaporates, about 5 minutes.
  • Sprinkle mushrooms with flour and cook for 1 minute, stirring constantly. Stir in broth and bring to simmer, stirring constantly. Add cream, reduce heat to medium, and simmer until sauce thickens, about 2-3 minutes. Season to taste with salt and pepper.

Assembly Instructions

  • Preheat the oven to 350°F. Place the green beans in a 9×13 baking dish. Pour the sauce over the beans. Top with an even layer of fried onions. Bake for 15-20 minutes, until the beans and sauce are hot and bubbling.

Mushroom Quesadilla

My kids aren’t always fans of mushrooms, but every once in a while I find a recipe that leaves everyone coming back for seconds. And, these mushroom quesadillas do exactly that.

Ingredients and Substitutions

Mushrooms – I use fresh button or cremini mushrooms, because I like their flavor and it’s a breeze to slice them up and cook them.

Tortillas – I prefer flour tortillas for flavor and texture. You can use corn tortillas if you prefer.

Cheese – Monterey jack cheese is my go-to for quesadillas with more savory, complex flavors.

Butter – I prefer salted butter in all of my cooking unless specifically noted otherwise.

Garlic – I will always prefer fresh garlic. However, frozen garlic is often in my kitchen and it is my second choice here.

How to Make a Mushroom Quesadilla

Cooking the Mushrooms

Prepping the Ingredients: I start by cleaning the mushrooms and then slicing them and chopping the parsley. You’ll also mince the garlic at this point.

Warming the Skillet: You’ll need to set a large non-stick skillet over medium-high heat on the stove for 2-3 minutes to heat it up fully.

Cooking the Mushrooms: Add the oil to the pan and then add the mushrooms. Toss to coat them in oil and then spread them out in an even layer. You’ll want to let them cook for about 3 minutes per side, flipping and stirring the mushrooms with a spatula. They should release and reabsorb their liquid, and develop a lovely brown crust.

Adding Butter and Garlic: Add 2 teaspoons of butter and the minced garlic to the pan and cook for another minute or so with the mushrooms, until fragrant.

Seasoning: I remove the pan from the heat and season with salt and pepper to taste. Transfer the finished mushrooms to a plate.

Making the Quesadilla

Cleaning the Skillet: I use a paper towel or cloth to wipe out the skillet before returning it to the pan at medium heat.

Buttering the Tortilla: I lightly butter one side of the tortilla. Then, I place it in the hot pan, butter-side down.

Filling the Quesadilla:  You’ll want to sprinkle half of the cheese on one side of the tortilla. Then spread mushrooms out over the cheese and sprinkle with parsley. Top with the remaining cheese.

Folding the Tortilla: Once all the filling ingredients have been added to the quesadilla, you’ll want to fold the tortilla over on itself and let cook for 2-3 minutes, until the cheese has begun to melt and the bottom tortilla is golden brown.

Flipping the Quesadilla: Flip the folded quesadilla over to cook the other side for another 2-3 minutes.

Serving: Once the quesadilla has browned on both sides, you can use a spatula to transfer the quesadilla to a cutting board. Slice into wedges and serve warm.

Expert Tip

If the tortilla is beginning to char before the cheese has fully melted, that’s a job for one of my favorite superheroes: Heat Management! Reduce the heat to medium-low and cover the skillet with a lid for about a minute. That will trap heat above the quesadilla to help melt the cheese while preventing it from burning.

Buttering the Tortilla – Taking a moment to spread butter directly on the tortilla gives you much more even browning than greasing the skillet, guaranteeing that perfect golden crispy texture all the way around. (Thanks to my sister Jenny for teaching me this trick!)

Slicing the Mushrooms – Make sure to take the time to finely slice the mushrooms when preparing the ingredients. I typically halve the mushrooms, then slice any large sections left over into uniform, quarter-inch pieces. This helps the mushrooms cook quickly and evenly in the skillet.

Fully Cooking Mushrooms – When you’re cooking the mushrooms by themselves, they will first release liquid into the pan, and then reabsorb it. You want to leave them on one side until they brown, then stir and let them brown again. You shouldn’t have to tend them more than a time or two. Once they have absorbed the liquid, you’ll be ready to add the butter and garlic.

Gelatin-Rich "Liquid Gold" Chicken Stock (Instant Pot, Pressure Cooker, or Stockpot)

📅 Oct 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

I keep canned chicken broth in my pantry, and I also make my own for excellent flavors that tilt toward Asia. I've been making chicken stock for decades and used to use only chicken parts. With the price of chicken going up and parts not easy to obtain, I've changed my recipe to include meaty bone-in parts because they are easier to get, chicken feet for their rich collagen and round mouthfeel, and everyday vegetables. You can omit the vegetables but scraps are what go into my chicken stock. It's affordable and sophisticated but you don't have to spend a lot of money or time.

The reward is luxurious "liquid gold" to enhance your recipes, whether it's noodle soup, a stir-fry, or braise. Or, just warm 1 cup in a pot, add leafy greens and or tofu, for a nourishing soup. I also love to sip on the broth as a nourishing life giving beverage.

Below you'll find my recipe to make flavorful stock fast in a pressure cooker (it takes about 1 hour of actual cooking and you don't have to monitor the pot much), tips for cooking stock in stockpot on the stove, and an explainer on why I add certain vegetables to my chicken stock formula. From there, you can confidently put your own imprint on your chicken stock.

The Secret to "Liquid Gold" Stock: Chicken Feet & Vegetable Scraps

After years of making stock from scratch, I know this is true: choose chicken parts and veggies wisely for flavorful results.

Chicken parts like legs and breasts alone don't yield super-tasty stock because, although they are meaty but they don't have enough skin or fat for richness. Given that, combine chicken parts with chicken feet (often sold as 1-lb | 450g packages at the market). Or, use backs, though they can be harder to find sometimes. may be fatty, they aren't meaty enough. Don't see chicken feet or backs? Ask for them at the meat counter or, save backs when you break down a whole chicken. If backs and feet are totally unavailable, use all meaty parts.

To build flavor, add produce. You don't need much and can use scraps. Over-the-hill veggies that are mildly dehydrated work well because they have concentrated flavor. Here are my favorites, which are listed in the recipe below. You don't have to use all of these vegetables! HOWEVER, ginger and onion are required.

  • Ginger and onion lend sweet pungency.
  • A sweet apple, such as Fuji, lends a mild sweetness.
  • Carrot lends sweetness and golden color. Scrub and don't peel because the peel adds complexity to the stock.
  • Corn cobs have sweetness but add too much and the stock will have a corny flavor. After cutting off kernels, freeze the cobs.
  • Asparagus ends are full of umami. Trim them when prepping asparagus and freeze them for stock, if you're not using them ASAP.
  • Mushrooms add earthiness and umami but don't add too much or they may darken the broth or take over flavor. Use dried shiitake mushroom, or fresh cremini or white button mushroom. Save the dried stems from dried shiitake prep for stock.
  • Cabbage leaves (napa or green kinds) add verdant sweetness and minerality. Wilted leaves that you'd discard actually have extra potent flavor. Red cabbage will color your stock reddish!
  • Celery has a verdant minerality to support the chicken vibe well. Include the leaves of the rib too.
  • Dried kombu (kelp) add gelatinous texture and umami flravor.
  • Cilantro sprigs or coriander seeds contribute a pungent citrusy notes.

Instant Pot vs pressure cooker vs. stockpot chicken stock

Which to use? Despited being branded a multicooker, the Instant Pot is basically a pressure cooker but you can set it and forget it. The pressure cooker requires a little monitoring but is easier to clean than the Instant pot. Pressure cookers extra flavor fast and efficiently. I like it for stock. (If you need assistance, check my guide to buying a pressure cooker and Instant Pot.) On the other hand, the stockpot produces lovely stock too. It just takes about 30 to 40 percent longer and you need to monitor water level.

Chicken stock tips

Some bonus tips to further guide you to beautiful chicken stock:

  • Chicken part swaps: When whole chickens are on sale, buy a couple, cut them up (or ask a butcher to do the deed) and use the backs and some of the meaty parts for stock. You want about 2 ½ (pounds total.
  • Parboil parts or not? If your chicken parts are hacked though the bone, parboil them for about 1 minute in water to cover to remove the scummy denatured protein bits. That will yield cleaner tasting and clearer stock. Parboiling does not diminish the flavor.
  • To make stock in a stockpot: Put all the ingredients in a 6 to 8-quart stockpot. Add 8 cups water. Bring to a gentle simmer and continue simmering, uncovered, for 2 hours. Add water if the liquid level falls below the solids. Remove from the heat and let cool and rest for 15 minutes before straining. If needed, boil to reduce to about 6 cups, or add water if the stock is too strong.
  • Save the harvested fat for stir-frying, refried beans, and other dishes. Freeze it. If it's liquidy, watch out when reheating because it will spit.

Once things get going, gingery and chicken-y aromas will soon fill your kitchen, and you'll have a rich, viscous stock to enhance your food.

Stock tips for using your liquid gold

A good chicken stock in the fridge or freezer is like money in the bank. Add quick cooking vegetables, meat or tofu to a cup for a nourishing soup lunch. Employ it for my fast chicken pho and yours will be extra delicious. You'll have a headstart on a sensational batch of Viet asparagus, shrimp, and egg drop soup too!

Finally deploy this stock to seed flavor in my shortcut Vietnamese rotisserie chicken noodle soup (mi ga tiem) and Hainan chicken rice (com ga Hai Nam). Both recipes are at my newsletter, Pass the Fish Sauce.

Instant Pot Chicken Stock

You can use this recipe to make the stock in a regular pressure cooker. See the main post to make this stock in a stockpot. Makes about 6 cups

Equipment

  • Instant Pot or pressure cooker

Ingredients

  • 1 to 1 ½ pounds | 450g to 1.13kg chicken drumsticks thighs, or breast
  • 1 pound chicken feet
  • Chubby 1½-inch | 45g section ginger unpeeled, cut into 3 pieces and bruised
  • 1 medium yellow onion halved and thickly sliced
  • 1 ¼ teaspoons fine sea salt

Optional additions (use some, all, or none)

  • ½ small Fuji apple peeled and chopped, or ½ corn cob
  • 1 medium carrot scrubbed and sliced
  • 1 celery rib coarsely chopped
  • 1 small handful asparagus woody ends
  • 3 large cremini 2 medium dried shiitake mushrooms, or 6 to 8 dried shiitake mushroom stems
  • 2 or 3 napa or green cabbage leaves coarsely chopped
  • 6 square inches 7.5cm kombu
  • ¼ lightly packed up coarsely chopped cilantro stems or 1 teaspoon coriander seed

Instructions

  • Cook the stock: Put all the ingredients in a 6-quart Instant Pot-style multicooker or pressure cooker. Add 6 ½ cups (1.5L) water. Lock the lid in place. Bring to high pressure, adjust the heat to maintain pressure (if needed), and cook for 45 minutes. Unplug the Instant Pot or remove the cooker from the heat, depressurize naturally for 20 to 25 minutes, and then manually release the residual pressure.
  • Strain and use: Unlock the cooker and wait for any bubbling to subside, about 5 minutes. Using a metal ladle, remove most of the fat on top, leaving some for flavor. (Or, remove the fat after straining and chilling the stock, when it's easier.) Using a slotted spoon, remove the large solids. Position a fine mesh strainer over a medium or large saucepan (line the strainer with muslin for super-clear stock and easy straining) and ladle or pour the stock through. Use the stock immediately, or transfer to airtight containers and refrigerate for up to 3 days or freeze for up to 3 months.

Vegan Pumpkin Whoopie Pies

These vegan pumpkin whoopie pies are the fall treat you absolutely need in your life! Made with two soft pumpkin cakes sandwiched with luscious vegan cream cheese filling, every bite is like an explosion of pumpkin season in your mouth. Course Dessert Cuisine American Prep Time 30 minutes minutes Cook Time 15 minutes minutes Total Time 45 minutes minutes Servings 16 Calories 468kcal Author Alissa Saenz

Ingredients

For the Cakes

  • 2 ¾ cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1 tablespoon pumpkin pie spice
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • ¾ teaspoon salt
  • 1 (15 ounce/425 gram) can pumpkin puree
  • 1 cup organic granulated sugar
  • ⅔ cup organic brown sugar
  • ⅔ cup vegetable oil
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract

For the Filling

  • 1 (8 ounce/227 gram) package Tofutti vegan cream cheese, chilled (Note 1)
  • ½ cup vegan butter, room temperature
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Pinch salt
  • 4 ½ cups organic powdered sugar, plus up to 1 additional cup, if needed
US Customary - Metric

Instructions

  • Preheat the oven to 350°F and line a couple of baking sheets with parchment paper.
  • Whisk the flour, baking powder, baking soda, pumpkin pie spice, cinnamon, and salt together in a large mixing bowl.
  • In a separate bowl, stir together the pumpkin puree, sugar, brown sugar, oil, and vanilla.
  • Add the pumpkin mixture to the bowl with the flour mixture, then stir just until combined. Don't overmix — a few lumps are fine. The batter will be very thick and fluffy.
  • Drop the batter by 2 tablespoon rounds (a cookie scoop works great here) on the prepared baking sheets, spacing them at least 3 inches apart. You can bake the cakes in 2 batches if you don't get through all the batter on the first one.
  • Bake the cakes until they've spread out and puffed up, and the tops feel set, about 14 to 16 minutes. Lightly touch the top of one with your finger to test for doneness; it should spring back.
  • Place the baking sheets on cooling racks and let the cakes cool for about 5 minutes, then remove them from the sheets and transfer them directly to the racks to finish cooling.
  • Make the filling while the cakes cool. First, beat the vegan cream cheese, butter, vanilla, and salt together at high speed with an electric mixer. Begin beating in the powdered sugar at low speed, adding about a cup at a time, until the mixture is thick enough for stuffing the cakes.
  • Once the cakes are cool, spread or pipe about 2 tablespoons of the filling onto the flat sides of half the cakes, then close each whoopie pie with a second cake to create a sandwich.
  • Serve immediately or chill in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days.

Notes

  1. Tofutti gives me the most reliable and consistent results for frostings and fillings. Other brands will work, but the amount of powdered sugar needed will vary.
