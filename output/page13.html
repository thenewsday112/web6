This easy and fuss-free dessert comes together with minimal effort. The hardest part is just waiting for the bars to set. When it’s finished, you’ll have a beautifully layered treat to share with your friends and family.

Why You’ll Love This Recipe

  • Easy no-bake filling. Coconut oil helps everything set in the fridge, so you won’t need a water bath or worry about your cake cracking. 
  • Two beautiful layers, one base recipe. Make a single cheesecake filling, divide it in half, and stir in pumpkin and spice to make 2 distinct layers. 
  • Beautiful presentation. After the bars set, you get 3 distinct layers when you slice them that will wow your guests. 
  • Perfect make-ahead dessert. Since these bars need at least 8 hours in the fridge, they’re perfect to make ahead of a holiday or party. 
  • Real pumpkin flavor. Just half a cup of purée is enough to taste and see while still keeping the filling light. 

Ingredients You’ll Need

For the graham cracker crust:

  • Vegan graham cracker sheets. Since most graham crackers contain honey, check the labels carefully. We like to use the classic Nabisco graham crackers in the red box, or the original Kroger brand in the blue box.  Since brands vary in size, we recommend weighing your graham crackers when measuring. 
  • Vegan butter. We like to use stick-style butter, melted, instead of dairy-free butter from a tub. We like the Country Crock plant butter sticks. 
  • Sugar and Salt. Both balance the crust’s flavor and sweetness level. 

For the cheesecake filling:

  • Vegan cream cheese. Softened to room temperature. Since this is the core of the recipe, choose your favorite brand. We like to use Violife, Tofutti, or the Philadelphia Dairy-free. 
  • Granulated sugar. To sweeten the filling. Opt for organic if you want to ensure it’s 100% vegan-friendly. 
  • Non-dairy milk. Make sure it’s room temperature so the coconut oil doesn’t start to solidify too soon. We like to use either soy milk or almond milk. 
  • Lemon juice. Helps add that signature cheesecake tang. Use fresh-squeezed lemon juice for best results. 
  • Vanilla extract. Enhances the flavor of both cheesecake layers. 
  • Salt. Balances the sweetness.
  • Coconut oil. To avoid an overly coconut-flavored filling, we recommend using refined coconut oil for this recipe. 
  • Pumpkin purée. Use pure pumpkin rather than pumpkin pie filling, which is already sweetened and spiced.
  • Pumpkin pie spice. A blend of your favorite fall spices. Think cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, allspice, and clove.

Equipment Needed

  • Food processor
  • 8×8 inch baking dish
  • Parchment paper
  • Electric hand mixer or stand mixer
  • Large mixing bowl
  • Offset spatula
  • Kitchen scale
  • Rubber spatula

Tips for the Best Results

  • Cool the crust completely. Let it sit for at least 30 minutes. If you’re in a rush, you can also pop it in the fridge to cool. 
  • Bring your ingredients to room temperature. Keeping your cream cheese and milk at room temperature will prevent a lumpy filling or seizing the coconut oil. 
  • Cool the coconut oil. If you add melted coconut oil while it’s too hot, it can curdle the cream cheese, so make sure it has cooled to room temperature before mixing. 
  • Do not skip the freezer step between layers. A quick freeze firms up the vanilla layer just enough that the pumpkin layer sits on top instead of swirling into it.
  • Chill the full 8 hours. This helps the bars set properly. Slicing early gives you soft layers that will not hold their shape.

Serving Suggestions

These Pumpkin Cheesecake Bars are best served cold, directly from the fridge. You can also top them with a dollop of our 2-Ingredient Vegan Whipped Cream and a dusting of cinnamon, but they’re great on their own. Enjoy with a tall glass of soy or almond milk, coffee, tea, or chai latte. 

Storage Instructions

  • Refrigerator: Store the bars in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. Keep them cold, since the coconut oil softens at room temperature and causes the bars to lose their structure.
  • Freezer: These freeze beautifully. Slice them first, then freeze the bars in a single layer on a parchment-lined tray. Once solid, transfer to a freezer-safe container, with parchment between layers, and store for up to 2 months.
  • Thawing: Move frozen bars to the fridge for a few hours, or let them sit at room temperature for about 15 minutes. Some prefer them partially frozen, like ice cream bars, which is another great way to enjoy them. 

Recipe FAQs

Can I use a different pan size?

An 8×8 inch pan gives you nice thick bars. A 9×9 will work, but the layers will be thinner. We do not recommend a 9×13, since the layers become too thin to hold up.

My filling looks grainy or has little white flecks. What happened?

That is coconut oil seizing from contact with something cold. Usually, it’s cold milk or cold cream cheese. Everything should be at room temperature before mixing.

Can I substitute the coconut oil?

Not with a liquid oil, since the coconut oil is what makes the bars firm up in the fridge. Vegan butter can work as a substitute, though the set will be a little softer. Please note that we haven’t tested this option. 

Can I make this gluten-free?

Yes. Swap in gluten-free graham crackers or a similar crisp cookie and use the same 155g weight. Everything else in the recipe is already gluten-free.

Do I need a food processor for the crust?

It is the easiest way, but if you don’t have one, seal the graham crackers in a zip-top bag and crush them into crumbs with a rolling pin, then stir in the butter, sugar, and salt by hand.

Pumpkin Cheesecake Bars

Description

These Pumpkin Cheesecake Bars are the perfect fall sweet treat. They’re layered with a golden graham cracker crust, a tangy dairy-free cheesecake, and a delicately spiced pumpkin cheesecake on top. This easy and fuss-free dessert comes together with minimal effort. The hardest part is just waiting for the bars to set. When it’s finished, you’ll have a beautifully layered treat to share with your friends and family. 

Ingredients

For the graham cracker crust

  • 10 (155g) vegan graham cracker sheets
  • 4 tablespoons vegan butter, melted
  • 1 teaspoon granulated sugar
  • Pinch of salt

For the cheesecake filling

  • 16 ounces vegan cream cheese, softened
  • 1/2 cup (100g) granulated sugar
  • 1/3 cup non-dairy milk, room temperature
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/3 cup plus 2 tablespoons coconut oil, melted and cooled, divided
  • 1/2 cup pumpkin purée
  • 1 teaspoon pumpkin pie spice

Instructions

Make the graham cracker crust

  1. Preheat the oven. Preheat the oven to 350ºF.
  2. Process the crumbs. In a food processor, process the graham cracker sheets until broken down into fine crumbs. Add the melted vegan butter, sugar, and salt, then pulse until combined. The mixture should resemble wet sand.
  3. Press into the pan. Line an 8×8 inch baking dish with parchment paper. Pour the graham cracker mixture into the dish and press evenly into the bottom.
  4. Bake and cool. Bake for 12 to 15 minutes, until the crust is golden brown and smells toasted. Remove from the oven and let cool for at least 30 minutes before continuing.

Make the cheesecake filling

  1. Whip the base. Add the softened vegan cream cheese and sugar to a large bowl. Using an electric hand mixer or stand mixer, beat until well combined and fluffy, about 2-3 minutes.
  2. Add the wet ingredients. Add the non-dairy milk, lemon juice, vanilla extract, salt, and 1/3 cup of the melted coconut oil. Mix until well combined and smooth.
  3. Check the pan temperature. Make sure the baking dish is not too hot, or the filling will melt. If needed, refrigerate it for a few minutes to cool.
  4. Add the first layer. Once the baking dish cools, spread half the filling over the graham cracker crust. Use an offset spatula to smooth it into an even layer.
  5. Set briefly. Place the baking dish in the freezer for about 10 minutes to firm up the cheesecake layer.
  6. Make the pumpkin layer. Add the remaining 2 tablespoons of coconut oil, the pumpkin purée, and the pumpkin pie spice to the remaining filling.
  7. Beat until smooth. Use your hand mixer to beat together until well combined and smooth.
  8. Add the second layer. Spoon the pumpkin filling over the vanilla cheesecake layer and smooth it into an even layer.
  9. Chill. Cover and refrigerate for at least 8 hours, or until set.
  10. Slice and serve. Once fully set, lift the bars out using the parchment overhang, slice, and enjoy.

Notes

  • Room temperature matters: Cold cream cheese or cold milk will cause the melted coconut oil to seize into flecks. Set everything out about an hour ahead.
  • Cool the crust fully: A warm pan will melt the coconut oil out of the filling, and the bars will not set properly.
  • Clean slices: Wipe your knife between cuts with a warm, damp cloth for the sharpest edges.
  • Storage: Refrigerate for up to 5 days, or freeze sliced bars for up to 2 months.

How To Make Reese’s Pieces Peanut Butter Bars

(More detailed description and video in recipe card below.)

  1. In a saucepan, melt the butter and brown sugar.
  2. Stir in the peanut butter and let cool.
  3. Whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.

Type Of Peanut Butter To Use

When baking, I find it is best to use traditional peanut butter that does not need to be stirred like Jif or Skippy.

Recipe Tip

Be sure to measure the flour correctly or your bars may be dried. First fluff the flour up some in the bag or container. It tends to get packed down over time. Then gently scoop it with a large spoon into a dry measuring cup. Overfill it slightly and then run the back of a knife across the top to get rid of excess.

Reese’s Pieces Peanut Butter Bars

Reese’s Pieces Peanut Butter Bars have so much peanut butter flavor and a thick layer of rich chocolate frosting. Reese’s Pieces candies are sprinkled on top and added to the batter for lots of crunchy candy flavor.  Course Dessert Prep Time 20 minutes minutes Cook Time 30 minutes minutes Total Time 50 minutes minutes Servings 16 servings Calories 678kcal

Equipment

  • 9×13-inch baking dish

Ingredients

  • ¾ cup butter
  • 3 cups packed light brown sugar
  • ¾ cup creamy peanut butter
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 ½ teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 3 ¾ cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 ½ teaspoons baking powder
  • ½ teaspoon baking soda
  • ¾ teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup Reese’s Pieces candies

Frosting

  • 6 tablespoons butter, cut into pieces
  • 6 tablespoons milk
  • 1 ½ cups granulated sugar
  • 1 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips
  • ¼ teaspoon vanilla extract
  • ¼ cup Reese’s Pieces

Instructions

  • Preheat oven to 350 degrees and grease a 9×13-inch baking pan.
  • In a saucepan over medium heat, melt butter and brown sugar. Stir until smooth.
  • Take saucepan off heat, stir in peanut butter until smooth. Let cool.
  • In a large bowl, whisk together flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt. Set aside.
  • Once peanut butter mixture has cooled, whisk in eggs one at a time. Stir in vanilla. 
  • Add peanut butter mixture to dry ingredients, and stir until blended. 
  • Stir in Reese's Pieces and transfer batter to prepared pan, spreading it evenly. Bake for 25 to 30 minutes or until set in middle and golden brown around edges.
  • Make frosting. Combine butter, milk, and sugar in a saucepan and bring to a boil. Cook at a rolling boil for 30 seconds.
  • Remove from heat and stir in chocolate chips and vanilla until smooth. Pour over warm bars.
  • Sprinkle Reese's Pieces on top. Let cool completely before cutting into squares.

Bars can be stored in an airtight container at room temperature for 4 days.

Taco Bell’s Nacho Fries just got closer to home. These are crispy, seasoned fries paired with a perfectly warm, creamy nacho cheese for dipping. These are the type of fries you have a hard time stopping eating. The fries are tossed in a simple homemade seasoning blend with smoky paprika, cumin, garlic, a little heat, and just enough sweetness to give them that fast-food flavor. The cheese is made with deli-style American cheese, evaporated milk, and pickled jalapeños, so it melts into a smooth, glossy sauce with a little tang. Best of all, you start with a bag of frozen fries and put all of the effort into the seasoning and nacho cheese that really elevate the fries. They are perfect for taco night, burger night, movie nights, and game days.

Better Than the Drive-Thru

I’m guilty of Taco Bell being one of my favorite fast food joints. The Nacho Fries on the menu are good, but I was determined to make them GREAT. I wanted a version at home that quenched that fast food craving, but with elevated flavor and texture. All while using a handy bag of frozen fries. These come together in about 25 minutes and make 4 servings, so they are easy to pull off for a group whenever the craving hits.

The biggest thing I wanted to get right was the seasoning. Taco Bell’s Nacho Fries don’t taste like they’re covered in traditional taco seasoning. There’s a very specific combination of smoky, savory, salty, slightly sweet, and spicy flavors that makes them taste like the real thing. That is why this blend uses paprika, cumin, garlic, and onion for depth, chili powder and black pepper for a little heat, and a pinch of sugar for that subtle sweetness you notice in the original. It’s not simply spicy. It has layers.

American cheese is the key to getting that smooth, creamy nacho sauce texture. Evaporated milk keeps it silky and stable, while pickled jalapeños and a little jalapeño brine add the tangy heat that makes the cheese taste more like a true nacho sauce instead of plain melted cheese.

The goal was never to make a complicated copycat recipe. It’s about getting the flavor right while keeping the whole thing easy enough that you can make it any night of the week. The fries should come out deeply golden and crisp with a generous coating of smoky, savory seasoning, and the cheese should be smooth and creamy with a little tang and just enough jalapeño heat. Together, they hit that salty, spicy, cheesy combination that makes the original so good.

Do I Have to Deep Fry the Fries?

Not at all. I fry the frozen fries in oil at 350°F because that gives me the crunchiest, most fast-food-style result, but this recipe is designed around a shortcut, so you can absolutely skip the pot of oil.

Choose your favorite frozen fries and cook them in the oven or air fryer according to the package directions. An air fryer is especially great for getting them extra crispy. Whichever method you use, cook them until they are deeply golden, not just pale and heated through. A crispier fry holds onto the seasoning better and stands up to the cheese, while a soft fry gets soggy the second it meets the sauce.

I use Ore-Ida Crispy Fast Food Fries because the thin, crispy cut is the closest match to the real thing, but any thin-cut frozen fry works well here.

Tips for the Best Nacho Fries

  • Season the fries the second they come out. The seasoning sticks much better immediately after the fries come out of the oil, oven, or air fryer. While the fries are still screaming hot, toss them in a large bowl with the seasoning. Once they cool, the seasoning just falls off and ends up at the bottom of the bowl instead of on the fries.
  • Don’t be shy with the seasoning. The fries should have a generous coating so you get that flavor in every bite. If your fries look lightly dusted, keep sprinkling and tossing until every fry is coated.
  • Keep the cheese over low heat the whole time. Don’t let the cheese sauce boil. That’s how you get a smooth, glossy nacho sauce instead of a broken cheese sauce. High heat causes the sauce to separate and lose that smooth texture, so keep the burner low and stir constantly while it melts.

I’ve been wanting to make Rasta Pasta for a while because I’m a BIG fan of bold, spicy flavors. This Jamaican-inspired dish brings all that flavor to the table with tender jerk-seasoned chicken, sweet bell peppers, and penne coated in a creamy Parmesan sauce. The peppers and creamy sauce help balance the warmth of the jerk seasoning, and everything cooks in one skillet for less cleanup! It’s colorful, hearty, genuinely delicious, and ready in 45 minutes. Better yet, at just $2.80 per serving, it’s a fun way to switch up pasta night without spending a fortune!

A Spiced Chicken and Peppers Pasta

If you’ve never heard of Rasta pasta, here’s a little background! It’s believed to have originated in Negril, Jamaica, in 1985, when chef Lorraine Washington served homemade fettuccine with tomato sauce and ackee (Jamaica’s national fruit) to a group of construction workers. The red, gold (yellow), and green colors associated with the Rastafari movement inspired one of the workers to name it Rasta pasta. And you’ll spot those same colors in the three bell peppers I use in my recipe, too! There are plenty of versions of this dish today, but the one I’m most familiar with is this creamy, jerk-seasoned pasta. My recipe is a simple, budget-friendly take on this dish, rather than a recreation of the original.

For my version, I’ve kept things easy on both the cook AND the grocery budget! I sear the jerk-seasoned chicken first, then cook the colorful peppers in the same skillet so none of those delicious browned bits go to waste. I also skip boiling the penne separately and simmer it right in the chicken broth, where it absorbs all that savory seasoning and releases starch to help thicken the sauce. Half-and-half keeps things creamy without the extra cost of heavy cream, and I stir in Parmesan off the heat for a smooth finish. It’s a similar approach to our creamy Cajun chicken pasta, but with the bold flavor of jerk seasoning and plenty of colorful peppers.

Recipe Success Tips

  1. Cut the chicken into thin cutlets. I slice each chicken breast horizontally in half before seasoning. This gives me four thinner pieces that cook more evenly and quickly, without the outside drying out before the center is done. Aim for 3-4 minutes per side, but always check the thickest part reaches 165°F with a meat thermometer. And here’s a little budget win: I only need 1 lb. of chicken for four hearty servings, thanks to all those peppers and pasta!
  2. Adjust the jerk seasoning to your spice tolerance. Jerk blends can vary a LOT in heat and saltiness. For a milder pasta, I’d choose a mild jerk blend rather than leaving out most of the seasoning. That way, you still get those lovely aromatic flavors from spices like allspice and thyme. The broth and Parmesan add salt, too, so give the finished sauce a taste before adding any more.
  3. Keep an eye on the pasta and the liquid. Unlike some of our other one-pot pasta recipes, I simmer this one uncovered. This lets some of the broth evaporate as the penne releases starch, creating a flavorful, slightly thickened base for our creamy sauce. Keep the broth at a steady simmer and stir often. The penne should still have a little firmness in the center, with most of the broth absorbed. It’ll finish cooking once you add the half-and-half. If the pan looks dry before the pasta is ready, add a splash of hot broth or water. Still looking too soupy? Give it another minute or two to reduce before adding the dairy. Different pans and stovetops can make a difference here!
  4. Be gentle with the creamy sauce. Half-and-half is my budget-friendly alternative to heavy cream, but its lower fat content makes it more likely to separate if you boil it too hard. Once you add it, keep the heat low and let the sauce thicken around the pasta. Then turn off the heat before stirring in the Parmesan to help prevent clumping. I also save the fresh lime for serving rather than adding it directly to the hot dairy sauce. Everyone can add a squeeze to their own bowl, and there’s less risk of the acidity making the sauce grainy.

Meal Prep it!

I’m all for getting an extra lunch out of dinner, and this Rasta pasta is PERFECT for meal prepping! Whenever I’m serving veggies on the side, I’ll usually make a little extra and pack them alongside the pasta (like the broccoli and green beans shown here). I like using divided meal prep containers to keep the veggies from sitting in the creamy sauce. Then, once the pasta is cool, I’ll just pop the lid on and store everything in the fridge for up to 4 days. The pasta does soak up some of the sauce as it sits, so I add a little splash of water or broth when reheating in the microwave to bring back its saucy consistency.

If you know you’ll be reheating the pasta throughout the week, cook it just until tender rather than letting it get overly soft in the pot.

This vegan brown butter pasta is tossed in a rich, nutty dairy-free sauce with crunchy pecans and tender broccolini. It’s super easy to throw together with minimal prep, but oh-so-comforting and satisfying!

My favorite type of recipe is this: feels luxuriously delicious, tastes like it came from a fancy restaurant, but is stupidly simple to throw together. And I noticed something recently — those kinds of recipes often involve pasta. You'd never guess how easy dishes like my cherry tomato pasta, vegan cacio e pepe, and truffle marinara pasta are. They all scream "fancy!"

Vegan brown butter pasta is a new one for me. This dish barely requires a cutting board, but the flavor, textures, and overall vibe make it feel like a seriously special dinner.

[feast_advanced_jump_to]

Vegan brown butter was the challenge in creating this one. Vegan butter doesn't contain the proteins that help conventional butter brown, so we need to improvise. That's where pecans come in — they infuse the melted vegan butter with nutty flavor, while a touch of miso paste adds savory notes, making it taste like brown butter (even if you're not actually browning it!).

I've paired my brown butter pasta with earthy fried sage, and I threw the pecans I used to create the brown butter right in with everything. A little seared broccolini balances out the richness, and you'll end up with a seriously comforting meal that might just turn into a weeknight staple in your home.

Ingredients You'll Need

Below you'll find a list of ingredients in this recipe, with notes and substitutions. Scroll all the way to the bottom of the post to see the full recipe, including the amount of each ingredient.

  • Broccolini. This is a cross between conventional and Chinese broccoli. It looks like broccoli with long, thin stems and is a touch sweeter than traditional broccoli. If your store doesn't carry it, try regular broccoli, kale, or asparagus as an alternative.
  • Ziti pasta. You can absolutely swap this out with another pasta shape.
  • Chopped pecans.
  • Vegan butter. Most grocery stores carry this in the refrigerated section, near the regular butter.
  • Fresh sage.
  • White miso. Look for this in the refrigerated section or international aisle of the grocery store. Another variety of miso, like red or yellow, can be substituted, though the flavor will vary a bit.
  • Olive oil.
  • Salt and pepper.
  • Vegan Parmesan cheese. This is optional, but a delicious addition. Use store-bought or try my homemade vegan Parmesan cheese.

How It's Made

Below is a detailed photo tutorial on how to make this dish. Scroll all the way down if you'd like to skip right to the recipe!

Steps 1 & 2: Blanch the broccolini and cook the pasta. You'll be using the same pot of salted boiling water for both steps. First, blanch the broccolini for about a minute, take it out of the pot with tongs, then boil the pasta. Be sure to reserve about ½ cup of the pasta cooking water before you drain it.

Step 3: Toast the nuts. Heat up a large pot, then add chopped pecans in an even layer. Toast them briefly, until they darken slightly and smell deliciously nutty. Remove them from the pot.

Step 4: Fry the sage. Melt your vegan butter in the pot, then add your sage leaves. Fry them for about 2 minutes, until they become crispy and darken a bit. Be careful not to let them burn. Remove them from the pot, but leave the butter.

Tip: This recipe requires a lot of moving things in and out of the same pot. Have some plates and a small bowl set out in advance for when you need to remove ingredients.

Step 5: Brown the butter. You're not actually browning it. You're infusing it. Remove the pot from the heat, let the butter stop sizzling, then add the nuts back to the pot. Let them sit for 5 minutes. Next, add even more flavor — combine some of your pasta water with miso paste, then stir it in. Pour the mixture into a bowl.

Step 6: Sear the broccolini. Return the pot to heat, add a bit of olive oil, then add your blanched broccolini stalks. Cook them briefly over medium-high heat, until they just start to brown. Sprinkle them with some salt and pepper, then remove them from the pot.

Tip: Don't expect your butter to actually turn brown. This recipe goes for the flavor of brown butter, by infusing it with pecans and miso, but we're not actually altering the color.

Step 7: Finish the pasta. Return the pasta to the pot, along with your brown butter pecan mixture, and heat everything up briefly. Add a bit of your pasta cooking water if it seems dry.

Steps 8 & 9: Finish and serve. Give the pasta a sprinkle with salt and pepper, then divide it into portions and top each one with fried sage leaves, broccolini, and, optionally, some vegan Parm.

Tip: Want to use my vegan brown butter for another recipe? Do it! You can skip the sage for most recipes. Just infuse the nuts and add the miso.

Variations

  • Brown butter gnocchi. Replace the pasta with a pound of shelf-stable gnocchi.
  • Veggie swaps. Try Brussels sprouts, broccoli, asparagus, green beans, kale, or spinach.
  • Add protein. Baked tofu, sautéed seitan, or vegan sausage would round out this meal beautifully.
  • Swap the nuts. Walnuts or hazelnuts would be great choices.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can this recipe be made gluten-free?

Yep! Use your favorite gluten-free pasta and make sure your miso paste is certified gluten-free.

Can this recipe be made nut-free?

Possibly, but I haven't tried. You could try substituting sunflower seeds or pepitas for the nuts. The flavor will be different, and I'm honestly not sure how close it will be to brown butter, but I'm pretty sure it will at least be tasty!

Can I use dried sage?

Unfortunately, no. You'll need whole, fresh sage leaves for frying.

How should I store my leftovers?

Leftover vegan brown butter pasta will keep in an airtight container in the fridge for 3 to 4 days. Add a splash of water when you reheat it if it seems dry.

Vegan Brown Butter Pasta with Fried Sage & Pecans

This vegan brown butter pasta is tossed in a rich, nutty dairy-free sauce with crunchy pecans and tender broccolini. It’s super easy to throw together with minimal prep, but oh-so-comforting and satisfying! Course Entrees, Main Cuisine American Prep Time 10 minutes minutes Cook Time 20 minutes minutes Total Time 30 minutes minutes Servings 4 Calories 615kcal Author Alissa Saenz

Ingredients

  • 12 ounces broccolini, stems trimmed (Note 1)
  • 12 ounces dried ziti pasta (or pasta of choice)
  • ½ cup chopped pecans
  • ½ cup vegan butter
  • 25 fresh sage leaves (about 1 large bunch)
  • 1 teaspoon white miso paste
  • 1 teaspoon olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Vegan Parmesan cheese, for serving (optional)
US Customary - Metric

Instructions

  • Place a large pot of salted water over high heat. Once the water comes to a boil, add the broccolini. Boil the broccolini for about 1 minute, until the pieces become slightly tender and bright green. Remove the broccolini from the pot using tongs, and transfer the pieces to a plate. Set them aside for now.
  • Add the pasta to the water and cook it according to the package directions, until al dente. Reserve about ½ cup of the pasta cooking water, then drain the pasta into a colander when done.
  • While the pasta cooks, place a large, wide-bottomed pot over medium heat. Give the pot a minute to heat up, then add the pecans. Toast the pecans until they begin to darken and smell nutty, stirring occasionally, about 2 minutes. Remove the nuts from the pot and transfer them to a plate or bowl.
  • Add the vegan butter to the pot. Melt the butter completely, then add the sage leaves, arranging them in an even layer. Let the leaves sizzle in the butter for about 2 minutes, until they darken and crisp up. Remove the sage leaves from the pot, leaving the melted butter. Arrange the leaves on a plate.
  • Remove the pot from heat. Once the butter stops sizzling, stir in the pecans. Let the pecans sit in the hot melted butter for about 5 minutes to infuse it with their flavor. In the meantime, whisk 1 tablespoon of your pasta water together with the miso paste. After the nuts have infused the butter for 5 minutes, stir the miso mixture into the melted butter. Pour the nuts and butter into a bowl.
  • Place the pot over medium-high heat, then add the olive oil. Give the olive oil a minute to heat up, then add the broccolini, arranging the pieces in an even layer. Sear the broccolini for 1 to 2 minutes on each side, until lightly browned. Sprinkle the pieces with salt and pepper, then remove them from the pot and transfer them to a plate.
  • Add the pasta to the pot, then pour the pecans and butter over the pasta. Toss everything to combine and coat the pasta in the butter, and just cook everything until heated through. Add a splash of your reserved pasta water if the pasta seems dry.
  • Remove the pot from heat and season the pasta with salt and pepper to taste.
  • To serve, divide the pasta among plates or bowls, topping each portion with the fried sage leaves, broccolini, and optionally some vegan Parmesan cheese. Enjoy!

Notes

  1. Trim off only the tough, woody ends of the stems. The tender stems are edible.
