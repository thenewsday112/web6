Why You’ll Love This Recipe

  • Approachable for everyone. If you can mix and knead dough, you can make these! They’re easy to make at any baking skill level.
  • Perfectly savory. The herby cream cheese center really hits the spot. 
  • Impressive presentation. While these olive chive cream cheese rolls aren’t a hassle to make, they look like they’re fresh from a bakery or cafe. 
  • The options are endless. Use your favorite olives, add your favorite herbs, or switch up the flavor with roasted garlic or sun-dried tomatoes. The possibilities are endless!
  • Feeds a crowd. One recipe makes eight large rolls, making them perfect for brunch or a dinner party. 

Ingredients You’ll Need

For the dough:

  • Vegan butter – We like to use the country-crock plant butter sticks for this recipe rather than a dairy-free buttery spread from a tub. 
  • Non-dairy milk – Lukewarm milk helps activate the yeast and leaven the dough. We like to use soy milk or almond milk, but any unsweetened plant milk will do.
  • Granulated sugar – This feeds and activates the yeast. Use organic sugar if you’d like to ensure it’s not processed through bone char. 
  • Active dry yeast – Helps the dough rise and stay nice and fluffy. Always use yeast that isn’t expired. Expired yeast will not activate, and your dough may not rise. 
  • All-purpose flour – For the most accurate results, we recommend measuring your flour in grams instead of cups, so if you have a food scale at home, now is the time to use it. 
  • Salt – Helps balance the flavor in the savory dough, 
  • Vegan parmesan – Folds into the dough to add a unique savory flavor to the rolls. We like to freshly grate the Violife Foods parmesan wedge rather than using a pre-grated vegan parm. 

For the cream cheese filling:

  • Vegan cream cheese – Make sure it softens up at room temperature before making the filling. This will help ensure it’s smooth and lump-free. We like to use the Violife Supreme Cream Cheese block or the dairy-free Philadelphia cream cheese. 
  • Cornstarch – Helps firm up the filling so the cream cheese doesn’t run and the rolls stay intact. 
  • Vegan parmesan – Adds a subtle, salty, nutty depth to the filling. 
  • Fresh chives – Brings a bright onion flavor to the filling. We recommend fresh chives over dried. 
  • Black pepper – Adds the perfect peppery hint to the filling. We recommend freshly cracked pepper for the best flavor. 

For assembly:

  • Non-dairy milk and olive oil – Whisk these two together and brush over the unbaked dough so it gets perfectly golden in the oven.
  • Olives – Grab a 10 oz jar of your favorite variety. Castelvetrano are buttery and mild, Kalamata are punchy and briny, and an olive medley gives you a bit of both. Make sure the pits are removed. 
  • Chopped chives – For garnishing the finished rolls.

Tips for the Best Results

  • Check the milk temperature. Use a thermometer to make sure your milk is between 105-110ºF; if it’s too hot, it will kill the yeast and the dough won’t rise. 
  • Don’t skimp on the kneading. Knead the dough for 10 minutes to fully develop it. You’ll know it’s ready when it’s smooth and bounces back slightly when you poke it.
  • Do not overfill the rolls. Fill each with about 2 tablespoons. Adding more than that may cause the filling to spill out while baking.
  • Dry your olives. Remove excess brine to keep the filling from loosening too much. 
  • Allow them to cool fully. Straight out of the oven, the filling will be scorching hot. Let them rest for about 15 minutes to help the rolls set properly. 

Serving Suggestions

These are great with brunch or dinner. They pair perfectly with a big bowl of soup or alongside our Brussels Sprout Caesar Salad. You can also serve them with our Tofu Scramble for a hearty brunch with friends. These Olive Chive Cream Cheese Rolls are also perfect on a holiday appetizer table. Of course, you can also eat them straight from the tray for any anytime snack. 

Storage Instructions

  • Refrigerator: Keep cooled rolls in an airtight container for up to 4 days. Because the filling contains cream cheese, we don’t recommend leaving these out at room temperature. 
  • Freezer: Freeze baked and fully cooled rolls in a single layer, then transfer to a freezer-safe bag for up to 2 months.
  • Reheating: Reheat in a 325ºF oven for 8 to 10 minutes, or until warmed through. From frozen, thaw overnight in the fridge first. We do not recommend the microwave, since it can make the dough gummy. 

Recipe FAQs

Can I make the dough ahead of time?

Yes, you can! Once the dough rises the first time, cover it tightly and refrigerate overnight. Allow it to come back to room temperature for about an hour before dividing and shaping into balls.

Why didn’t my dough rise?

More often than not, the problem is expired yeast or milk that was too hot. After 10 minutes of activating, your yeast should look foamy. If it doesn’t, repeat the process instead of continuing with the recipe.

Can I use a stand mixer instead of kneading by hand?

You can! Use the dough hook on medium-low for about 6 to 8 minutes, until the dough is smooth and pulls away from the sides of the bowl.

What if I do not like olives?

Swap them for halved cherry tomatoes, sun-dried tomatoes, roasted red pepper strips, or caramelized onions. The cream cheese base works with all of them.

Can I make these gluten-free?


We have not tested a gluten-free version, and enriched yeast doughs are tricky to convert. If you experiment with a one-to-one gluten-free bread flour blend, let us know how it goes.

Description

These Olive Chive Cream Cheese Rolls deserve a spot at the top of your baking list. Perfectly soft with an herbaceous cream cheese center and topped with green olives, these rolls are basically a mix between a dinner roll and a savory danish. They come together simply and even an entry-level baker can whip up a batch effortlessly. We hope you love them as much as we do!

Ingredients

For the dough

  • 6 tablespoons melted vegan butter
  • 1 cup non-dairy milk, lukewarm
  • 3 tablespoons granulated sugar
  • 2 1/4 teaspoons active dry yeast (1 standard packet)
  • 3 1/2 cups (420g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup vegan parmesan

For the cream cheese filling

  • 8 ounces vegan cream cheese, softened
  • 1 tablespoon cornstarch
  • 1/4 cup vegan parmesan
  • 3 tablespoons chopped chives
  • Black pepper, to taste

For assembly

  • 2 tablespoons non-dairy milk
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 (10-ounce) jar olives of choice, pitted
  • Chopped chives, for topping

Instructions

  1. Activate the yeast. In a large bowl, add the melted vegan butter, lukewarm non-dairy milk, and sugar. Stir until dissolved, then add the yeast. Set aside for 8-10 minutes to allow the yeast to activate. It will appear foamy when it’s ready.
  2. Form the dough. In the same bowl, add the flour, salt, and vegan parmesan, and mix until uniform and a rough dough forms.
  3. Knead. Transfer the dough to a floured work surface. Knead for 10 minutes, adding more flour as necessary. The dough should be smooth and bounce back slightly when you poke it.
  4. First rise. Place the dough in an oiled bowl, toss to coat, and cover with a damp towel or plastic wrap. Set aside in a warm place to rise for about 1 hour, or until doubled.
  5. Make the filling. Meanwhile, add all the filling ingredients to a medium bowl and mix until well combined. Chill until ready to use.
  6. Divide and shape. Once the dough has doubled, transfer it to a clean work surface. Divide into 8 equal portions and shape each into a smooth ball.
  7. Second rise. Place the dough balls onto a parchment-lined baking sheet, spread evenly apart. Cover with a damp towel or plastic wrap and prove for 45 minutes.
  8. Preheat. Preheat the oven to 350ºF.
  9. Fill the rolls. Use a glass or flat-bottomed measuring cup to press down the center of each dough ball, creating a well with a border about 1/2 inch wide. Use a spoon or piping bag to add about 2 tablespoons of the cream cheese mixture to each roll. Be careful not to overfill, or the cream cheese will overflow.
  10. Add the olives. Press 2-3 olives into the cream cheese center of each roll.
  11. Brush with the wash. Mix together the non-dairy milk and olive oil in a small bowl, then brush onto the exposed dough of each roll.
  12. Bake. Bake for 30-35 minutes, or until golden brown. Allow to cool for at least 15 minutes.
  13. Finish and serve. Top with additional fresh chives and black pepper, then enjoy.

Notes

  • Milk temperature: Lukewarm means about 105 to 110ºF. If it feels hot to the touch, let it cool before adding the yeast.
  • Olive prep: Pat olives dry before pressing them in. Excess brine will loosen the filling.
  • Make ahead: After the first rise, refrigerate the dough overnight. Bring to room temperature for about an hour before shaping.
  • Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days or freeze up to 2 months. Reheat at 325ºF for 8 to 10 minutes.

🌱 Substitutions

This recipe is versatile and easy to adapt. Below are some simple ingredient substitutions.

  • Blueberries: Use wild or regular frozen blueberries. I prefer organic when available to help limit exposure to certain pesticide residues. Organic wild blueberries can be difficult to find, so I used regular organic frozen blueberries here. Conventional blueberries are still a nutritious option-use what fits your budget.
  • Water: Substitute unsweetened nondairy milk for extra creaminess. Soy milk also adds protein.
  • Silken tofu: Use plain, unsweetened Greek yogurt or soy yogurt for a creamy texture and extra protein. Dairy proteins may bind to cocoa flavanols, potentially affecting their absorption, although human studies have shown mixed results. That's why I prefer silken tofu or soy yogurt for this smoothie.
  • Pumpkin seeds: Substitute hemp hearts for a similarly creamy texture and added protein.
  • Ground flaxseed: Use ground chia seeds instead. Both thicken the smoothie, though their nutrients differ.
  • Medjool date: Use another soft date or a little maple syrup, or omit for a less sweet smoothie.
  • Baby bok choy: Substitute a small amount of kale, another lower-oxalate green.
  • Cinnamon: Use regular cinnamon if Ceylon is unavailable, or leave it out.

📝 How to Make Blueberry Cocoa Smoothie

The complete recipe with exact amounts is below. These additional tips will help you get the best results.

  1. Steam the greens: If you prefer not to eat raw greens, lightly steam them before blending. This softens their texture, makes them easier to digest for some people, and reduces certain compounds, such as oxalates, depending on the green. Let them cool before adding to the smoothie. Steaming is optional-raw bok choy is perfectly fine to use.
  1. Add liquids first: Pour the water into the blender before adding the silken tofu, greens, blueberries, and remaining ingredients. This helps everything blend smoothly.
  1. Adjust the consistency: Add a splash of cold water or nondairy milk if the smoothie is too thick, or a few ice cubes if you prefer it colder and frostier.
  1. Add chia seeds if making ahead: Mix in 1 tablespoon of ground chia seeds after blending if you're saving the smoothie for later. Chia absorbs liquid as it sits, creating a thicker, creamier texture while adding fiber and plant-based omega-3 fats.

🍌 Why No Banana?

Bananas are nutritious, but they contain an enzyme called polyphenol oxidase (PPO) that can reduce the absorption of cocoa flavanols. While we don't have large clinical trials showing that this affects long-term health outcomes, I don't miss the banana, and blueberries are expensive-so why take the chance? Instead, I use heart-healthy silken tofu for a creamy, milkshake-like texture and extra plant protein. A date adds just enough natural sweetness.

✔️ Storage & Meal Prep Tips

Enjoy this smoothie immediately for the freshest flavor and to help preserve its flavanols, which may decline through oxidation during storage.

Meal prep: Steam the greens ahead of time, let them cool, and refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. You can also portion the other ingredients in advance for quick blending.

Making ahead: After blending, stir in 1 tablespoon of ground chia seeds. They absorb liquid as the smoothie sits, helping it thicken while adding extra fiber and plant-based omega-3 fats.

Refrigerate promptly in an airtight container with minimal headspace for up to 24 hours. Stir before drinking and add a splash of water or nondairy milk if it has thickened.

Why is this blueberry cocoa smoothie made without banana?

Does this smoothie provide the flavanol dose used in COSMOS?

Low-Calorie, High-Protein Blueberry Cocoa Smoothie Recipe

Creamy Blueberry Cocoa Smoothie with heart-healthy flavanols and fiber to help lower bad cholesterol. High in protein without protein powder! Ingredients

  • 1 ½ tablespoons unsweetened cocoa powder (see notes)
  • 1 cup frozen blueberries (see notes)

  • 1 Medjool date pitted

  • Add ice: For a frostier smoothie, add 2 to 4 ice cubes and blend again. Serve immediately.

Ingredients You’ll Need

For the maple pecan topping:

  • Vegan butter – Use a stick-style vegan butter rather than a tub spread. Tub spreads carry more water, which can leave the caramel thin. We like to use Country Crock Plant Butter.

  • Non-dairy milk – A small splash loosens the caramel so it spreads evenly over the pecans.

  • All-purpose flour – Added in two stages. We give both cup and gram measurements because flour is easy to over-measure, and a kitchen scale takes the guesswork out.
  • Baking powder and salt – Mixed in after the first rise. Baking powder gives the buns extra lift. 

For the filling:

  • Softened vegan butter, brown sugar, and cinnamon – Softened butter makes a spreadable filling that stays inside the swirl instead of leaking out the bottom.

Tips for the Best Results

  • Check your milk’s temperature. It should feel just warm to the touch, about 105-110°F if you have a thermometer. Anything warmer will kill the yeast, so your dough won’t rise.
  • Go easy on the extra flour. Weigh your flour if you can, and when kneading, add more a tablespoon or two at a time. Stop as soon as the dough is smooth and springs back when you poke it. Too much flour is the most common cause of dry and dense buns. 
  • Roll tight and cut with floss. A tight log makes for a prominent swirl. Slide a piece of unflavored floss under the log, cross the ends over the top, and pull for a clean cut that doesn’t squash the rolls. You can also cut gently with a serrated knife. 
  • Don’t wait to flip then. The caramel needs a few minutes to settle, but if the buns sit much longer than 10 minutes, it starts to set and stick to the parchment.

Serving Suggestions

These Maple Pecan Sticky Buns are best served warm right after the flip, while the caramel is still glossy. For a brunch spread, pair them with something savory like our classic tofu scramble or breakfast potato hash, plus fresh fruit and a big pot of coffee. They also work as dessert: warm one up and top with a scoop of vegan vanilla ice cream.

Storage Instructions

  • Room temperature: Keep the buns covered for up to 2 days on the counter. 
  • Refrigerator: Store in an airtight container for up to 5 days.
  • Freezer: Wrap the buns individually and freeze in a freezer bag for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge.
  • Reheating: Microwave a single bun for 15-20 seconds, or warm the pan, covered with foil, in a 350˚F oven for about 10 minutes. The caramel softens back up as it warms.

Recipe FAQs

Can I make maple pecan sticky buns ahead of time?

Yes. Assemble the buns through step 11, arranging them in the pan, then cover tightly and refrigerate overnight instead of doing the second rise. In the morning, let the pan sit at room temperature for about an hour, until the buns look puffy, then bake as directed.

Can I use instant yeast instead of active dry?

Yes, use the same amount. You can still add it to the warm milk mixture as written. Instant yeast tends to work faster, so go by how the dough looks (doubled in size) rather than the clock.

Why didn’t my dough rise?

The usual culprits are expired yeast or milk that was too hot. If the yeast mixture isn’t foamy after 10 minutes, start over with a fresh packet before adding any flour. A chilly kitchen can also slow things down, so find a warm spot and give the dough extra time.

Can I make these nut-free?

Yes. Leave out the pecans and pour the maple caramel straight into the parchment-lined dish. You’ll still get a sticky glaze on top, just without the crunch.

What’s the difference between sticky buns and cinnamon rolls?

They start from the same rolled, cinnamon-filled dough. Cinnamon rolls are usually frosted after baking, while sticky buns bake on top of a caramel layer and get flipped out of the pan, so the caramel becomes the topping.

Ingredients

For the maple pecan topping

  • 2 cups roughly chopped pecans
  • ½ cup (1 stick) vegan butter
  • ¾ cup (155g) brown sugar
  • ¼ cup maple syrup
  • ¼ cup non-dairy milk
  • ½ teaspoon vanilla extract
  • ½ teaspoon salt

For the dough

  • 2 cups non-dairy milk, warm to the touch
  • ½ cup (100g) granulated sugar
  • ½ cup (1 stick) vegan butter, melted
  • 1 packet (2¼ teaspoons) active dry yeast
  • ½ teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 5 cups (600g) all-purpose flour, divided, plus more as needed for kneading
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt

For the filling

  • 6 tablespoons vegan butter, softened
  • ½ cup (100g) brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon ground cinnamon

Instructions

Make the maple pecan topping

  1. Prep the pan. Line a 9×13″ baking dish with parchment paper and spread the chopped pecans in an even layer.
  2. Cook the topping. In a small saucepan, heat the vegan butter, brown sugar, maple syrup, and non-dairy milk over medium heat. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the butter melts and everything combines. Simmer for 2-3 minutes, then remove from the heat.
  3. Pour over the pecans. Stir in the vanilla extract and salt, then pour the mixture evenly over the pecans. Set aside to cool.

Make the dough and assemble

  1. Combine the wet ingredients. In a large bowl, whisk together the non-dairy milk, sugar, and melted vegan butter. The mixture should be just warm to the touch. If it’s too hot, it will kill the yeast.
  2. Proof the yeast. Sprinkle the yeast evenly over the mixture and stir. Let it sit in a warm place for 10 minutes. The yeast should be foamy and bubbly, which means it’s alive.
  3. Add the vanilla. Whisk until combined.
  4. Mix and rise. Add 4 cups (480g) of the flour and stir with a wooden spoon until just combined. Cover with a clean kitchen towel and let rise in a warm place for 1 hour, until doubled in size.
  5. Make the filling. While the dough rises, add the softened vegan butter, brown sugar, and cinnamon to a medium bowl, then mix until combined. Set aside.
  6. Add the remaining flour. Once the dough has risen, stir in the remaining 1 cup (120g) of flour, baking powder, and salt.
  7. Knead. Transfer the dough to a clean work surface and knead for 10 minutes, adding more flour as needed, until it forms a smooth ball. The dough shouldn’t be sticky and should spring back when you poke it.
  8. Roll out. Roll the dough into a ½” thick rectangle. It doesn’t need to be perfect, but you can trim the edges for a cleaner shape.
  9. Fill. Spread the filling evenly over the dough.
  10. Roll and cut. Starting at the long side closest to you, roll the dough into a tight log and pinch the seam to seal. Cut the log into 12 even pieces using a serrated knife or unflavored floss.
  11. Second rise. Arrange the rolls evenly in the baking dish on top of the maple pecan mixture. Cover with a damp, clean towel and let rise in a warm place for 1 hour. About 30 minutes into the rise, preheat the oven to 350˚F.
  12. Bake. Bake for 27-30 minutes, until golden brown.
  13. Flip. Let cool in the pan for 10 minutes. Place a large baking sheet or serving tray on top of the baking dish and carefully flip it upside down so the buns release onto the tray. Remove the baking dish and parchment paper.
  14. Serve. Serve warm and enjoy!

  • Milk temperature: Warm to the touch means about 105-110°F if you have a thermometer.
  • Flour: Weighing is the most accurate. The amount of extra flour you need during kneading varies by humidity and brand, so add it gradually.
  • Cutting with floss: Slide unflavored floss under the log, cross the ends over the top, and pull for a clean cut.
  • Flipping: Flip after about 10 minutes, while the caramel is still loose. Spoon any topping or pecans left in the pan back over the buns.
  • Make ahead: After arranging the buns in the pan, cover and refrigerate overnight. Let sit at room temperature for about 1 hour, until puffy, then bake.

How to Store and Reheat Chicken Spaghetti

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 4 days. The spaghetti keeps absorbing the sauce as it sits, so it will look thicker the next day. That’s completely normal, and a splash of liquid brings it right back.

Freezer

I don’t recommend freezing this one. Cream cheese sauces tend to separate and turn grainy after thawing, and spaghetti that has already soaked up sauce gets soft and mushy. It’s best enjoyed fresh or as leftovers from the fridge.

Reheating

  • Stovetop. This is the best way to bring back the creamy texture. Add the leftovers to a pot with a splash of chicken broth or milk and warm over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally, until heated through.
  • Microwave. Add a splash of broth or milk, cover the bowl loosely, and heat in 1-minute intervals, stirring between each one so the sauce stays smooth.

Make Ahead

This recipe is best made fresh because the spaghetti continues to absorb the sauce as it sits. You can still do almost all of the prep earlier in the day. Chop the chicken and onion and keep them covered in the refrigerator, measure out the seasonings, and shred the cheese. When it’s time for dinner, everything is ready to go straight into the pot. Pre-chopped onion and rotisserie chicken make it even easier.

  1. Break the noodles into roughly 2-inch pieces. I find this size works well for stirring the lasagna noodles through the skillet without them getting tangled up. It makes scooping out individual servings much easier, too.
  2. Give everything enough room to cook. You’ll need a large, deep, broiler-safe skillet with a lid, about 12 inches in diameter.   3. Keep the lid on during simmering. Press the broken noodles down so they’re mostly submerged in the broth, then cover and maintain a gentle simmer. I give them an occasional stir to separate any pieces sticking together, then pop the lid straight back on. The trapped steam helps cook the noodles that aren’t completely covered by liquid.
  4. Leave a little liquid in the skillet! The noodles will keep absorbing moisture as the sauce comes together, so the mixture should still look slightly loose once the pasta and broccoli are tender. If the noodles are still firm but most of the broth has disappeared, add a splash of water or broth, cover, and let them cook a little longer. Nobody wants dry chicken and broccoli lasagna!
  5. Be gentle with the cream and cheese. I turn the heat down to low before stirring in the heavy cream, Parmesan, and shredded chicken. Boiling the sauce can make it separate or turn grainy. Also, keep a close eye on the mozzarella under the broiler. It only needs 2-4 minutes to turn bubbly and lightly golden.

🙋🏽‍♀️ Recipe FAQs

Can I make cauliflower cream without milk?

Use vegetable broth instead of nondairy milk. Add more gradually after blending if needed to reach your preferred consistency.

Can I use frozen cauliflower for cauliflower cream sauce?

Steam frozen florets until very tender, then drain well and blend as directed. Use tenderness rather than a fixed cooking time as your guide.

Why is my cauliflower cream grainy?

The cauliflower may be undercooked or need more blending. It should mash easily with a fork before going into the blender. If the sauce is already blended but still grainy, gently cook it until the remaining pieces soften, then cool enough for your blender and blend again.

Can I use a food processor or immersion blender?

Yes, although a countertop blender generally makes it easier to achieve a silky texture. With a food processor, stop to scrape down the sides as needed. With an immersion blender, use a deep container and keep the blade submerged while blending.

Sweet Potato Black Bean Soup

This sweet potato black bean soup is made with hearty black beans and tender sweet potatoes simmered in a thick broth infused with zesty spices. It's incredibly satisfying and loaded with flavor. A big bowl of this totally hits the spot on chilly fall evenings! Course Soup Cuisine American Prep Time 15 minutes minutes Cook Time 30 minutes minutes Total Time 45 minutes minutes Servings 5 Calories 344kcal Author Alissa Saenz

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 2 teaspoons ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 3 cups vegetable broth
  • 2 (15 ounce/425 gram) cans black beans, drained and lightly rinsed
  • 1 medium sweet potato (about 1 pound), diced
  • 1 tablespoon lime juice
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Chopped fresh cilantro, optional, for serving
US Customary - Metric

Instructions

  • Coat the bottom of a large pot with the oil and place it over medium heat. Give the oil a minute to heat up, then add the onion. Cook the onion for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it begins to soften.
  • Stir in the garlic, tomato paste, cumin, smoked paprika, and oregano. Cook the mixture for about 1 minute, stirring constantly, until it becomes very fragrant.
  • Stir in the broth, beans, and sweet potato. Raise the heat and bring the mixture to a boil, then reduce the heat to a simmer and let the soup cook for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sweet potatoes are very tender.
  • Remove the pot from heat and blend about half of the soup, either using an immersion blender or by carefully transferring it to a conventional blender. Always use caution when blending hot liquids. You can thin the soup with some water if it seems too thick.
  • Stir in the lime juice. Season the soup with salt and pepper to taste.
  • Ladle into bowls and top each one with a sprinkle of fresh cilantro, if desired. Serve.
