New community fundraising initiative launches at 18 restaurants, connecting neighborhood schools, nonprofits and local organizations with opportunities to raise funds while bringing people together over great food

Dallas, TX  (RestaurantNews.com)  Rusty Taco the fast-casual restaurant brand known for its fresh, street-style tacos, welcoming atmosphere and neighborhood roots, is strengthening its commitment to community engagement through a new partnership with Force4Good, a technology platform designed to make local fundraising easier, more accessible and more effective for restaurants and the organizations they support.

Launched in October 2026 across 18 participating Rusty Taco restaurants, the initiative creates new opportunities for local schools, youth sports teams, nonprofits and community organizations to connect with Rusty Taco franchisees and host fundraising events that bring people together for a shared purpose.

The partnership represents an important step in Rusty Taco’s ongoing commitment to supporting the neighborhoods it serves, while giving franchise operators additional resources to establish meaningful relationships with local organizations.

“Rusty Taco has always been about more than great tacos. It’s about creating a place where people feel welcome, where neighbors connect and where communities come together,” said Michael Meche, President of Rusty Taco. “Our partnership with Force4Good gives our franchisees a simple, sustainable way to turn those values into action. Whether it’s helping a local school raise funds, supporting a youth sports team or partnering with a neighborhood nonprofit, we want Rusty Taco to be a place that makes a positive difference.”

Making Community Fundraising Easier and More Accessible

Through Force4Good, participating Rusty Taco restaurants can streamline the entire fundraising process, from coordinating events and promoting them to tracking sales and managing fundraising administration.

The platform helps eliminate many of the time-consuming logistical challenges traditionally associated with restaurant fundraising events, making it easier for franchisees to support local causes while remaining focused on delivering a great guest experience. For participating organizations, the initiative offers an accessible way to connect with a neighborhood restaurant, engage supporters and raise funds through community dining events.

The partnership is designed to provide several benefits:

  • For local schools and nonprofits: Opportunities to organize restaurant-based fundraising events that bring together families, supporters and community members.
  • For Rusty Taco franchisees: A repeatable local marketing and community engagement program that simplifies planning, promotion and reporting.
  • For restaurant guests: New opportunities to support causes that matter to them while enjoying their favorite Rusty Taco menu items.
  • For local communities: A collaborative approach to strengthening relationships between neighborhood businesses and the organizations working to make a difference.

By simplifying the fundraising experience, Rusty Taco hopes to encourage more frequent and meaningful collaboration between participating restaurants and the communities they serve.

A Proven Platform for Community Engagement

Force4Good works with thousands of restaurant and retail locations to make fundraising and community partnerships easier to organize and measure. According to Force4Good’s published platform statistics, more than 9,500 retail locations use its community giving technology, while more than 45,000 nonprofit organizations are part of its fundraising network.

Force4Good also reports that its marketing programs helped secure more than 2,250 fundraising events in 2025, generating approximately $1.77 million in restaurant sales across participating businesses. These platform-wide results demonstrate the opportunity for restaurants to combine community support with customer engagement and sustainable local marketing.

For Rusty Taco, the partnership provides the infrastructure to develop those opportunities across its franchise network, creating a more consistent approach to community involvement while preserving each restaurant’s ability to build relationships with organizations in its own neighborhood.

Supporting Franchisees and Building Stronger Neighborhood Connections

As Rusty Taco continues to grow, maintaining its identity as a neighborhood gathering place remains an important part of the brand’s strategy. The Force4Good partnership reflects the belief that restaurants can play a meaningful role in their communities beyond serving food. By providing franchisees with the tools to coordinate fundraising events, promote local partnerships and track participation, Rusty Taco is creating a scalable program that supports both community involvement and restaurant-level business development.

“Community engagement shouldn’t be complicated or limited to a once-a-year event,” added Meche. “We want to make it easier for our franchisees to say yes when a local organization reaches out and to build relationships that continue long after a single fundraiser.”

The program also offers an opportunity to introduce Rusty Taco to new guests, encourage repeat visits and create shared experiences centered around food and community.

Looking Ahead to 2027

Rusty Taco launched the Force4Good partnership in October 2026 with 18 participating restaurants and plans to add three additional locations during the first quarter of 2027. As the initiative develops, participating restaurants will have opportunities to establish partnerships with local schools, sports organizations, charitable groups and other neighborhood organizations.

The company anticipates that the program will become an increasingly valuable part of its community engagement strategy, helping franchisees build stronger local relationships while creating new opportunities for organizations to raise awareness and support for their missions.

We cover the wines of Paso Robles’ Ancient Peaks with regularity, so these 2026 offerings should not come as a surprise. The value present in these bottlings, however, is more of a shock…

Updated late 2026 with the latest releases.

2024 Ancient Peaks Sauvignon Blanc Paso Robles – Quite tropical at first, this wine moves into grassier territory, with a slight astringency developing late in the game. Gentle white florals complement an emerging and insistent lemon peel character, giving the wine a clean, crisp finish. A- / $20

2024 Ancient Peaks Chardonnay Paso Robles Santa Margarita Ranch – Vanilla-laced but far from overwhelming, this is a refreshing and easygoing chardonnay with layers of vanilla, lychee, and honeysuckle — though all are quite balanced and restrained. Emerging lemon notes on the finish give the wine a custard-like quality, though it’s never overly sweet or overly woody. Pairs perfectly with lighter fare, though the rich body can hold up to spice and richness. Outstanding value. A- / $20

2023 Ancient Peaks Zinfandel Paso Robles Santa Margarita Ranch – This is a wine we’ve seen many times before, and this 2023 expression doesn’t change the formula dramatically — though it does build on its previous iterations. Notes of black cherries in syrup and chocolate sauce are expected and provide something of a holiday vibe — but it’s one which is quite sweet but also balanced by a gentle tartness. Those cherry notes and some light, herbal greenery inform a finish that feels a bit more elevated than previous renditions, although now at 15% abv, it’s certainly not because of any light-handedness in the vineyard. A- / $24

2022 Ancient Peaks Oyster Ridge Santa Margarita Ranch – This cab-heavy (65%) Bordeaux blend hits heavy with graphite and licorice from the start, meandering its way to notes of dense blackberry and prune, then cola and dark chocolate. Brooding but quite sweet, things build to a crescendo on the spice-dusted finish, which sees more dried fruits giving this wine an almost Port-like quality, laced with notes of chocolate-covered cherries. A bit too overripe this year, its 14.9% abv is hardly a surprise. B+ / $60

2022 Ancient Peaks Pearl Cabernet Sauvignon Santa Margarita Ranch – 100% cabernet in 100% new French oak makes for a decadent expression of the grape, and this 2022 Pearl hits all the high points for California cab, while keeping prices well below $100. While plenty boozy (another 14.9%er), the exuberant fruits, both black and red, currants and strawberries, crushed together with plums, leads the way to a lip-smacking midpalate that, eventually, finds its way to bittersweet chocolate and moderated baking spice to add a touch of nuance. While far less overtly sweet than Oyster Ridge, it’s equally decadent, almost chewy in its opulence, with lingering hints of cinnamon and nutmeg adding an exotic bent. Lovely. A / $72

Copycat Taco Bell Caramel Apple Empanadas

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1-1/2 cups finely chopped peeled tart apples
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/8 teaspoon ground allspice
  • 1/3 cup thick caramel apple dip
  • 2 sheets refrigerated pie crust
  • 1 large egg yolk
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 1/4 cup butter, melted
  • 1 cup cinnamon sugar
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. In a small saucepan, combine apples, sugar, cinnamon and allspice. Cook until apples are tender, 4-5 minutes. Drain; discard liquid. Transfer to a small bowl. Add caramel; stir to coat. Scoop by tablespoonfuls onto waxed paper. Freeze until firm, about 1 hour.
  2. Unroll pie crust; cut into 14 circles using a 4-in. circle cookie cutter (rerolling dough scraps as needed).
  3. In a small bowl, whisk together egg yolk and water. Brush edges of crusts with egg wash; place apple filling in the center. Fold crusts over filling and seal edges well with a fork. Place 2 in. apart on parchment-lined baking sheets. Freeze until firm, about 30 minutes.
  4. Brush remaining egg wash over tops. Cut 2 small slits on the top of each empanada.
  5. Bake at 425° for 12-14 minutes or until golden brown. Remove from oven and brush with melted butter. Carefully toss in cinnamon sugar. Serve warm.

Nutrition Facts

1 piece: 234 calories, 13g fat (6g saturated fat), 29mg cholesterol, 159mg sodium, 28g carbohydrate (14g sugars, 0 fiber), 2g protein.

Cherry Peach Pie with Salted Pecan Streusel

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1-1/3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1-1/2 teaspoons sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon pumpkin pie spice
  • 4 tablespoons shortening, cubed
  • 4 tablespoons cold butter, cubed
  • 3 to 4 tablespoons water
  • TOPPING:
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1/4 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/4 cup crushed gingersnap cookies
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup old-fashioned oats
  • 1/2 cup chopped pecans
  • 1/4 cup butter, melted
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • FILLING:
  • 2 cups sliced peeled peaches
  • 1 cup frozen pitted dark sweet cherries, thawed
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/3 cup sugar
  • 1/3 cup packed brown sugar
  • 4 tablespoons potato starch
  • Pinch salt
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. In a large bowl, mix flour, sugar, salt and pie spice; cut in shortening and butter until crumbly. Gradually add ice water, tossing with a fork until dough holds together when pressed. Shape into a disk; wrap and refrigerate 1 hour or overnight.
  2. Preheat oven to 400°. On a lightly floured surface, roll dough to a 1/8-in.-thick circle; transfer to a 9-in. pie plate. Trim crust to 1/2 in. beyond rim of plate; flute edge. Refrigerate while preparing filling.
  3. For the topping, in a medium bowl, whisk together flour, sugars, crushed cookies and salt. Stir in oats and pecans. Add the butter and vanilla until small clumps form. Set aside.
  4. In a large bowl, toss peaches and cherries with lemon juice and vanilla. In a separate bowl, whisk together sugars, potato starch and salt. Add to fruit, toss until well coated.
  5. Transfer filling to prepared crust. Sprinkle with reserved topping; pressing down to adhere as needed. Bake for 20 minutes. Reduce oven setting to 350°, bake until crust and topping are golden brown and fruit is bubbly, 20-25 minutes longer. Cool completely on a wire rack.

Nutrition Facts

1 piece: 503 calories, 23g fat (9g saturated fat), 31mg cholesterol, 563mg sodium, 70g carbohydrate (38g sugars, 2g fiber), 5g protein.

More than any other American holiday, Thanksgiving demands quite a bit from the wine list. There needs to be something that can stand up to turkey, cut through whatever’s in the gravy and still work with that mystery dish your aunt brings that is of indeterminable origin. New York’s Konstantin Frank has inadvertently constructed a lineup for this exact litmus test. Riesling remains one of the stable cores of their operations, bright enough to handle the richest of foods without flinching. However, the reds and rosé also earn their seat at the table, and today we’re checking out a trio suitable for the big dinner and every preference.

2025 Dr. Konstantin Frank Dry Riesling – While they may call this a dry Riesling, what pours in the glass clearly has other ideas. Lime zest, nectarine and cold apple leap out before even committing to a sip. There’s a slow, almost syrupy viscosity across the tongue, and a trace of sweetness cutting through from underneath. Acidity arrives at the right moment, bright and crisp as biting into an actual apple, and the orchard flavors stay put as the finish lingers. A steal at this price. A- / $20

2024 Dr. Konstantin Frank Old Vines Pinot Noir – Dried herbs, baking spices and a touch of graphite meld with red berries, giving this a lovely evocation of winter’s imminent arrival. As the palate progresses, the light body carries the fruit forward, but its balanced with a nice kick of clove. The finish carries on with a touch of chocolate and dark cherry, with tannins framing everything quite nicely. It’s incredibly approachable and versatile: it will pair well with a well-dressed turkey, but also as a solo standalone for when the relatives have left, the dishes are drying, and the silence of your house returns to provide merciful and sweet relief. A- / $28

2025 Dr. Konstantin Frank Cabernet Franc Rosé – There’s a surprisingly big and complex aroma of juicy cherry, fresh peach and a touch of floral influence on the nose, with a distinctive layer of mocha and oak cutting through with some time in the glass. The light-to-medium weight palate is rich with orange and fig, and there’s a slightly chalky edge to the finish, providing a bit of grit. It’s well-balanced and holds up incredibly well for the price point. This one deserves a spot on the table before the main event. B+ / $20

This quick pickled red cabbage is an easy, delicious way to eat more cruciferous vegetables. It provides fiber and antioxidants behind its vibrant purple color-with a tangy crunch that brightens salads, sandwiches, tacos, and grain bowls. Made with apple cider vinegar, fresh lime juice, no added sugar, and a low-sodium option.

[feast_advanced_jump_to]

🔍 Quick Look: Pickled Red Cabbage

  • ⏱️ Prep Time: 10 minutes
  • 🎛️ Cook Time: 5 minutes
  • 👥 Servings: 6
  • 📊 Calories: ~40 kcal (based on nutrition panel)
  • 👩🏽‍🍳 Flavor Profile: Tangy, bright, and garlicky, with a crunchy texture and optional heat.
  • 💪🏼 Nutrition: High in vitamin C to help boost iron absorption from plants.
  • ⭐ Difficulty: Easy-just shred the cabbage, briefly simmer the brine, and combine.

👩🏽‍⚕️ Dietitian's Note

Red cabbage is a cruciferous vegetable, like broccoli and kale. It contains glucosinolates, plant compounds that break down into substances studied for their potential health benefits. Its purple color comes from anthocyanins, which have antioxidant properties. It also provides fiber to support regular bowel movements and nourish beneficial gut bacteria.

For an iron-rich meal, pair this pickled cabbage with lentils, beans, or tofu. Cabbage contains vitamin C, which helps your body absorb plant-based iron, although heating and storage reduce some of it. Add a squeeze of fresh lime before serving for extra vitamin C.

This is a quick vinegar pickle rather than a fermented food, so it is not a reliable source of probiotics.

Why You'll Love This Recipe:

This pickled red cabbage recipe makes it easy to add more vegetables to your meals, and boost iron absorption. Plus, it's:

  • Quick to prepare: Just a few minutes of shredding and a simple homemade brine.
  • No added sugar or oil: Bright, tangy flavor from vinegar, lime, garlic, and spices.
  • Crunchy and colorful: An easy way to make simple meals more satisfying.
  • Made with low-sodium salt: Reduces the sodium added to the brine compared with regular salt.
  • Meal-prep friendly: Keep a jar refrigerated for an easy vegetable topping throughout the week.

👩🏼‍🌾 Ingredients

Below, I share ingredient notes from both a nutrition expert's and trained chef's perspective.

  • Red cabbage: Cabbage heads vary in size, so measure the shreds to keep the cabbage-to-brine ratio consistent.
  • Apple cider vinegar: Gives this pickled cabbage recipe a fruity tang. Choose vinegar labeled 5% acidity.
  • Lime juice: Adds a fresh citrus flavor that complements the vinegar.
  • Low-sodium salt (optional): Seasons the brine with less sodium than regular salt. The amount of sodium varies by brand.
  • Garlic: Lightly crush the clove to release its flavor into the brine.
  • Jalapeño: Adds fresh heat. Remove the seeds and inner membranes for a milder result, or leave it out.
  • Coriander: I use coriander seeds, but ground coriander also works. The seeds add a subtle citrusy flavor, while ground coriander disperses throughout the brine and may make it slightly cloudy.
  • Black peppercorns and red pepper flakes: Optional additions for warmth and extra heat.

See the printable recipe card below for quantities.

🌱 Substitutions

This recipe is versatile and easy to adapt. Below are some simple ingredient substitutions.

  • Apple cider vinegar: Substitute white wine vinegar or distilled white vinegar labeled 5% acidity. Distilled vinegar gives a sharper flavor.
  • Lime juice: Use the same amount of lemon juice.
  • Red cabbage: Green cabbage works, although it won't have the same vibrant color or anthocyanin content.
  • Coriander: Use the same amount of coriander seeds or ground coriander. Both work well.
  • Less heat: Omit the jalapeño and red pepper flakes.
  • Salt: Regular salt works in the same amount, or omit entirely, if preferred.

📝 How to Make Pickled Red Cabbage

How to Shred Cabbage:

Is pickled red cabbage fermented?

Pickled Red Cabbage Recipe (No Added Sugar)

This quick pickled red cabbage recipe has fiber and antioxidants, with no added sugar. A crunchy topping for sandwiches, salads, and bowls. Course Condiment, Side Dish Cuisine Vegan Diet Vegan, Vegetarian Prep Time 10 minutes minutes Cook Time 5 minutes minutes Total Time 15 minutes minutes Servings 6 servings Calories 41kcal Author Nisha Melvani

Ingredients

  • 1 small head red cabbage about 4 to 5 cups thinly shredded
  • 1 cup apple cider vinegar labeled 5% acidity
  • ½ cup water
  • 2 tablespoons fresh lime juice
  • ½ teaspoon salt preferably low-sodium (see notes)
  • 1 clove garlic lightly crushed
  • ½ jalapeño thinly sliced, optional
  • ½ teaspoon black peppercorns optional
  • ½ teaspoon coriander seeds or ground coriander, optional
  • ¼ teaspoon red pepper flakes optional

Instructions

  • Shred the cabbage: Remove damaged outer leaves and rinse the cabbage. Cut into halves or quarters. Use a cabbage peeler to shave thin ribbons from a cut face, keeping the cabbage stable and your fingers clear of the blade. Alternatively, use a sharp knife, mandoline with a hand guard, or food processor fitted with a slicing disc. Discard the tough core.
  • Measure: Transfer 4 to 5 cups shredded cabbage to a large heatproof glass or stainless-steel bowl.
  • Make the brine: Combine the vinegar, water, lime juice, low-sodium salt, garlic, and jalapeño in a small saucepan. Add the peppercorns, coriander seeds or ground coriander, and red pepper flakes, if using. Bring to a simmer, stirring to dissolve the salt, then remove from the heat.
  • Combine: Pour the hot brine over the cabbage. Toss well with tongs and gently press the cabbage into the liquid.
  • Cool and pack: Let cool briefly, tossing occasionally as the cabbage softens. Pack into clean jars, pour in all the brine, and gently press the cabbage down.
  • Refrigerate: Cover and refrigerate within 2 hours of preparation. Enjoy once chilled, or refrigerate overnight for the best flavor. Keep refrigerated and use within 2 weeks.

Notes

  • Salt: Optional. I used SaltWise Salicornia White Salt, a lower-sodium seasoning made from salicornia-an edible coastal plant with a naturally salty taste.
  • Coriander: I use whole coriander seeds, but the same amount of ground coriander works, too.
  • Milder flavor: Omit the jalapeño and red pepper flakes.
  • Refrigerator pickle: This recipe is not suitable for canning or room-temperature storage.

French Onion Stuffing

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 16 ounces sourdough bread, cut into 1-in. cubes
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 3 pounds medium onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon pepper
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/4 cup dry sherry
  • 2 tablespoons minced fresh thyme sprigs, plus more for garnish
  • 2 large eggs, room temperature
  • 2 cups reduced-sodium beef broth
  • 6 ounces Gruyere cheese, grated
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. Preheat oven to 350°. Spread bread cubes in a single layer on two baking sheets. Bake 10 minutes; flip bread cubes and rotate baking sheets. Bake until very dry and golden brown, 5-8 minutes. Set aside to cool.
  2. Meanwhile, in a Dutch oven, heat butter and olive oil over medium heat. Add onions; cook until softened, 10-15 minutes, stirring frequently. Stir in salt and pepper. Reduce heat to medium-low; cook until deep golden brown, 40-50 minutes, stirring occasionally. Add garlic; cook until fragrant, 30-60 seconds. Add dry sherry; use a wooden spoon to scrape up browned bits. Stir in thyme. Remove from heat.
  3. In a large bowl, whisk eggs until smooth. Stir in cooked onions and bread cubes. Stir in beef broth until evenly moistened.
  4. Spoon stuffing into a greased 13x9-in. baking dish. Cover with foil; bake 30 minutes. Uncover; bake until crispy and browned, 15-20 minutes.
  5. Remove from the oven; increase oven temperature to 425°. Sprinkle cheese on top. Bake until melted and bubbly, 3-5 minutes. Garnish with thyme to serve.
