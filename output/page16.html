This peri peri chicken is my kind of dinner. It has big flavor, juicy chicken, and a marinade that lets the blender do the work. Boneless chicken thighs soak in a smooth blend of Fresno chiles, roasted red peppers, garlic, and lemon, then roast until the edges are deeply golden and a little charred. You only need about fifteen minutes of hands-on prep, plus time for the chicken to marinate. Every bite is smoky, garlicky, bright, and just spicy enough to keep things interesting. I brush on a buttery finishing sauce before serving, and trust me, you’ll want to catch every drop. This has become one of my favorites for backyard gatherings and casual dinners with friends. Most of the prep happens the night before, which leaves me more time to enjoy the company.

The Grocery Store Shortcut to Real Peri Peri Flavor

This recipe starts with a tiny pepper and a surprisingly long journey. Traditional peri peri chicken traces its roots to African Bird’s Eye chiles, peppers known for their intense heat and distinctive fruity flavor. It’s often roasted as a whole butterflied bird, like spatchcock chicken, but I went with thighs here because they stay juicier at high heat. The challenge was figuring out how to recreate those flavors with ingredients that most home cooks can actually find at the grocery store.

I tested a few different pepper combinations before landing on Fresno chiles and roasted red peppers. Some versions were too aggressive with heat and overwhelmed the chicken. Others lacked the bright fruity character that makes peri peri sauce so distinctive. The Fresnos provided a clean, bright heat, while the roasted peppers added sweetness, color, and body to the sauce. Together they created something that felt true to the spirit of peri peri while remaining approachable for everyday cooking.

The high oven temperature creates deeply caramelized edges while the meat underneath stays incredibly juicy. Even batches that cooked a few minutes longer than intended stayed tender and flavorful. Under the broiler, the edges begin to char slightly, creating little pockets of smoky flavor that balance the sweetness from the peppers and honey. The spice level builds gradually rather than hitting all at once, which makes the chicken approachable even for people who don’t typically gravitate toward spicy foods.

Where the Finishing Sauce Came From

After the first round of testing, the chicken itself tasted great, but I felt like some of the brightness from the lemon disappeared during roasting. That’s where the finishing sauce came from.

I reserve ¼ cup of the marinade before it touches the raw chicken, then whisk it with melted butter and fresh lemon juice. Brushed over the hot chicken right out of the oven, it adds a final layer of flavor that completely transforms the dish. The butter rounds out the heat, and the fresh lemon wakes everything back up.

Setting the sauce aside first matters for safety, too. Marinade that has been sitting with raw chicken can’t be brushed onto cooked chicken without boiling it first. Reserving it up front skips that step entirely. It may seem like a small extra step, but it’s the detail that transforms the chicken from good to unforgettable.

Tips for Beginners

  • Give the chicken room in the pan. Spacing is one of the most important details in this recipe. One of my test batches was crowded onto a smaller baking sheet, and the chicken released enough moisture to partially steam instead of caramelize. It still tasted good, but I lost the crispy edges that make this recipe special. Use a large sheet pan and leave a little space between each thigh so the hot air can reach every side. If your pan is on the smaller side, split the chicken between two pans.
  • Don’t skip the broiler. Those final few minutes under the broiler create the charred spots that add so much flavor and visual appeal. This is where the smoky pockets come from that balance the sweetness of the peppers and honey. Stay close to the oven and watch it, since the honey in the marinade means the tops can go from golden to too dark quickly.

Peri Peri Chicken

Juicy boneless chicken thighs soak in a quick blender marinade of Fresno chiles, roasted red peppers, garlic, and lemon, then roast until golden and lightly charred. A buttery finishing sauce brushed on at the end makes every smoky, garlicky bite even better. How to Make Peri Peri Chicken Step by Step

Gather all the ingredients together. Since the marinade comes together quickly in the blender, having everything prepped and measured makes it easy to blend, marinate the chicken, and get dinner started without any last-minute scrambling.

Blend the marinade: Add ¼ cup drained Fresno chiles, ½ cup drained roasted red bell peppers, 6 cloves garlic, the zest and juice of 1 lemon, 2 tbsp white vinegar, 2 tbsp olive oil, 1 tbsp honey, 1 tbsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp dried oregano, 1 tsp kosher salt, and ½ tsp black pepper to a blender. Blend until completely smooth. The marinade should be a vibrant red-orange and pourable, with no visible chunks of pepper or garlic. Measure out ¼ cup of the marinade and set it aside in the refrigerator for the finishing sauce. Do this before the marinade touches the raw chicken.

Marinate the chicken: Place 12 boneless, skinless chicken thighs in a large bowl or zip-top bag. Pour the remaining marinade over the chicken and toss until every piece is thoroughly coated. Cover and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, or preferably overnight. A 4-hour marinade works well, but the overnight version consistently produces deeper flavor throughout the meat.

Arrange on the pan: Preheat the oven to 425°F and line a large sheet pan with parchment paper. Arrange the chicken thighs smooth side up on the prepared baking sheet, leaving a little space between each piece.

Roast the chicken: Bake for 35-40 minutes, or until the chicken is fully cooked and the tops begin to caramelize. The edges should look deep red and slightly sticky, and the thickest piece should reach 165°F.

Broil: Switch the oven to broil and cook for an additional 2-3 minutes until the tops are deeply golden and slightly charred in spots.

Make the finishing sauce: While the chicken rests, whisk together the reserved ¼ cup marinade, 2 tbsp melted butter, and 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice. It should be glossy and smooth.

Sauce and serve: Brush the finishing sauce generously over the hot chicken. Garnish with fresh parsley and serve with rice or crispy smashed potatoes.

Storage and Freezing Instructions

Leftovers store exceptionally well. Keep the chicken in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to four days. One thing I noticed during testing is that the flavors continue to develop overnight. The garlic, peppers, and spices seem to settle together even more, making leftovers every bit as enjoyable as the original meal.

Freezer

Let the cooked chicken cool completely, then store it in a freezer-safe container or zip-top bag for up to 3 months. Thaw it overnight in the refrigerator before reheating. The charred edges soften a bit after freezing, so a quick reheat in the oven or air fryer helps bring them back.

Reheating

I prefer reheating it in the oven or air fryer because it helps restore some of the texture on the outside and brings back those caramelized edges. Warm it at 375°F until heated through. The microwave works in a pinch, but the chicken will be softer.

Make Ahead

This recipe was practically built for make-ahead cooks. The marinade can be prepared up to two days in advance and stored in the refrigerator until needed. The chicken can also marinate overnight, which is my preferred approach whenever possible. When I’m hosting, I almost always prep everything the day before. That way all I have to do is roast the chicken, make the finishing sauce, and serve.

Serving Suggestions

Rice is the classic partner here because it soaks up every bit of that smoky red sauce. The first time I made this, I served it over those little microwave Spanish-style rice pouches, and it was amazing for a last-minute side. When I have a little more time, I’ll make a pot of fluffy white rice and spoon the extra finishing sauce right over the top.

When I want something with more crunch, my go-to is a pan of easy smashed potatoes. Their crispy edges echo the charred edges on the chicken, and they can roast on a second rack while the chicken cooks, so everything comes out at the same time.

For a fresh counterpoint to the heat, I love serving this chicken with grilled vegetables. The zucchini, bell peppers, and onions pick up the same smoky char, which makes this a natural fit for backyard gatherings. I’ll also add a bright green salad like this kale salad, since its lemon dressing cools things down and ties right back to the lemon in the sauce. Warm bread is always welcome too, for catching any sauce left on the plate.

For gatherings, I like arranging everything on a large platter with charred lemons scattered around the chicken. It creates a beautiful presentation and encourages people to squeeze extra lemon over the top.

Ready for the healthy upgrade you didn't know your chili needed? It's kale! Kale adds great texture and earthy flavor that pair perfectly with smoky spices, tender beans, and that thick tomato base.

Kale is my new favorite addition to chili, and quite frankly, it has nothing to do with making the chili healthier or adding "sneaky" veggies. Any nutritional benefit is just a bonus. The kale is absolutely not hiding in here — it's front and center and delicious! If you're a kale lover who goes crazy for dishes like my Tuscan kale soup, creamy kale curry, and kale pasta, then this kale chili is for you!

You can really throw kale into almost any of my chili recipes and it'll be good, but this particular chili is crafted around it. I tweaked the spices to make the flavor extra smoky, using both smoked paprika and chipotle chili powder, plus fire roasted tomatoes. And then there's a surprise ingredient you might never detect, but it makes the chili extra rich and intense — cocoa powder! (Trust me on this one.)

The result is a rich, smoky chili that's perfect for the earthy flavor andl texture of kale. And it's a super simple one-pot deal!

Ingredients You'll Need

Below you'll find a list of ingredients in this recipe, with notes and substitutions. Scroll all the way to the bottom of the post to see the full recipe, including the amount of each ingredient.

  • Vegetable oil. Any high-heat oil you like to cook with is fine. Olive, canola, coconut, and corn oil will all work.
  • Onion.
  • Bell peppers. The recipe calls for one red and one green. Feel free to switch that up with other colors, keeping in mind that the flavor will vary.
  • Tomato paste.
  • Garlic.
  • Cocoa powder. Say what?! Yes, we're putting chocolate in chili. You won't detect the taste as chocolate, but it will enhance the rich, smoky flavor of the chili base.
  • Spices. You'll need a blend of chili powder, cumin, smoked paprika, and chipotle chili powder.
  • Water.
  • Fire roasted tomatoes. Regular diced tomatoes can be substituted, but the chili will be less smoky.
  • Beans. The recipe calls for a can of red kidney beans and a can of black beans, but you're welcome to swap them out with whatever you have in the pantry, such as pinto beans, cannellini beans, chickpeas, or lentils.
  • Salt.
  • Kale. I use curly kale for this recipe, because it's readily available. Other varieties like red kale or Tuscan kale will work just fine.
  • Black pepper.

Tip: Don't forget the toppings! This chili is great with sliced avocado, chopped scallions, cilantro, chives, vegan cheese, guacamole, vegan sour cream, or cashew cream.

How It's Made

Below is a detailed photo tutorial on how to make this dish. Scroll all the way down if you'd like to skip right to the recipe!

Step 1: Cook the vegetables. Heat your oil in a large pot, then add diced onion and bell peppers. Cook the mixture for about 10 minutes to start softening them up.

Step 2: Cook the tomato paste. Add it to the pot and cook it for about 2 minutes, until it darkens a bit. Stir the entire time to prevent burning.

Step 3: Cook garlic and spices. Add minced garlic, cocoa powder, and all of your spices. Cook the mixture for about a minute, until the garlic and spices become very fragrant. Stir constantly.

Step 4: Simmer. Stir in the water, tomatoes, beans, and salt. Crank up the heat and bring everything to a boil, then reduce the heat and let the mixture simmer for about 15 minutes.

Step 5: Add the kale. It'll seem like a lot of kale, but it will cook down. Add a handful at a time, stir it in, then add more. Once it's all in, let the chili simmer for about 15 minutes.

Step 6: Finish the chili. Take the pot off of heat, season the chili with additional salt to taste and some black pepper, and adjust any other seasonings to suit your preference.

Step 7: Serve. Your kale chili is done! Ladle it into bowls, pile on the toppings, and dig in! This chili is perfect with some vegan cornbread or vegan corn muffins.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this chili gluten-free?

Yep! The recipe as written includes no gluten-containing ingredients.

Is this chili spicy?

It's spicy in the sense that it's flavorful and includes lots of spices, but it's not hot. If you'd like some heat, add cayenne pepper or your favorite hot sauce to taste.

Does this chili taste like chocolate?

Nope! The cocoa enhances the flavor, but it's super subtle and you won't even know it's in there. You might just decide you love it so much that you start adding cocoa to all of your chili recipes!

How should I store leftovers and how long do they keep?

Leftover kale chili will keep in an airtight container in the fridge for about 4 days, or in the freezer for about 3 months.

Kale Chili

Ready for the healthy upgrade you didn't know your chili needed? It's kale! Kale adds great texture and earthy flavor that pair perfectly with smoky spices, tender beans, and that thick tomato base. Course Entree, Main, Stew Cuisine American Prep Time 15 minutes minutes Cook Time 45 minutes minutes Total Time 1 hour hour Servings 7 Calories 277kcal Author Alissa Saenz

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 1 medium red bell pepper, diced
  • 1 medium green bell pepper, diced
  • 3 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 5 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 ½ tablespoons unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 ½ tablespoons chili powder
  • 1 tablespoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon chipotle chili powder
  • 2 cups water
  • 2 (15 ounce/425 gram) cans fire roasted tomatoes
  • 1 (15 ounce/425 gram) can red kidney beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 (15 ounce/425 gram) can black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 pound curly kale (about 1 large bunch), stems removed and leaves roughly chopped
  • Black pepper, to taste
US Customary - Metric

Instructions

  • Coat the bottom of a large pot with the oil and place it over medium heat. Give the oil a minute to heat up, then add the onion and both bell peppers. Cook the mixture for about 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the veggies begin to soften up.
  • Stir in the tomato paste and cook it with the veggies for about 2 minutes, stirring constantly, until it just begins to darken.
  • Stir in the garlic, cocoa, chili powder, cumin, smoked paprika, and chipotle chili powder. Cook the mixture for about 1 minute, stirring constantly, until it becomes very fragrant.
  • Stir in the water, tomatoes, kidney beans, black beans, and salt. Raise the heat to high and bring the mixture to a boil, then reduce the heat to low and let the chili simmer for 15 minutes, until slightly thickened.
  • Stir in the kale, adding a handful at a time then stirring it in before adding more. Once all of the kale has been added, bring the chili back to a simmer and cook it for about 15 minutes more, until the kale is tender and the chili has thickened, stirring occasionally. Add a splash of water if the chili gets too thick at any point.
  • Remove the pot from heat and season the chili with additional salt to taste and black pepper.
  • Ladle into bowls and serve.

How To Make Sloppy Joe Frito Casserole

(Full instructions and video in recipe box below)

  1. Brown the ground beef and cook the onion.
  2. Add the ketchup, chili beans, brown sugar, mustard, Worcestershire sauce, and garlic powder. Simmer until it thickens a little. Season to taste with salt and pepper.
  3. Transfer to a baking dish and top with Fritos and cheese.
  4. Bake for 15 minutes.

Recipe Tips

I like using chili beans for this recipe. They have lots of southwestern seasonings added. You can omit the beans if you don’t like them.

Drain the ground beef well. Removing excess grease helps keep the casserole from being oily.

The Fritos are at their crunchiest right after baking so this dish is best eaten right away.

You will probably not need the entire bag of Fritos. You be the judge of how much crunchy topping you want.

Taste before adding salt. Fritos, cheese, and chili beans already contain salt, so go easy on any additional salt.

Variations

  • Make it spicy. Add 1/2 teaspoon of cayenne pepper or add a chopped jalapeno when you add the onion.
  • Change the cheese. I use shredded Mexican cheese. I like its mild flavor. For a stronger flavor, use cheddar cheese or Pepper jack for a spicier flavor.
  • Skip the beans. Omit the beans if your family doesn’t care for beans. If you want to replace them with something, you can add some corn.
  • Use ground turkey. Substitute ground turkey for beef, cooking it thoroughly.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the sloppy joe filling ahead of time and refrigerate it in an airtight container. When you are ready to eat, transfer the filling to a baking dish, add the Fritos and cheese, and bake until hot and bubbly.

Storage

Leftovers will keep for 3 days in an airtight container in the refrigerator but the fritos get soggy. They will crisp back up a bit if you reheat it in a toaster oven or air fryer.

Sloppy Joe Frito Casserole

Sloppy Joe Frito Casserole- this ground beef casserole has it all. Tons of flavor, ground beef, beans, cheese, and a crunchy Frito topping. Course Dinner, Main Dish Cuisine American Keyword ground beef casserole Prep Time 15 minutes minutes Cook Time 15 minutes minutes Servings 6 Calories 751kcal

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds lean ground beef
  • 1 large sweet or yellow onion diced
  • 1 ½ cups ketchup
  • 1 can chili beans
  • ⅓ cup light brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon yellow mustard
  • 2 teaspoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • salt and pepper
  • 1 (9-ounce) bag Frito chips
  • 3 cups shredded Mexican cheese

Instructions

  • Preheat oven to 375 degrees and spray a 9×13-inch baking dish with cooking spray.
  • Crumble and brown the ground beef in a large nonstick skillet over medium-high heat. Once the ground beef is broken into pieces, add the onion. Cook until there is no longer any pink in the ground beef. Drain off any grease.
  • Add ketchup, chili beans, brown sugar, mustard, Worcestershire sauce, and garlic powder. Mix well and bring to a simmer. Season to taste with salt and pepper.
  • Transfer mixture to prepared baking dish.
  • Sprinkle Fritos on top of ground beef mixture. Sprinkle cheese on top of Fritos.
  • Bake for 15 minutes.

This is Amused Bouche, SAVEUR’s food questionnaire that explores the culinary curiosities of some of our favorite people. This interview series dives deep into their food routines, including dinner party strategies, cherished cookbooks, and the memorable bites they’d hop on a flight for.

Comedian and actor Hasan Minhaj is known for quippy questions, biting comebacks, and colorful commentary, but his most delightful soundbites are about food. During a recent  hour-long chat, he named a wide range of favorites, including bananas and oranges (“they come prepackaged and are great for traveling”); macaroni and cheese (“I love every category and version of it, from the box of Kraft to anyone’s Thanksgiving mac ’n’ cheese”); and dal chawal (specifically his mom’s version of the lentils and rice dish, “pure comfort food and my love language”).

These are the best-of-the-best bites for Minhaj, which is coincidentally the name of his new movie—Best of the Best, streaming now on Netflix—that he cowrote and produced with Prashanth Venkataramanujam and stars in as a professor and former dancer. The film pays homage to real-life Bollywood competitions, following collegiate dancers Maya and Anjali (played by Maitreyi Ramakrishnan and Priyanka Kedia, respectively), whose characters were inspired by “deeply passionate college kids in the biggest world that a lot of people have never heard of.”

Fiery passion is at the center of Minhaj’s life, especially when it comes to actual spice. “Some people want to drive at 85, some like a nice even 65, and some take it five miles below the limit,” he explains in a speedy analogy. “My spice level? I want to feel things in all caps. Life is to be lived. So I’m like, ‘Give it to me!’ Give me the full robustness of flavor that’s possible. I am a maximalist and love it all!”

His other spice of life is chai, “a form of commiseration, community, and love in Indian households—and an entry point to gossiping.” He loves that everyone does chai differently (he and his wife, Beena, prefer a more ginger-forward profile, while his parents like it sweeter). That’s why he became an investor and partner in the New York City-based Kolkata Chai in 2024, working with brothers Ani and Ayan Sanyal to expand the reach of a drink so engrained in their lives and culture. “It’s a welcome change to a lot of people’s only chai experience being at Starbucks in Middle America. It’s really amazing how you can change the complete perspective of an entire culture because of a drink that people can explore and make their own.”

Read on for Minhaj’s answers to our Amused Bouche questionnaire—it’s no surprise they’re chock-full of equally heartwarming and appetizing soundbites.

If you could only eat one thing 24/7/365, what would it be?

America is celebrating its 250th-year anniversary, and I think as a country, America’s greatest accomplishment is what we brought to cereal. I have been caught many a time red-handed with my full paw inside of some Golden Grahams, going to town. I will kill half a box, straight up, no milk. It is a snacking cereal. A strawberry Toaster Strudel, one of my all-time favorites. I popped it in the toaster as a kid, and it was an eye-opening experience. Why You’ll Love This Recipe

  • Deeply savory broth. Kombu and shiitake mushrooms create an authentic, umami-rich base that you can’t get from shortcuts.
  • Silky soft tofu. The tofu is incredibly delicate and practically melts in your mouth.
  • Customizable spice level. Adjust the gochugaru to make it mild, medium, spicy, or extra spicy.
  • Comforting and warming. Perfect for cold days or when you need something cozy and satisfying.

Ingredients You’ll Need

For the Broth:

  • Water – The broth base.
  • Dried kombu (kelp) – Essential for authentic, savory depth.
  • Dried shiitake mushrooms – Adds earthy umami to the broth.
  • Onion – Halved for sweetness and depth.
  • Garlic – Smashed cloves add aromatic flavor.
  • Korean soup soy sauce (guk-ganjang) – Lighter and saltier than regular soy sauce. You can use regular soy sauce if needed.

For the Soup Base:

  • Neutral oil – Vegetable, avocado, or grapeseed oil works well.
  • Onion – Thinly sliced for the base.
  • Garlic – Minced for aromatic flavor.
  • Gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes) – The key to the stew’s spice and color. Not the same as regular pepper flakes.
  • Gochujang (Korean red pepper paste) – Adds depth, umami, and heat.
  • Korean soup soy sauce – Adds saltiness and savory flavor.
  • Sugar – Balances the heat and acidity.
  • Sesame oil – Adds a nutty, toasted flavor.

For the Stew:

  • Soft or extra-soft silken tofu (sundubu) – The softer, the better. This is the star of the dish.

Equipment Needed

  • Medium pot (for making the broth)
  • Korean earthenware pot (ttukbaegi/dolsot) or medium pot or deep skillet
  • Knife and cutting board
  • Spoon or ladle
  • Trivet or heat-safe surface (if using an earthenware pot)

Tips for the Best Results

  • Don’t skip the broth step. The kombu-shiitake broth is what gives this soup its authentic, deeply savory flavor.
  • Toast gochugaru. Add gochugaru to the hot oil to release flavor and give the stew its red oil.
  • Use the softest tofu you can find. Look for packages labeled “sundubu” or extra-soft silken tofu.
  • Handle tofu gently. Add in large chunks and stir minimally to prevent it from breaking apart.
  • Start with less spice. You can always add more gochugaru, but you can’t take it away. Start with 2 tablespoons for medium heat.

Serving Suggestions

  • Traditional style – Serve in individual bowls or in the earthenware pot with steamed white rice and kimchi on the side. Add spoonfuls of rice directly into the stew as you eat.
  • With banchan – Serve alongside Korean side dishes like pickled radish, seasoned spinach, or bean sprouts.
  • For a heartier meal – Add glass noodles (dangmyeon) or ramen noodles to the stew.
  • Kimchi sundubu – Stir in 1 cup of chopped, well-fermented kimchi when building the soup base for a tangy, funky variation.

Storage Instructions

  • Refrigerator: Store leftover soup in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The tofu will firm up slightly when cold. Reheat gently on the stovetop.
  • Freezer: Not recommended. Silken tofu doesn’t freeze well and will become grainy when thawed.
  • Make-ahead broth: You can make the mushroom-kombu broth up to 3 days in advance and store it in the refrigerator.

Recipe FAQs

What is gochugaru, and where do I find it?

Gochugaru is Korean red pepper flakes with a unique, mildly spicy, slightly sweet, smoky flavor. Find it at Korean markets or online. Regular red pepper flakes won’t give you the same taste.

Can I skip the kombu-shiitake broth?

Technically, yes, but it won’t taste authentic. The broth is what gives this soup its deeply savory, umami-rich flavor.

How spicy is this?

As written (with 2-3 tablespoons of gochugaru), the recipe is medium spice. See the notes for adjusting to mild, spicy, or extra spicy.

What’s the difference between Korean soup soy sauce and regular soy sauce?

Korean soup soy sauce (guk-ganjang) is lighter and saltier than regular soy sauce. If you can’t find it, use regular soy sauce, reduce the amount slightly, and add salt to taste.

Can I make this ahead?

You can make the broth up to 3 days in advance. The finished stew is best enjoyed fresh, but leftovers keep for up to 3 days in the fridge.

Description

This vegan Sundubu-Jjigae is a Korean Soft Tofu stew that we couldn’t stop thinking about after our honeymoon in Seoul last year. It starts with a rich broth made with kombu and shiitake mushrooms, layered with gochugaru, gochujang, garlic, and sesame oil. Silken tofu breaks into chunks in the pot and simmers until bubbling and fragrant. It’s spicy, rich, savory, and everything you want in a soup.

Ingredients

For the Broth:

  • 8 cups of water
  • 4 pieces dried kombu (kelp), about 4×4 inches each
  • 12–16 dried shiitake mushrooms
  • 1 large onion, halved
  • 8 cloves garlic, smashed
  • 2 tablespoons Korean soup soy sauce (guk-ganjang) or regular soy sauce

For the Soup Base:

  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil (vegetable or grapeseed oil)
  • 1 small onion, thinly sliced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2–4 tablespoons gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes) – see notes for spice levels
  • 1 tablespoon gochujang (Korean red pepper paste)
  • 1 tablespoon Korean soup soy sauce (guk-ganjang) or regular soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 2 teaspoons sesame oil

For the Stew:

  • 1 package (14-16 oz) soft or extra-soft silken tofu (sundubu)
  • 2 green onions, cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 1 small zucchini, sliced into half-moons
  • 1–2 Korean or serrano peppers, sliced (optional, for extra heat)
  • Salt, to taste

For Serving:

  • Cooked white rice
  • Sesame seeds (optional)
  • Extra green onions, chopped (optional)
  • Kimchi (for serving on the side)

Instructions

Make the Broth:

  1. Simmer the broth. Add the water, kombu, dried shiitake mushrooms, halved onion, and smashed garlic to a medium pot. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat.
  2. Cook low and slow. Once simmering, reduce the heat to low and let it simmer for 20-25 minutes. The broth should develop a deep, savory flavor and turn slightly amber.
  3. Strain and season. Remove and discard the kombu, onion, and garlic. Remove the shiitake mushrooms and set aside. Slice them and add them back to the soup if you like. Stir in 2 tablespoons of soy sauce. Taste the broth. It should be rich, savory, and slightly salty. Set aside.

Build the Soup Base:

  1. Heat the oil. In a Korean earthenware pot (ttukbaegi/dolsot), a medium-sized pot, or a deep skillet, heat the neutral oil over medium heat.
  2. Cook the aromatics. Add the sliced onion and serrano peppers (if using) and cook for 3-4 minutes, until softened and slightly translucent. Add the minced garlic and cook for 1 minute, until fragrant.
  3. Toast the spices. Add the gochugaru (start with 2 tablespoons for medium spice) and gochujang. Stir everything together and cook for 1-2 minutes, stirring constantly. The oil should turn a deep red color and smell incredibly aromatic.
  4. Add seasonings. Add 1 tablespoon each of soy sauce, sugar, and sesame oil. Stir to combine.

Finish the Stew:

  1. Add the broth. Pour in the prepared broth (about 3-4 cups, depending on your pot size and desired consistency). Bring to a simmer.
  2. Add vegetables. Add the zucchini and, if using, sliced shiitake mushrooms, and simmer for 3-4 minutes, until tender.
  3. Add the tofu. Gently add the entire block of silken tofu to the pot. Use a spoon to break it into large, irregular chunks. Traditionally, people break tofu by hand or with a spoon rather than pre-cutting it into neat cubes.
  4. Finish with green onions. Add the green onion pieces. Simmer for 3-5 minutes, letting the tofu heat through and the flavors meld.
  5. Taste and adjust. Taste and adjust the seasoning with salt or more soy sauce, as needed. The soup should be deeply savory, slightly spicy, and well-balanced.

Serve:

  1. Serve bubbling hot. Carefully transfer the earthenware pot to a trivet or heat-safe surface (if using ttukbaegi, it will be extremely hot). Otherwise, ladle the soup into individual bowls.
  2. Garnish and enjoy. Garnish with sesame seeds and extra chopped green onions if desired. Serve immediately with a bowl of steamed white rice and kimchi on the side. Traditionally, add spoonfuls of rice directly into the bubbling stew.

Notes

Spice Level Guide:

  • Mild: 1-1½ tablespoons gochugaru + ½ tablespoon gochujang
  • Medium: 2-3 tablespoons gochugaru + 1 tablespoon gochujang (recommended)
  • Spicy: 4 tablespoons gochugaru + 1½ tablespoons gochujang
  • Extra Spicy: 5 tablespoons gochugaru + 2 tablespoons gochujang + add fresh Korean peppers

Ingredient Notes:

  • Kombu: Found in the Asian section of most grocery stores or at Korean/Asian markets. Essential for authentic flavor.
  • Korean soup soy sauce (guk-ganjang): Lighter and saltier than regular soy sauce. If unavailable, use regular soy sauce, reduce the amount slightly, and add salt to taste.
  • Gochugaru: Korean red pepper flakes are different from regular red pepper flakes. Mildly spicy with a slightly sweet, smoky flavor.
  • Sundubu (soft silken tofu): Look for the softest silken tofu you can find. In Korean markets, packages are specifically labeled “sundubu.”

Storage:

  • Refrigerator: Store in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The tofu will firm up slightly when cold. Reheat gently.
  • Freezer: Not recommended. Silken tofu becomes grainy when frozen and thawed.
  • Make-ahead broth: You can make the mushroom-kombu broth up to 3 days in advance.

Instructions

  1. Prep: Preheat the oven to 350 degrees. Spray a muffin tin with avocado oil.
  2. Blend: Add ingredients to the blender in order, but leave out the apples for now. Blend in short bursts until it starts to move (you may need to help it along initially). An authentic wheel of Grana Padano carries a signature brand burned into its rind—so you know that if you see a diamond pattern and the cheese’s name, it must have been produced in the Valle Padana (Po River Valley) of Northern Italy according to strict rules with roots in a 12th-century Cistercian monastery. At the time, the region surrounding the city of Milan was primarily soggy marshland. The monks who built the monastery—Chiaravalle Abbey—were followers of the Rule of Saint Benedict, which, along with devotion to prayer, mandated that they practice agriculture with a focus on responsible, sustainable use of land. They first figured out how to turn the marshy landscape into arable fields so they could grow high-quality feed for dairy cattle. Since milk (and fresh cheese) didn’t last long, the next challenge for the monks was how to preserve this nutritious, valuable food source to nourish themselves and their community.

The method they developed—heating the milk in copper cauldrons, adding rennet (a coagulating enzyme), cutting the curds into tiny pieces, and molding, salting, and aging the cheese—remains largely unchanged today. By producing the first iteration of what is now known around the world as Grana Padano, the monks of Chiaravalle Abbey earned the distinction of operating the world’s first cheese dairy.

Easy to like, portable, and a good source of protein, the cheese became an emblematic food, enjoyed by Italians at all levels of society through the Renaissance and into the modern era. Its iconic name, meanwhile, developed over time. The monks called it “caseus vetus”—Latin for “old cheese”—but since the peasants didn’t know Latin, they referred to it as “grana” for its distinctive grainy texture. As production spread well beyond the monastery, each province had its own grana: Cheese made in Milan was “grana Milanese,” and “grana Mantovano” was from Mantua.

It wasn’t until 1954 that the Italian government codified the name Grana Padano to unite the different varieties of grana cheese under a single wider geographical indication and that the Grana Padano Protection Consortium was established. In 1996, Grana Padano was awarded the European Union’s Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) designation. Today the consortium incorporates 134 member dairies, who together produced over 6 million wheels of cheese in 2025. In addition to safeguarding the name, the organization is also responsible for the global promotion of Grana Padano PDO and governs every detail of production to ensure that each wheel meets the strict standards established under the PDO. 

The specifications start with the cows’ diet: All fresh forage and at least 75 percent of dry fodder (such as hay) must be grown within the cheese production area. Raw milk must be collected within 24 hours of milking and be partially skimmed, allowing for natural separation of the cream. After a natural whey starter is introduced to the copper (or copper-lined) milk vat, calves’ rennet is added for coagulation. The cheese is then broken into rice-size curds using a spino, which resembles a large whisk, and packed into round molds, where it sits for at least 36 hours. The wheels are immersed in brine for anywhere from 14 days to a month and aged for at least nine months and up to 24 months or longer. Wheels aged over 20 months can be labeled Riserva.

Bite into a chunk of Grana Padano PDO, and you taste the sweetness of fresh milk coupled with a savory hint of toasted nuts. Riservas may be flakier and more crystalline in texture than younger versions of the cheese but never dry or crumbly, and they must still have a delicate flavor. Grana Padano PDO is an exceptionally versatile cheese, equally delicious on a cheese board, wrapped with a slice of Prosciutto di Parma PDO and drizzled with aged balsamic for a sophisticated appetizer, as a filling for ravioli, or grated over soup or pasta.

It’s good for you, too. Lactose- and gluten-free, Grana Padano PDO is low in fat and an excellent source of protein, calcium, and vitamin B12. A 30-gram piece (just over one ounce) has as much nutrition as a pint of milk, making it a beneficial as well as tasty solution to the “afternoon slump” for both kids and adults. Whoever you share Grana Padano PDO with, be sure to tell them what they have in common with Italians from more than 1,000 years ago—that they’re savoring a taste of European culinary history.

  • Cajun seasoning– I use Tony Chachere’s.

How To Make

  1. Cook the bacon until crisp and reserve it.
  2. Cook the Andouille sausage until browned and reserve it.
  3. Add the onion and cook until soft.
  4. Make a roux with butter and flour and then gradually whisk in chicken broth.
  5. Add the seasonings and simmer the green beans.
  6. Add the potatoes and cook until tender. Add the sausage back in after 15 minutes.

Slow Cook The Green Beans

What makes these green beans so flavorful is how long they cook. They get really soft and soak up so much flavor from the onion, bacon grease, sausage, chicken broth, and cajun seasoning.

Seasoning

If you want to add a little more heat, you can add a little TABASCO sauce or a pinch of crushed red pepper flakes.

The chicken broth and Cajun seasoning will most likely add enough salt, but check for seasoning at the end and add salt if needed.

Make-Ahead and Storage

These green beans are actually a great make-ahead side dish. Cook them completely, let them cool, and refrigerate. Reheat gently on the stovetop or in the microwave. Add a little fresh crispy bacon before serving if desired.

Leftovers will keep for 4 days in an airtight container in the refrigerator. Reheat in a microwave or a covered pot over medium-low heat.

Smothered Cajun Green Beans

Smothered Cajun Green Beans are a great side dish to serve with any southern meal. Fresh green beans are slow cooked in chicken broth with red potatoes and flavored with bacon, onion, cajun seasoning, and Andouille sausage. Course Side Dish Cuisine Southern Keyword cajun Prep Time 10 minutes minutes Cook Time 55 minutes minutes Total Time 1 hour hour 5 minutes minutes Servings 6 Calories 347kcal

Ingredients

  • 4 slices bacon cut into 1/2-inch pieces
  • 8 ounces Andouille sausage sliced
  • 1 medium onion diced
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 1 1/2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 2 cups chicken broth
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 1 1/2 pounds fresh green beans trimmed and cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 1 teaspoon Cajun seasoning
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 4 red potatoes cut into 6 to 8 pieces each

Instructions

  • Cook bacon in a Dutch oven or large pot until crisp. Remove with a slotted spoon to a paper towel-lined plate and set aside.
  • Add sausage to the pot and cook in the bacon grease until browned. Remove with a slotted spoon and set aside.
  • Add onion to the pot and cook until softened, 4 to 5 minutes.
  • Add butter and when it melts, sprinkle flour over the onions. Cook and stir for 1 minute.
  • Gradually whisk in chicken broth, scraping up the browned bits off the bottom of the pot.
  • Add water, green beans, Cajun seasoning, black pepper, and garlic powder. Bring to a boil. Reduce heat to maintain a simmer. Cover and cook for 25 minutes.
  • Add potatoes. Stir them in so they are submerged as much as possible. If you need to, add a little more water. Cover and cook for 15 minutes.
  • Uncover the Dutch oven and add sausage. Cook uncovered for 15 minutes.
  • Stir in bacon and serve.

Notes

Taste before adding any salt. The broth, bacon, Cajun seasoning, and sausage are all salty. Creole seasoning can be substituted for Cajun seasoning.
