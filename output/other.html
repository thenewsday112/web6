In 1973, Jerry Brassfield bought a sprawling cattle ranch in Clearlake Oaks, designed to be a retreat from hectic city life. Then the neighbors began planting grapes. And so by 1996, he was digging hundreds of soil pits, studying the climate patterns and learning what his land could do. The winery was established four years later, the first vines went in the following year, and the inaugural vintage, made from leased fruit, soon followed. Estate wines arrived in 2005 from vines planted beside an old volcano. That same year, High Valley won its own appellation, a rare east-to-west valley where cool ocean air lingers through the night. Today, Brassfield has 500 acres planted across nearly 5,000, leaving the rest of the land to the wildlife. And now, we are uncorking its flagship wine for the first time.

Aromas of cassis and overripe plum rise from the glass first, undercut by tones of black licorice and damp earth. On the palate, blueberry provides a lingering youthful character at first, eventually receding to a vibrant combination of blackberry, orange peel and rosemary. Dark chocolate wraps around the fruit on the lingering finish, while hints of black truffle and minerality add depth. There’s quite a bit going on here, and it somehow holds together nicely. Wines with this much layering and polish usually come affixed with a price tag demanding some sort of occasion. This one doesn’t. It is remarkably fair in cost, and is the sort of bottle you can open on a humdrum weeknight and still feel you’ve been treated to something special.

A- / $35

Copycat Taco Bell Caramel Apple Empanadas

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1-1/2 cups finely chopped peeled tart apples
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/8 teaspoon ground allspice
  • 1/3 cup thick caramel apple dip
  • 2 sheets refrigerated pie crust
  • 1 large egg yolk
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 1/4 cup butter, melted
  • 1 cup cinnamon sugar
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. In a small saucepan, combine apples, sugar, cinnamon and allspice. Cook until apples are tender, 4-5 minutes. Drain; discard liquid. Transfer to a small bowl. Add caramel; stir to coat. Scoop by tablespoonfuls onto waxed paper. Freeze until firm, about 1 hour.
  2. Unroll pie crust; cut into 14 circles using a 4-in. circle cookie cutter (rerolling dough scraps as needed).
  3. In a small bowl, whisk together egg yolk and water. Brush edges of crusts with egg wash; place apple filling in the center. Fold crusts over filling and seal edges well with a fork. Place 2 in. apart on parchment-lined baking sheets. Freeze until firm, about 30 minutes.
  4. Brush remaining egg wash over tops. Cut 2 small slits on the top of each empanada.
  5. Bake at 425° for 12-14 minutes or until golden brown. Remove from oven and brush with melted butter. Carefully toss in cinnamon sugar. Serve warm.

Nutrition Facts

1 piece: 234 calories, 13g fat (6g saturated fat), 29mg cholesterol, 159mg sodium, 28g carbohydrate (14g sugars, 0 fiber), 2g protein.

Cherry Peach Pie with Salted Pecan Streusel

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1-1/3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1-1/2 teaspoons sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon pumpkin pie spice
  • 4 tablespoons shortening, cubed
  • 4 tablespoons cold butter, cubed
  • 3 to 4 tablespoons water
  • TOPPING:
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1/4 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/4 cup crushed gingersnap cookies
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup old-fashioned oats
  • 1/2 cup chopped pecans
  • 1/4 cup butter, melted
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • FILLING:
  • 2 cups sliced peeled peaches
  • 1 cup frozen pitted dark sweet cherries, thawed
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/3 cup sugar
  • 1/3 cup packed brown sugar
  • 4 tablespoons potato starch
  • Pinch salt
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. In a large bowl, mix flour, sugar, salt and pie spice; cut in shortening and butter until crumbly. Gradually add ice water, tossing with a fork until dough holds together when pressed. Shape into a disk; wrap and refrigerate 1 hour or overnight.
  2. Preheat oven to 400°. On a lightly floured surface, roll dough to a 1/8-in.-thick circle; transfer to a 9-in. pie plate. Trim crust to 1/2 in. beyond rim of plate; flute edge. Refrigerate while preparing filling.
  3. For the topping, in a medium bowl, whisk together flour, sugars, crushed cookies and salt. Stir in oats and pecans. Add the butter and vanilla until small clumps form. Set aside.
  4. In a large bowl, toss peaches and cherries with lemon juice and vanilla. In a separate bowl, whisk together sugars, potato starch and salt. Add to fruit, toss until well coated.
  5. Transfer filling to prepared crust. Sprinkle with reserved topping; pressing down to adhere as needed. Bake for 20 minutes. Reduce oven setting to 350°, bake until crust and topping are golden brown and fruit is bubbly, 20-25 minutes longer. Cool completely on a wire rack.

Nutrition Facts

1 piece: 503 calories, 23g fat (9g saturated fat), 31mg cholesterol, 563mg sodium, 70g carbohydrate (38g sugars, 2g fiber), 5g protein.

2025 Dr. Konstantin Frank Dry Riesling – While they may call this a dry Riesling, what pours in the glass clearly has other ideas. French Onion Stuffing

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 16 ounces sourdough bread, cut into 1-in. cubes
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 3 pounds medium onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon pepper
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/4 cup dry sherry
  • 2 tablespoons minced fresh thyme sprigs, plus more for garnish
  • 2 large eggs, room temperature
  • 2 cups reduced-sodium beef broth
  • 6 ounces Gruyere cheese, grated
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. Preheat oven to 350°. Spread bread cubes in a single layer on two baking sheets. Bake 10 minutes; flip bread cubes and rotate baking sheets. Bake until very dry and golden brown, 5-8 minutes. Set aside to cool.
  2. Meanwhile, in a Dutch oven, heat butter and olive oil over medium heat. Add onions; cook until softened, 10-15 minutes, stirring frequently. Stir in salt and pepper. Reduce heat to medium-low; cook until deep golden brown, 40-50 minutes, stirring occasionally. Add garlic; cook until fragrant, 30-60 seconds. Add dry sherry; use a wooden spoon to scrape up browned bits. Stir in thyme. Remove from heat.
  3. In a large bowl, whisk eggs until smooth. Stir in cooked onions and bread cubes. Stir in beef broth until evenly moistened.
  4. Spoon stuffing into a greased 13x9-in. baking dish. Cover with foil; bake 30 minutes. Uncover; bake until crispy and browned, 15-20 minutes.
  5. Remove from the oven; increase oven temperature to 425°. Sprinkle cheese on top. Bake until melted and bubbly, 3-5 minutes. Garnish with thyme to serve.

Nutrition Facts

Ingredients

  • 1 boneless beef chuck roast (3 pounds), cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt, divided
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon paprika
  • 2 tablespoons canola oil
  • 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1 jalapeno pepper, seeded and chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 bottle (12 ounces) beer
  • 2 cups beef broth
  • 4 teaspoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 2 to 3 teaspoons crushed red pepper flakes
  • 2 teaspoons chili powder
  • 1-1/2 teaspoons ground cumin
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 2 large unpeeled red potatoes, chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon red wine vinegar
  • Tortillas, hot cooked rice, lime wedges and chopped fresh cilantro, optional
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. In a large bowl, toss beef and 1/4 teaspoon salt. In a separate large bowl, combine flour and paprika. Add beef, a few pieces at a time, and toss to coat.
  2. In a Dutch oven, heat oil over medium heat. Brown beef in batches, adding additional oil as necessary. Remove with a slotted spoon. In same pan, add onion; cook and stir until tender, 3-5 minutes. Stir in tomato paste, jalapeno and garlic; cook until fragrant and color starts to darken slightly. Add browned beef back to the pan. Add beer; bring to a boil, scraping the bottom to release browned bits. Simmer until reduced by half, about 5 minutes. Stir in broth, Worcestershire sauce, crushed red pepper flakes, chili powder, cumin, bay leaf and remaining 1/2 teaspoon salt; bring to a boil. Reduce heat; cover and simmer until meat is almost tender, about 1-1/2 hours.
  3. Add potatoes and red wine vinegar. Cover; simmer until meat and vegetables are tender, about 30 minutes longer.
  4. Discard bay leaf. Using the back of a spoon or ladle, slightly mash potatoes. If desired, shred pork slightly with 2 forks. Serve tortillas, rice, lime and cilantro.

This Buttermilk Pound Cake is a classic southern dessert with a rich, buttery flavor, a tender crumb, and a perfectly golden crust. The buttermilk adds a subtle tanginess and also helps create an incredibly moist cake that stays fresh for days.

Whether served plain, dusted with powdered sugar, or topped with fresh berries, this old-fashioned pound cake is perfect for holidays, potlucks, summer cookouts, family gatherings and Sunday suppers.

Why You’ll Love This Recipe

  • Rich, buttery flavor with moist and tender texture
  • Uses simple pantry ingredients
  • Even better the next day making it a great make-ahead dessert
  • Freezes beautifully
  • Delicious on its own or with toppings
  • A timeless Southern favorite

What Makes Buttermilk Pound Cake So Good

Buttermilk helps create a soft, tender crumb while adding a slight tang that complements the sweetness. Combined with plenty of butter and eggs, it produces a pound cake that’s rich without being overly heavy. There’s also some almond extract added which will please almond lovers. The result is a pound cake that’s moist, flavorful, and perfect for any occasion.

How To Make Buttermilk Pound Cake

(More detailed instructions in recipe card below.)

  1. Preheat the oven.
  2. Mix dry ingredients. Whisk together flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
  3. Cream the butter, shortening, and sugar. Use an electric mixer (preferably stand mixer) to beat the butter, shortening, and sugar until light and fluffy.
  4. Add eggs one at a time.
  5. Beat in extracts.
  6. Add the flour mixture, alternating with the buttermilk.
  7. Spray a Bundt pan with baking spray with flour and add batter.
  8. Bake for 1 hour 15 minutes to 1 hour 30 minutes at 325. Place a piece of foil over it halfway through.
  9. Cool in pan 15 minutes and then invert onto a cake stand.

Can I Use Low Fat Buttermilk?

Yes. It will not be quite as rich, but you can use lowfat buttermilk.

Recipe Tips

  • Use room temperature ingredients. Butter should be softened and eggs and buttermilk at room temperature.
  • Don’t overmix once the flour is added.
  • Measure the flour correctly. Fluff the flour up some in the bag or container. Use a large spoon the gently scoop it into a dry measuring cup, overfilling it slightly. Use the back of a knife to run across the top and get rid of excess.
  • Properly grease the pan. Make sure the pan is fully coated with baking spray with flour.

Why Is My Pound Cake Dry?

Overbaking or measuring the flour incorrectly are the most likely reasons for a dry pound cake.

How To Serve

Can be served plain, dusted with powdered sugar or with-

  • Whipped Cream
  • Fresh Berries
  • Vanilla Ice Cream
  • Lemon Curd
  • Strawberry Sauce

Storage

Store at room temperature tightly covered for 4 days, in the refrigerator for up to a week, or freeze whole or in individual slices for up to 3 months.

Buttermilk Pound Cake

This Buttermilk Pound Cake is a classic southern dessert with a rich, buttery crumb, a tender crumb, and a soft, moist texture. Buttermilk gives the cake a subtle tang that balances the sweetness. Enjoy a slice plain, dusted with powdered sugar, or topped with fresh berries and a dollop of whipped cream. Course Dessert Prep Time 20 minutes minutes Cook Time 1 hour hour 15 minutes minutes Total Time 1 hour hour 35 minutes minutes Servings 16 Calories 422kcal

Equipment

  • 10-inch Bundt Pan
  • Baking spray with flour

Ingredients

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • ½ teaspoon baking powder
  • ½ teaspoon baking soda
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup salted butter softened
  • ½ cup vegetable shortening
  • 3 cups sugar
  • 5 large eggs room temperature
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 1 teaspoon almond extract
  • 1 cup buttermilk room temperature
  • whipped cream and berries for serving optional

Instructions

  • Preheat oven to 325 degrees.
  • Whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking sods, and salt. Set aside.
  • Use an electric mixer (preferably stand) to beat the butter, shortening, and sugar until light and fluffy, about 5 minutes.
  • Beat in eggs one at a time, stopping several times to scrape down the sides of the bowl.
  • Beat in vanilla extract and almond extract.
  • Add the flour mixture in 3 additions, alternating with the buttermilk in 2 additions, beating on low speed just until incorporated.
  • Spray bundt pan with baking spray with flour. Pour batter into pan.
  • Bake for 1 hour 15 minutes to 1 hour 30 minutes. Place a piece of aluminum foil over the cake halfway through the baking time so the top doesn't get too brown.
  • Let cool in pan for 10 to 15 minutes and then invert onto a cake stand. Serve with whipped cream and berries if desired.

Notes

You can use unsalted butter instead of salted butter. If you do, add 1/4 teaspoon more salt to the recipe.

Squash Casserole with Stuffing Top Recipe

This recipe is a wonderful way to turn yellow squash into a hearty side dish that pairs well with everything from Fried Chicken to Baked Ham. Best of all it uses simple ingredients and takes just 30 minutes in the oven.

In addition to squash and sweet onion, this Squash Casserole with Stuffing Top also has shredded carrot for a little color and extra flavor. A can of condensed cream of chicken soup makes it really creamy and hearty. Cream of Mushroom soup could be used instead.

American cheese slices are place on top of the squash. They melt and provide a mild cheese flavor and lots of creaminess.

To finish it off, a buttery stuffing layer adds so much flavor. It’s such a filling casserole it could be eaten as a main dish. In fact, you could add some shredded chicken and you would have a delicious main dish casserole.

How To Make Squash Casserole with Stuffing Top

(More detailed instructions and video in recipe card below.)

  1. Steam the squash and onion. Steam just until crisp-tender and then blot dry with paper towels. Place in prepared baking dish and season with salt and pepper.
  2. Mix the creamy filling. Stir together the shredded carrot, condensed soup, and sour cream. Add to casserole dish and mix into the squash and onions.
  3. Add the cheese. Arrange the cheese slices on top of the casserole.
  4. Add the stuffing. Mix the dry stuffing with melted butter and sprinkle on top.
  5. Bake until golden. Bake for 30 minutes or until golden on top.
  6. Serve.

Recipe Tips

  • Don’t overcook the squash. I like to steam the squash and onions before adding them to the casserole since it only bakes for 30 minutes. Just until they are crisp-tender. This ensures they will be tender and soft in the finished dish, but still have some texture.
  • Drain the squash well. Yellow squash contains a lot of moisture. After steaming, drain it thoroughly and dry it with paper towels to help prevent a watery casserole.
  • Try a combination of squash. Use a combo of yellow squash and zucchini to make it more colorful.
  • Customize the condensed soup. Condensed Cream of Mushroom or Cream of Celery Soup can be used instead of Cream of Chicken.
  • Watch the topping as it browns. Bake uncovered so the stuffing can brown. If it needs more color, allow a little more baking time.

Can I Make Squash Casserole Ahead Of Time?

Yes! The casserole can be completely assembled ahead of time and refrigerated until ready to bake. I recommend adding the stuffing just before baking so it doesn’t get soggy.

Storage

Leftovers will keep for about 4 days in an airtight container in the refrigerator. Reheat in a 350 degree oven to het the stuffing crispy again.

Squash Casserole with Stuffing Top

Squash Casserole with Stuffing Top is a creamy, cheesy casserole made from summer squash, onions, and sour cream. On top it has a delicious layer of stuffing. This is a vegetable casserole that's great to eat anytime of year.

 Course Side Dish Cuisine Southern Keyword Casserole Prep Time 20 minutes minutes Cook Time 30 minutes minutes Total Time 50 minutes minutes Servings 8 Calories 419kcal

Ingredients

  • 4-5 medium yellow squash
  • 1 medium sweet onion coarsely chopped
  • 1 shredded carrot
  • 1 (10.5-ounce) can condensed cream of chicken soup
  • 1/4 cup sour cream
  • 7 American cheese slices
  • 2 cups dry herbed stuffing mix
  • 4 tablespoons melted butter

Instructions

  • Preheat oven to 350 degrees F.
  • Steam squash and onion for 8 minutes. Blot dry with paper towels and place in a 2 1/2-quart baking dish that has been lightly greased. Lightly season with salt and pepper.
  • Add carrot, condensed soup, and sour cream to the casserole dish and mix together with the squash and onion.
  • Lay American cheese slices on top.
  • In a bowl, stir together stuffing mix and melted butter. Sprinkle over cheese slices.
  • Bake uncovered for 30 minutes.

Video

Notes

I use Pepperidge Farm Herb-Seasoned Stuffing. Stovetop stuffing also works. Use dry stuffing mix. Do not make the stuffing according to package directions. Just steam the squash until crisp-tender. It will continue to cook in the oven.
