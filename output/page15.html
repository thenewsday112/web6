Crispy rice treats are an iconic American sweet. When my family came to the United States, we had no clue that they were. We took them for an American version of a Vietnames puff rice bars called cơm nổ (exploded rice). The puff rice makers popped rice from scratch over a live fire in a drum that rotates constantly. After cooling the puffed rice, the cooks mix it in caramelized palm sugar then press the hot mixture into a wood mold. By comparison, the American version, Rice Krispies Treats, was so easy to prepare with breakfast cereal, butter, and marshmallows! Funny thing is that we never thought of tweaking the American recipe to give it an Asian twist.

That was about fifty years ago. Nowadays, people stir all kinds of add-ins to tweak rice crispy treats. One stand out recipe is chef Joyce Tang's Fancy Crispy Rice Treats. They are green from matcha and contain salty pretzels. Sweet-salty treats are a natural for Asian kitchen.

Putting an Asian Spin on Classics

Joyce's recipe, published in her debut cookbook, Bake Sum was born of nostalgia and perhaps even some boredom. Instead of making the same old Rice Krispies Treats, she boldy inserted her creativity to channel her preferences. In turn, she created a nod to her Asian American, adult palate. As Joyce writes in the Fancy Crispy Rice Treats recipe introduction:

During the global pandemic of 2020, I was one of many people who was moved to take stock and look at life differently. Stuck at home, I joined the masses in baking obsessively and finding comfort in nostalgia. In this era of bulk-buying dried beans and cereal, I made and devoured countless Rice Krispies Treats, which really brought me back to my childhood in a big way. This recipe is versatile; I've found that most types of cereal work well, but using smaller, similarly sized pieces yields the best results. In this "fancy" version, I love how matcha powder gives these treats both a unique green color and a slightly more grown-up touch of bitterness. Salty pretzels add nuance and texture too.

Joyce's cooking and book weaves together cultures and melts borders. That's how most of us exist nowadays so why shouldn't we cook and eat that way too? The pastries and treats in Joyce's Bake Sum cookbook reflect the clever and tasty collision of flavors and ingredients. Solid cooking traditions and techniques support the tasty outcomes.

For instance, you may be unfamiliar with the colorful toppings and red bean filling for the conchas on the cover of the book but the Bake Sum buns convey the history of Chinese people in Mexico. The dough for Bake Sum's conchas is an Asian-style milk bread dough using the tangzhong method developed in China. That milk bread magic creates a pillowy sturdy enough to contain oozy fillings like the purple ube shown on the right below.

There's happiness in what Joyce creates as well as deliciousness. I've eaten at the bakery and while I'm no pro-level, laminated dough fiend (the book has a whole chapter on croissants and the like for you to master), I know I can make fanciful crispy rice treat like this one.

For a throwback dessert spread, serve the fancy crispy rice treats with my updated Magic 7-Layer Cookie Bar. Sisters and I loved the puffed rice bars. My mom adored the coconut and chocolate cookie bar. You cannot go wrong with these classics.

Fancy Crispy Rice Treats

An Asian American take on crispy rice treats, this recipe has been slightly tweaked from the original one published in Bake Sum, a cookbook by Joyce Tang (co-authored with Soleil Ho, published by Hardie Grant, 2026). Check the main post for extra tips from Joyce. I suggested swaps for a vegan version! Course Dessert Cuisine Asian American Keyword crispy rice ceral, crispy rice treats, matcha, not-too-sweet Servings 12

Ingredients

  • Cooking spray for the pan
  • 6 tablespoons | 85g unsalted butter (cow or plant-based), at room temperature
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 2 tablespoons milk (cow, oat, or otherwise), at room temperature
  • 7 cups | 420g mini marshmallows (standard or vegan)
  • 1 tablespoon matcha powder
  • 10 cups | 255g crispy rice cereal
  • 1 cup | 50g mini pretzels
  • 1 ½ teaspoons vanilla extract

Instructions

  • Prep the pan: Grease a 9-inch (23 cm) square pan with cooking spray and line it with parchment paper, allowing the parchment to extend slightly beyond the edges. This will create handles for lifting the treats out of the pan later.
  • Cook the crispy rice mixture: In a large shallow saucepan over high heat, melt the butter, salt, and milk together. Once melted, lower the heat to medium and add 5 ¼ cups (320g) of the marshmallows and cook, stirring occasionally, until they fully melt. (You can do this step in the microwave: Just use a medium bowl and blast it for 30 seconds at a time.)
  • Sift the matcha into the melted marshmallows and mix until combined. Allow the matcha/marshmallow mixture to cool slightly, about 1 minute, before stirring in the rice cereal, pretzels, and vanilla. (This brief cooling period helps prevent the treats from becoming soggy by allowing some steam and moisture to escape.) Stir in the remaining 1 ¾ cups (100g) marshmallows using a stiff spatula, wooden spoon, or, my personal favorite, a fiberglass spatula while the mixture is still warm.
  • Mold, cut and serve: Transfer the mixture to the prepared pan and press it down flat with an oiled hand or spatula. Let it cool for several hours before cutting. For best results, use an oiled serrated knife and a ruler to cut the treats into even shapes. They're delicious no matter how you slice them. Store airtight at room temperature for up to 3 days.

Tender cabbage, savory ground beef, and a spicy, garlicky sauce make this Cabbage Stir Fry an instant favorite. Made with fresh vegetables and loaded with flavor, this 30-minute meal is an easy alternative to Chinese takeout.

Stir fries are a frequent fixture of my weekly dinner menus and stir fry noodles with chicken and vegetables is another one I make often. This week, since I had ground beef in the freezer, I switched things up and made a beefy cabbage version.

Cabbage Stir Fry

Similar to my egg roll skillet and sausage egg roll in a bowl, this has the ground meat, shredded cabbage, onion, and garlic you’d find inside a typical egg roll, and I’ve kicked up the flavor here with the punch of chili paste.

I expected the beef to be my favorite part of this stir fry. And as much as I enjoyed the savory meat, it’s the cabbage that kept me coming back for more. Cabbage is inexpensive and versatile, and when it’s sliced into noodle-like ribbons here, it’s perfect for lapping up all that incredible sauce.

I’m always amazed by how easy it is to turn an ordinary vegetable into a crave-worthy dish with just a few simple steps. I can’t wait for you to try this one.

Ingredients and Substitutions

The Ground Meat – I used ground beef here, but ground pork, turkey, and chicken will work well, too.

The Cabbage – You want green cabbage with dense, hearty leaves here.

The Aromatics – Fresh garlic, ginger, and yellow onion provide the flavor base for the stir fry.

The Sauce – Soy sauce, chili paste, and cornstarch create a slightly spicy, salty, savory sauce that thickens as it cooks.

The Garnishes – Green onions and sesame seeds provide the finishing touch for cabbage stir fry.

Notes on Equipment

  • Chef’s knife. Food prep is so much easier when you have a quality knife. It was over 10 years ago when I first tried this one. The price simply can’t be beat for quality.
  • Cutting board
  • Measuring cups and spoons
  • Wooden saute paddle. You can use any wooden or metal spatula you like, but I find that I reach for this super sturdy wooden utensil more often than not.
  • Large skillet. I love this enamel cast iron skillet for stir fries and skillet meals like this one. It goes from the stove to the table and it wipes clean like a dream.

How to Make Cabbage Stir Fry

Getting Things Ready: Before I start cooking anything, I prep the vegetables. Shred the cabbage, chop the onion, mince the garlic and the ginger.

Browning the Beef: I warm a large skillet over medium-high heat, and then add the ground beef. Cook the ground beef, crumbling it a bit after it begins to brown.

Adding the Aromatics: You’ll add the onion, garlic, and ginger to the beef and continue cooking and stirring the mixture until the onion has softened and the skillet is fragrant.

Mixing the Sauce: While the beef is cooking, I combine the soy sauce, chili paste, and cornstarch in a liquid measuring cup or bowl and whisk well to combine it.

Adding the Sauce: Pour the stir fry sauce into the skillet with the meat. Give it a good stir and scrape off any bits of beef that may have stuck to the pan.

Cooking the Cabbage: Now, we’ll add the cabbage to the skillet. Stir to mix the cabbage throughout the meat and then put a lid on the skillet. Let the cabbage steam until it looks slightly wilted.

Finishing the Stir Fry: I’ll take the lid off of the skillet once the cabbage begins to wilt and continue cooking the stir-fry and stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens.

Garnishing the Stir Fry: I sprinkle the stir-fry with thinly sliced green onions and sesame seeds before portioning it into bowls. And I’ll give it an additional drizzle of Sriracha, if desired, for a bit of heat.

Expert Tip

Whenever I make a stir fry, I prep every ingredient and line them up next to the stove before I turn on the heat. Once you start cooking, everything happens FAST. Being prepared helps you avoid accidentally burning the beef because you got distracted chopping cabbage.

Taste for Salt – I always taste my dishes as I’m cooking. It’s especially important here if you’re using something other than ground beef. When I made this with ground pork, I found I needed to add a bit more salt, and some extra chili paste too, to achieve the same depth of flavor as the beef.

Make It Spicier – If you love spicy heat, this cabbage stir fry is practically begging to be drizzled with Sriracha. Other sauces like Bang Bang totally belong here too.

Avoid Overcooked Cabbage – I remove the lid at the end of cooking, not only to reduce the sauce, but also to keep the cabbage tender-crisp instead of soggy.

Serving Suggestions

Serve this with bowls of Chinese Egg Drop Soup for a fantastic meal. If you want to round things out with a salad, I recommend trying my Asian Cabbage Salad or Harvest House Chop Salad. And I’ll never turn down an opportunity to serve my Orange Parsley Salad, that one still makes me grin all these years later.

Storage and Reheating

How to Store: Transfer any leftovers to an airtight container. Store it in the refrigerator and enjoy within 2-3 days.

How to Reheat: Single portions will reheat well in the microwave. I recommend covering the plate with a paper towel before heating to keep the beef moist. If I’m heating up a larger batch, then I use a skillet on the stove at low heat and add a splash of water to refresh the sauce.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I make this vegetarian?

To make a meatless cabbage stir fry, swap the ground meat for textured vegetable protein (TVP). I would add additional salt to taste if using TVP.

Can I use other kinds of cabbage?

Green cabbage works best for this recipe because it’s thicker and holds up well to the sauce. I wouldn’t recommend using varieties with thinner leaves, like napa.

Is this recipe gluten free?

It depends. Many soy sauces contain gluten. To make this gluten free, be sure to use a gluten free soy sauce or tamari.

How can I make this less spicy?

For a less spicy version, reduce the amount of chili paste in the sauce. If you don’t want any spice at all, leave the chili paste out entirely.

Cabbage Stir Fry

This Cabbage Stir Fry with ground beef, tender cabbage, and a spicy garlic sauce is an easy 30-minute dinner that beats Chinese takeout. Course Main Course Cuisine American Prep Time 10 minutes minutes Cook Time 20 minutes minutes Total Time 30 minutes minutes Servings 6 Calories 243kcal Author Mary Younkin

Ingredients

  • 1 pound ground beef (see note below for substitutions)
  • 1 medium yellow onion chopped into ½-inch pieces about 1 cup
  • 4 cloves garlic minced
  • 1 tablespoon minced ginger
  • ¼ cup low sodium soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon chili paste or Sriracha hot sauce
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch
  • 1 medium head green cabbage shredded thin, about 8-10 cups worth
  • ½ teaspoon kosher salt
  • ¼ teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
  • 4 green onions sliced thin
  • black and / or white sesame seeds
  • optional Bang Bang sauce or additional Sriracha

Instructions

  • In a large skillet over medium-high heat, cook and crumble the ground beef. Add the onion, the garlic, and the ginger and continue cooking and stirring for about 3 minutes until softened and fragrant.
  • While the beef and onion are cooking, whisk together the soy sauce, chili paste, and cornstarch in a small glass or bowl. Set aside. Pour in the sauce and stir to mix throughout.
  • Stir in the cabbage and reduce the heat to medium. Cover with a lid for about 3 minutes, until it begins to soften. Uncover and continue cooking uncovered for an additional minute or two, as needed, until the sauce has mostly been absorbed and the cabbage is tender-crisp.
  • Taste the stir fry and adjust the salt and pepper, as desired. Sprinkle with green onions and sesame seeds before serving. Drizzle with additional sauces, if desired.

Video

Notes

Ground chicken, ground turkey, or ground pork may all be substituted here. TVP is also a great option if that’s more your style. Regardless of your choice, you’ll want to taste before serving, because this recipe is written specifically for ground beef. When I tested it with ground chicken and pork, I needed a bit more salt in order to hit the same flavor profile. So, adjust as desired for your tastes.

Bourbon Chicken is one of our favorite easy Chinese Takeout meals to make at home on busy nights. Tender, moist chicken bites slathered in sweet and spicy homemade bourbon sauce are totally irresistible! If you have been craving the sticky, glossy bourbon chicken from the mall food court, this is the recipe you’re looking for. Bite-size chicken breast gets browned in a skillet, then simmered in a sauce made with brown sugar, soy sauce, apple juice, garlic, a pinch of red pepper flakes, and a splash of bourbon. The sauce cooks down into a sweet and savory glaze that clings to every piece. I guarantee you’ll simply love this one-pan, comforting meal that you can enjoy with the whole family!

5-Star Review

“Delicious and easy to make. Followed as written including the bourbon. Next time will garnish with some scallions and perhaps a few (chopped) red peppers.” -TR

Better Than Takeout, and Made in One Skillet

I never thought Asian-inspired foods would be easier to cook at home than picking up takeout. Homemade bourbon chicken is healthier (I know exactly what’s in it), freshly made, needs minimal preparation, and tastes so much better!

This mildly spicy, one-pot meal makes a perfect family dinner on busy nights. It takes about an hour from start to finish, and it serves 6.

When I tested this recipe, I found that the sauce was still quite thin at the 20-minute mark. So I added an optional cornstarch step. It takes less than a minute and gives you that thick, glossy glaze every time. You’ll know the sauce is ready when you drag a spoon across the bottom of the pan and the sauce briefly parts before flowing back together.

Asian-inspired meals are my go-to whenever I need something fast, and my kids drool over this recipe. When they want something a little crispier, I make my honey chicken, which has a light batter and a sweet honey garlic sauce. On nights when I want something nutty and savory, cashew chicken is on the table in about 30 minutes.

Do You Really Need the Bourbon?

Bourbon is listed as optional in this recipe, but I highly recommend adding it if you have some on hand. I tested this recipe with and without bourbon, and the difference was easy to taste. The bourbon adds a rich, slightly smoky flavor that balances nicely with the sweetness of the brown sugar and apple juice. It’s what gives bourbon chicken its name, after all!

The version without bourbon is still very flavorful. It’s a great option if you don’t have bourbon on hand or want to avoid cooking with alcohol. Just swap in an equal amount of apple juice or chicken broth.

Much of the alcohol cooks off during the 20-minute simmer, but a small amount can remain. If you’re cooking for anyone who avoids alcohol completely, leave it out. You don’t need anything fancy here, and any bourbon you would happily sip works great.

Tips for Beginners

  • Thicken the sauce with a cornstarch slurry. When I tested this recipe, the sauce was still quite thin when the 20-minute timer went off. If yours hasn’t thickened, stir in a slurry of 1 tablespoon cornstarch and 2 tablespoons cold water, and it will thicken right away. Keep the water to 2 tablespoons, because when I tested a full cup of water, the sauce got even more watery and took several extra minutes to start thickening.
  • Use the bourbon if you have it. The bourbon sauce is the star of this recipe, and much of the alcohol cooks down and evaporates as the cooking progresses. I tested it both ways, and the bourbon adds a rich, slightly smoky flavor that balances the sweet sauce. If you don’t have bourbon or want to skip it, you can swap it out with vegetable or chicken broth or apple juice, and the sauce is still very flavorful.
  • Add more veggies. I’m using ginger and garlic for flavor, but colorful bell peppers, bok choy, broccoli, zucchini, or cauliflower will also taste amazing. They soak up the sweet and spicy sauce and make this an easy one-pan meal.
  • Swap in chicken thighs. Boneless skinless chicken breast is my preferred cut because it’s lean protein, but boneless skinless chicken thighs also work. Dark meat takes a little longer, so give thighs a few extra minutes in the skillet to make sure they’re browned and cooked through.
  • Finish with a garnish. I like to top this dish with sesame seeds and sliced green onions right before serving. The green onions add a touch of freshness, and the sesame seeds add texture and a nutty flavor.

Bourbon Chicken Recipe

Bourbon Chicken is one of our favorite easy Chinese Takeout meals to make at home on busy nights. This Bourbon Chicken Recipe is the best sweet and spicy chicken recipe, with the best bourbon chicken sauce. Course Main Course Cuisine Chinese Difficulty Easy Prep Time 30 minutes minutes Cook Time 30 minutes minutes Total Time 1 hour hour Servings 6 servings Calories 301kcal Author Becky Hardin

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs. boneless chicken breasts cut into bite-size pieces
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 garlic clove crushed or 1 tsp pre-minced garlic
  • ¼ tsp ginger
  • ½ tsp crushed red pepper flakes
  • ½ cup apple juice
  • ⅓ cup brown sugar
  • 2 tbsp ketchup
  • 1 tbsp cider vinegar
  • ½ cup water
  • ⅓ cup soy sauce or Yoshida’s Gourmet Sauce
  • 2 tbsp Bourbon optional but recommended
  • 1 tbsp cornstarch + 2 tbsp warm water optional for thickening

For Serving/Garnish

  • cooked white rice I use Minute Rice, prepared per instructions
  • sesame seeds optional, for garnish
  • sliced green onions optional, for garnish

Instructions

  • Heat oil in a large skillet over medium-low heat. Add chicken pieces and cook until lightly browned. 2 tbsp olive oil, 2 lbs. boneless chicken breasts
  • Once cooked, remove chicken.
  • Add remaining sauce ingredients, heating over medium heat until well mixed and dissolved. 1 garlic clove, ¼ tsp ginger, ½ tsp crushed red pepper flakes, ½ cup apple juice, ⅓ cup brown sugar, 2 tbsp ketchup, 1 tbsp cider vinegar, ½ cup water, ⅓ cup soy sauce or Yoshida’s Gourmet Sauce, 2 tbsp Bourbon
  • Add chicken and bring to a hard boil.
  • Reduce heat and simmer for 20 minutes or until thickened to the desired consistency.
  • (Optional thickening step) If the sauce is still thin, whisk the cornstarch and cold water together in a small bowl until smooth. Stir it into the simmering sauce and cook for about 1 minute, until thick and glossy 1 tbsp cornstarch + 2 tbsp warm water
  • Serve over hot rice. Garnish with sesame seeds and sliced green onions, if desired. cooked white rice, sesame seeds, sliced green onions

Notes

  • Apple juice gives this sauce its fruity sweetness, but chicken broth or vegetable broth will do the trick if apple juice is not handy, so swap in an equal amount of broth. The sauce will taste a little more savory and a little less sweet.
  • The bourbon is optional, but it adds a rich, slightly smoky flavor that balances the sweetness of the sauce. If you don’t have it or want to avoid alcohol, replace it with an equal amount of apple juice or broth. The sauce will still be very flavorful.
  • If the sauce is still thin after 20 minutes of simmering, stir in a slurry of 1 tablespoon cornstarch and 2 tablespoons cold water. It thickens in about a minute. A larger amount of water thins the sauce out and takes much longer to cook down. You can also simmer for 3 to 5 extra minutes instead, but you’ll end up with less sauce and slightly tougher chicken.
  • Boneless skinless chicken breast is lean and cooks quickly, but chicken thighs also work. Since dark meat takes a bit longer, give thighs a few extra minutes to brown before you remove them from the skillet.
  • This sauce works with more than chicken. Swap the chicken for paneer or tofu for a vegetarian option. Pork and shrimp are other great options.
  • A simple garnish makes this dish feel finished, so sprinkle sesame seeds and sliced green onions over the top right before serving. The green onions add freshness and the sesame seeds add a nutty crunch.
  • Leftovers hold up well, so store them in an airtight container in the fridge for 3-4 days. Bourbon chicken also freezes for up to 3 months. The sauce may thin slightly after thawing, so simmer it for a few minutes while reheating to bring it back together.

How to Make Bourbon Chicken Step by Step

Gather all the ingredients together.

Prepare the chicken and cook: Cut 2 lbs of boneless chicken breasts into bite-sized pieces. Pat them dry. Heat 2 tbsp olive oil in a large skillet over medium-low heat. Add the chicken pieces and cook until lightly browned. Once cooked, remove the chicken. The chicken will change to a white color with a few light golden spots, and that’s when you need to remove it from the skillet. It doesn’t need to be cooked all the way through yet, since it will finish cooking in the sauce.

Make the bourbon sauce: Add 1 minced garlic clove, ¼ tsp ginger, ½ tsp crushed red pepper flakes, ½ cup apple juice, ⅓ cup brown sugar, 2 tbsp ketchup, 1 tbsp cider vinegar, ½ cup water, ⅓ cup soy sauce, and 2 tbsp bourbon to the same skillet. Heat over medium heat until well mixed and dissolved. The sauce is ready for the chicken when you no longer see any grains of brown sugar on the bottom of the pan.

Combine and simmer: Place the chicken back into the skillet and bring the mixture to a boil. Reduce the heat and let it simmer for 20 minutes, stirring now and then so the sugar doesn’t stick to the bottom of the pan.

Thicken the sauce (optional): After 20 minutes, check the sauce. If it is still thin and soupy, whisk 1 tbsp of cornstarch with 2 tbsp of cold water in a small bowl until smooth. Stir the slurry into the simmering sauce, and it will thicken right away, turning glossy and coating the back of a spoon. Use only 2 tbsp of water here. When I tested a larger amount of water, the sauce got even thinner and needed several more minutes on the stove before it started to thicken.

(If you’d rather skip the cornstarch, you can simmer the sauce for another 3-5 minutes until it caramelizes and thickens. To check, run a spatula across the bottom of the pan. If the sauce briefly parts and leaves a clear line before flowing back together, it’s ready. Just know that the extra time reduces the amount of sauce, and the chicken can turn a bit tough.)

Serve over rice: When the chicken is ready, serve it over rice. Sprinkle with sesame seeds and sliced green onions for a little crunch and freshness. Enjoy!

How to Store and Reheat

Transfer any leftovers to an airtight container and refrigerate for 3 to 4 days. The sauce will thicken as it chills.

Freezer

  • Freeze after cooking. Due to the simplicity of bourbon chicken, you can make it ahead of time and freeze it in freezer-friendly storage bags for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator and reheat in a saucepan or microwave. The sauce may thin out slightly after freezing, so let it simmer for a few minutes while reheating.
  • Freeze before cooking. If you love those fresh flavors from the sauce, then feel free to freeze the chicken and sauce before combining. Once the chicken is cooked, let it cool and come to room temperature. Prepare the sauce separately, and let that cool as well. Transfer them to separate containers and freeze. Thaw overnight and cook per the instructions.

Reheating

To reheat, use a saucepan over medium-low heat or microwave in 30 second intervals. If the sauce has gotten too thick in the fridge, add a splash of water and stir until it loosens back up.

Make Ahead

Stir the sauce ingredients together and refrigerate them in a jar for up to 2 days. Cut the chicken ahead too and keep it in a separate container. When it’s time for dinner, brown the chicken and pour in the sauce, and you’ve skipped most of the prep.

Serving Suggestions

Asian recipes usually pair well with plain steamed rice, and that’s exactly how I serve bourbon chicken most nights. The rice soaks up every drop of that sweet, sticky sauce. When I’m short on time, I use Minute Rice, and when I have a little longer, I make my Instant Pot Basmati Rice because it comes out fluffy without any babysitting.

To round out the plate, I add a big skillet of stir fry vegetables. The crisp veggies balance the sweet sauce, and I can make them while the chicken simmers.

For a full takeout night at home, I start with egg rolls or crab rangoon bites as an appetizer, then indulge in this delicious chicken! Just keep in mind the crab rangoon has shellfish if anyone at the table has an allergy. When I’m feeding a bigger crowd, I swap the plain rice for chicken fried rice to stretch the meal a little further.

These Vegan Dauphinoise Potatoes are a cozy comfort recipe we love making year after year. Thinly sliced Yukon Gold potatoes baked in an herb- and garlic-infused vegan cream make a decadent, delicious side dish, perfect for holiday dinners or a hearty weeknight meal. This is our dairy-free take on the classic French gratin dauphinois, and we finish it with an optional layer of melty vegan cheese because we couldn’t resist.

Why You’ll Love This Recipe

  • Super creamy without the dairy. We steep vegan heavy cream with garlic, thyme, sage, rosemary, and a pinch of nutmeg for a rich, savory classic dauphinoise flavor.
  • Crispy edges in each bite. Instead of lying flat, the potato slices stand up, so the tops get nice and crispy while the bottom absorbs the cream.
  • Simple ingredients, big flavor. This recipe uses only potatoes, cream, and a few herbs and spices, but it comes out of the oven looking like it took hours to make. 
  • Hands-off and holiday-friendly. You slice the potatoes while the cream infuses. Once it goes in the oven, your stovetop is free, and so are you. 

Ingredients You’ll Need

  • Vegan heavy cream: Store-bought plant-based heavy cream is the easiest way to get a rich, velvety sauce. Look for an unsweetened, neutral-flavored option. We like Dairy-Free Country Crock Heavy Cream.
  • Garlic: Give the cream a mellow garlicky flavor.
  • Fresh herbs and bay leaf: Thyme, sage, rosemary, and a bay leaf infuse the cream with earthy, savory notes.
  • Black peppercorns and salt: Whole peppercorns gently flavor the cream. The salt enhances the cream, which you then pour over the sliced potatoes.
  • Nutmeg: A classic in dauphinoise. A pinch of ground nutmeg adds subtle warmth.
  • Yukon Gold potatoes: Our top pick for dauphinoise. They taste naturally buttery, hold their shape when sliced thin, and turn silky soft as they bake.
  • Vegan butter: Used to grease the baking dish so the potatoes don’t stick. 
  • Vegan cheese shreds: They melt into a golden, bubbly top. They’re optional, but highly recommended.

The full ingredient list with measurements is in the recipe card below.

Equipment Needed

  • Small pot
  • Mandoline slicer
  • Cut-resistant glove
  • Fine-mesh strainer
  • 9×13-inch baking dish

Tips for the Best Results

  • Use a mandoline for even slices. When every slice is ⅛ inch thick, they all cook evenly. Always use the hand guard or a cut-resistant glove.
  • Pack the slices tightly. Stand the slices on their edges in snug rows and tuck extra slices into any gaps. A tightly packed dish holds its shape and bakes more evenly.
  • Cover the dish tightly. A snug layer of foil traps steam, so the potatoes cook through before the top browns.
  • Keep an eye on the broiler. Vegan cheese can go from golden to burnt very quickly. Stay close and pull the dish out as soon as the top looks the way you like it.
  • Let it rest before serving. Let it rest for 10 minutes out of the oven so the cream can settle and thicken, giving you clean scoops instead of a runny sauce.

Serving Suggestions

These vegan dauphinoise potatoes fit right in on a holiday table next to a vegan holiday roast or our lentil meatloaf, with pan-fried asparagus or maple chili crisp Brussels sprouts on the side. Since the potatoes are so rich, we love pairing them with something bright, like our Brussels sprout Caesar Salad. For a weeknight dinner, serve a generous scoop alongside crispy tofu cutlets and some sautéed greens.

Storage Instructions

  • Refrigerator: Let leftovers cool completely, then cover tightly or transfer to an airtight container. They’ll keep in the fridge for up to 4 days.
  • Reheating: Cover with foil and reheat in a 350°F oven for 20 to 25 minutes, or until warmed through. Uncover for the last 5 minutes to crisp the top again. The microwave works for single portions, but you’ll lose the crispy edges.
  • Freezing: We don’t recommend freezing. Cream-based potato dishes tend to turn grainy and watery once thawed.

Recipe FAQs

What’s the difference between dauphinoise and scalloped potatoes?

Gratin dauphinois comes from the Dauphiné region of southeastern France. You bake thinly sliced raw potatoes in garlic-infused cream, and the traditional version has no cheese. American-style scalloped potatoes are similar but usually bake in a thickened milk sauce. Potatoes au gratin are topped with cheese and sometimes breadcrumbs. Ours is a bit of a hybrid: classic infused cream with a golden, cheesy top.

Can I make vegan dauphinoise potatoes ahead of time?

Yes! You can infuse and strain the cream up to 2 days ahead and keep it in the fridge. To make the whole dish ahead, bake it covered, let it cool, and refrigerate overnight. When you’re ready to serve, reheat it covered at 350°F until warmed through. Then uncover it, add the cheese, and finish browning.

Do I need a mandoline?

Not at all. A sharp chef’s knife works just as well. Take your time and keep the slices as close to ⅛ inch as you can so everything cooks evenly. A mandoline just makes the job faster and more consistent.

Can I use a different type of potato?

Yukon Golds are our favorite here, but russets work too. Russets are starchier, so they bake up a bit softer, and the slices won’t hold their shape quite as neatly.

Can I make it without the cheese?

Absolutely. Classic dauphinoise doesn’t include cheese. Just uncover the dish and keep baking until the top is golden brown.

Description

These vegan dauphinoise potatoes are the cozy comfort recipe we love to make year after year. Thinly sliced Yukon Gold potatoes baked in an herb- and garlic-infused vegan cream make a decadent, delicious side dish, perfect for holiday dinners or a hearty weeknight meal. This is our dairy-free take on the classic French gratin dauphinois, and we finish it with a layer of melty vegan cheese because we couldn’t resist.

Ingredients

For the Infused Cream

  • 2 cups vegan heavy cream
  • 3 garlic cloves, smashed
  • 1 sprig fresh thyme
  • 1 large sprig fresh sage
  • 1 sprig fresh rosemary
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 3 black peppercorns
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • Pinch of ground nutmeg

For the Potatoes

  • 3 pounds Yukon Gold potatoes
  • Vegan butter, for greasing the dish

Optional Topping

  • Vegan cheese shreds

Instructions

  1. Infuse the cream. In a small pot, add the vegan heavy cream, garlic, thyme, sage, rosemary, bay leaf, peppercorns, salt, and nutmeg. Bring just to a boil over medium heat, then remove from the heat and let it infuse for 30 minutes.
  2. Preheat the oven. While the cream infuses, preheat the oven to 425°F and grease a 9×13-inch baking dish with vegan butter.
  3. Slice the potatoes. Using a mandoline (recommended) or a sharp knife, slice the potatoes into ⅛-inch rounds.
  4. Arrange the potatoes. Stand the potato slices on their edges in the baking dish and pack them tightly in rows.
  5. Add the cream. Strain the infused cream through a fine-mesh strainer into a bowl, discarding the garlic, herbs, and peppercorns. Pour the cream evenly over the potatoes, then cover the dish tightly with foil.
  6. Bake. Bake on the middle rack for about 1 hour and 15 minutes, or until most of the cream is absorbed and the potatoes are fork-tender.
  7. Add the cheese and brown. Uncover the dish, sprinkle the vegan cheese on top, and bake for 10 to 15 more minutes, until melted and golden. For extra color, broil for 1 to 2 minutes, watching closely so it doesn’t burn.
  8. Rest and serve. Let the potatoes cool for 10 minutes before serving. Enjoy!

Notes

  • Vegan heavy cream: Use an unsweetened, neutral-flavored store-bought vegan heavy cream for the best results.
  • Slicing: Aim for even ⅛-inch slices so the potatoes cook evenly. If you use a mandoline, always use the hand guard or a cut-resistant glove.
  • Don’t rinse the potatoes: The surface starch helps thicken the cream as it bakes.
  • Make ahead: Bake through step 6, let cool, and refrigerate overnight. Reheat covered at 350°F until warmed through, then continue with step 7.
  • Cheese-free option: For a more traditional dauphinoise, skip the cheese and bake uncovered until the top is golden.
  • Storage: Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container for up to 4 days.

Knowing how to roast vegetables well is a power move. It’s a single technique that can be deployed across an impressive range of vegetables turning them into platters of food that is simple, unfussy, and just plain good.

How To Roast Vegetables – The Basics

You can use this basic technique for a wide range of vegetables – potatoes, beets, broccoli, cauliflower, string beans, cabbage, artichoke hearts, asparagus, etc. Seriously, don’t overthink it. Once you get your roasting technique nailed down, you can fancify things with added spices, under-dressings, and the like. But to start, here’s what to know:

  • High heat is your friend. Somewhere in the range of 400°F – it will give you tender interiors and nice, golden exteriors. Go too hot and you’ll burn the edges before the insides have time to cook. Some recipes might go a bit higher, others a bit lower, but if I’m just doing a basic vegetable roast, I typically set the dial right around 400°F.
  • Don’t crowd the pan. Your vegetables are going to put off a good amount of moisture as they roast, and they like some space to crisp instead of steam.
  • Cut vegetables to a similar size. This way they will cook in the same amount of time.
  • Group vegetables by cooking time. Like with like  on the pan. You want to arrange your ingredients in zones based on estimated cooking time. For example, put asparagus which doesn’t take long to roast, on a small pan (or at the edge of a larger pan). Place ingredients that take longer to cook on another pan or in another zone, for example-potatoes or winter squash, That way you can remove them as they finish cooking.
  • Don’t skimp on the oil. You don’t have to go wild, but make sure everything gets a thin, even coat of olive oil before roasting.

Use this play-by-play: Heat oven to about 400°F. Toss each type of vegetable in a large bowl with a splash of olive oil and a good pinch of salt I often add a couple scoops of nutritional yeast in at this point but that’s entirely optional. Roast, tossing a couple times after things start browning, until vegetables are tender and have a good amount of golden color.

I know a lot of people make vegetable pot pies with store-bought puff pastry, but over the years I’ve come to believe a homemade pie crust is, by far, the superior way to go. It makes the whole pot pie endeavor worth it. These pies are rustic and creamy, often lovingly constructed. A great pie crust lends butter-kissed backbone and structure. A flaky, decadent counterpoint to the classic, creamy, vegetable filling. Let’s make a pie!

Vegetable Pot Pie: Real Talk

My main piece of advice here is this. If you’re are aiming to make a vegetable pot pie from scratch, plan ahead. Going from start to finish in one go can be a bit of an undertaking – pie dough, filling, assembly, bake, etc. But! If you keep pie crusts at the ready in your freezer, and make the filling a day or two in advance, pulling this pie together is an absolute breeze.

Pro Tips:

There are a few things I do as I’m making pot pies to level them up. Little details to help delineate my pie from your average vegetarian pot pie.

  • Homemade pie crust: Per the opening paragraph, I’ll go to the mat encouraging you to make your own pie dough. Make a few rounds of dough and keep it in the freezer for months. The flavor! The flakiness! Once you nail down the pie dough making process it’s hard to go back to a store-bought crust. Can you use a store-bought crust for this recipe? Yes, absolutely. Will it be better with a homemade pie crust? Yes, absolutely.

  • Caramelize your onions: Really go for it. Most pot pie fillings have you sauté onions until tender in the beginning. I have you go well beyond that. By browning the onions and celery you’re developing more depth and flavor as the base of your filling.
  • Lemon zest: Sprinkle your pie plate with lemon zest before lining with the pie crust. Add some to your filling as well. The zest combines with the butter from the crust to perfume the whole situation. The brightness of the zest really brings something to the filling as well.

Vegetable Pot Pies: Common Mistakes

There are a number of common pitfalls to avoid when making a pot pie.

  • Cool your filling completely: When you go to assemble your pie, it’s important that your filling is completely cold. Adding a warm filling is going to melt the butter in your crust prematurely and can contribute to a soggy pie bottom in the end. You want a golden, structured bottom crust. The solution? If you’re in a rush, spread the hot pie filling across a large plate, and place in the refrigerator, stirring now and then until cool.
  • Place pie on baking sheet: This is one mistake I still make too often, unfortunately. Placing your pie on a baking sheet while baking will protect your oven from any spills, overflows, or melted butter. It makes clean up much easier and you won’t have things burning onto the bottom of your oven.
  • Keep an eagle eye on your crust: I love a deeply golden, rustic pie crust. But the edges of a pie can get dark while the center is still underbaked. A problem! Have tinfoil strips or a pie shield ready to protect areas of your pie until the rest of the pie catches up. If you want to up your pie game over time, a basic pie shield is a great, inexpensive purchase!


Pot Pie Leftovers:

Refrigerate leftovers, covered, for up to 5 days. Reheat in a 350°F / 175°C oven, covered in foil, until filling is completely hot.

Can I Make Pot Pies Ahead of Time?

If you want to make a pot pie ahead of time, make the filling in advance and refrigerate or freeze until ready to assemble the pie. Thawing first, of course. Make the pie dough ahead of time as well. You’re going to have more success baking the day you want to serve the pie.

Vegetable Pot Pie Variations

  • Make it spicy: Add a couple teaspoons of your favorite curry powder (or to taste) to the filling. You might also add some to the pie crust as well.
  • Explore different vegetables: Use what you have and love! Or explore what is seasonal. Great vegetable options include: sweet potatoes, broccoli, cauliflower, shredded cabbage, pumpkin, and corn. Roasted cherry tomatoes add an amazing burst of flavor.
  • Coconut milk: Use coconut milk (full fat) in place of the heavy cream for a different flavor profile.
  • Pesto: Skip the other herbs in the recipe and stir a couple tablespoons of pesto into your filling at the end. Or dollop it on top of the filling as you’re assembling your pot pie, before putting the top crust on.
